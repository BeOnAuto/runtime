# @on.auto/prediction

The implementation of prediction functions. A prediction function answers a question from the playbook's own history: a quantity over a grain of time, one or more periods ahead or as a total to the end of a longer period, for one series at a time, as a likely value with a low and a high, in the `series` shape the runtime owns beside the last periods observed. The runtime's forecaster answers it as soon as the function is saved and the series' history is long enough, and keeps nothing between questions. Keep the reusable function, its dataset and the prediction a run returns distinct, as [Playbook terminology](../../docs/concepts/terminology.md) says.

This package holds the function's document, its derived schemas, its check at save, the dataset the playbook keeps for it, the window a question reads, the forecaster and the question it answers.

User documentation is [Prediction function format](../../docs/reference/prediction-format.md), published at [on.auto/docs](https://on.auto/docs/): how a prediction function forecasts, the document and its fields, the input and the output, a total to the end of a period, the dataset, the forecaster, a question and its record, how a run ends, the bounds and a prediction function in a workflow. Update it alongside behaviour changes; `src/document/reference-page.test.ts` checks that the page's example is the document the tests run, that the page's answer is a series of the example's grain, default history and horizons, that its bounds are the ones `predictionBounds` holds, and that its totals are the pairs of periods `longestSpanOf` sums, with their spans.

## The document

`parsePredictionDocument(source)` reads a document with the shared reader of [`@on.auto/definitions/document`](../../packages/definitions/README.md#reading-function-documents) and gives a `PredictionFunctionDefinitionDocument`: the optional `description`, `predicts`, with its `coverage`, 0.8 unless the document names another, and `data`, the digest of the data part, `dataSha256`, the `data` expressions of the filters with their pointers and lines, and the derived schemas. The front matter's keys are `description`, `predicts` and `data`; every other key, `input` and `output` among them, is an issue at its line, and so is a body after the front matter, since a prediction function says what it predicts in its `description`. Each part is checked in its own module (`src/document/predicts-part.ts`, `src/data/data-part.ts`), and every problem is reported at once, each at the line of the value it names: a repeated horizon, a history that is not a whole number from 0 to 500 or that is past the window, a coverage outside 0.5 to 0.95, a window that is not a whole number from 1 to 1,000 or that is shorter than the forecaster needs to backtest an answer, the longest horizon and 3 more, or 4 times the most periods the enclosing period of a total holds, the target named `series` or `period`, a `time`, `series` or target pointing where none of the named event types holds a value, `count` with `of` or another aggregation without it. The data part takes no features: the forecaster reads the target alone, so the dataset folds nothing else. A window that long means a series whose history is too short is put right by waiting: a question refused `history_too_short` is answered from the period the refusal names.

The digest of the data part, `dataSha256`, is the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON of what the rows of its dataset depend on: the data part without its `window`, with the grain beside it (`src/data/data-digest.ts`), since a row is folded from the filters, the pointers and the aggregation at the grain, and the window a question reads changes none. Two functions whose rows are folded alike share one dataset, and an edit that widens the window rebuilds nothing.

`summarize` answers the description, the input schema, the series shape as the output schema, and the `details` the registry keeps with the definition, through `publishedOutputOf` of `@on.auto/definitions`, as a reasoning function that declares `output.shape: series` publishes them: the details hold `shape: series` and `data`, the data part the dataset folds, whose digest, the SHA-256 of its canonical JSON, keys the dataset, so the host's projector folds a dataset without this package's parser, and a function recorded with a data part of an earlier release, whose fields this release no longer has, is folded under the digest of what this release reads of it, the one its document names now. The input schema is the question, `{ series, horizon?, marks? }` with `series` text of 1 to 256 bytes, whose `maxLength` of 256 a run's own byte count completes, `horizon` one of the horizons and none with `accumulates`, and `marks` the series shape's own `seriesMarksSchema`, at most 8 (`src/document/derived-schemas.ts`).

The capability's `check` (`src/checks/prediction-check.ts`), which runs at a save and never at a run, checks the filters' `data` expressions in the pool's check worker against `$data`, as a recall function's are, and refuses each fault at its line.

## The dataset

`datasetFolder({ filters, triesBeforeStall, rebuildsAtOnce })` is a folder of the host's projector ([`@on.auto/workflow-host`](../../packages/workflow-host/README.md)), beside the recall views, over two tables it creates in the ledger's database, which `datasetTables` names: `prediction_datasets`, one row a dataset of a playbook, keyed by `playbook_key` and `data_sha256`, with its `phase`, `checkpoint`, `checkpoint_at`, `rows`, `series`, `first_period`, `last_period`, `events_folded`, `passed_over`, `tries`, the stall's `stalled_record` and `stalled_detail`, and the `data` part it folds; and `prediction_rows`, one row a series and period, keyed by `playbook_key`, `data_sha256`, `series` and `period_index`, the whole number that orders the periods of its grain, with the `period` in its ISO 8601 form, the `target` as `DOUBLE PRECISION`, the `target_count` and `target_at` that `mean` and `last` need, and the `events` folded into it. The two tables are part of the layout of the ledger, so a change to them raises `ledgerLayout` of `@on.auto/ledger` and ships the step that carries a database of the layout before forward, registered beside them in `predictionLayoutSteps` (`src/dataset/dataset-statements.ts`), whose step 7 drops two tables of prediction functions this release no longer keeps and these two, whose rows no longer hold features, so every dataset is rebuilt from the history in the new shape. The server gives the ledger these steps with those of the other packages whichever capability answers prediction functions, since the tables are the runtime's (see [A layout step](../../packages/ledger/README.md#a-layout-step)). `datasetsProvidedOn({ pool, triesBeforeStall })` answers the folder over the pool's filter sandbox, which the forecaster provides and the server's projector runs with the other folders. A dataset is added when an active definition first names its data part, waits for one of `rebuildsAtOnce` slots, 2 a playbook, rebuilds from the first event of the history, goes live, and is dropped with its rows once no active definition names it.

The fold runs in the server's process over the page's decoded events (`src/dataset/dataset-fold.ts`): the filters are matched as a recall function's are, the `data` tests through the engine's filter sandbox, which in the server is the pool's one filter thread, shared with the workflows' trigger and listener filters. A dataset's tested filters are prepared once a page, in one session of the sandbox (`filterTestingOf` of `@on.auto/workflow-engine`), and each event of the page is tested in it, each filter's `type` matched before its test, so an event meets the tests of its own type's filters alone (`src/dataset/dataset-matching.ts`); a test the sandbox stops lets that session go, the event's other filters are tested in sessions of their own, and the next event takes a fresh one. Then for each event the time is read, in UTC, and its period found (`src/periods`: weeks from Monday as ISO 8601 numbers them, calendar quarters, days through the civil calendar), the series read, and the target merged into the row of that series and period with `count`, `sum`, `last` by the event's time, whatever order the events were recorded in, or `mean`; a missing or null value adds nothing. An event whose time is not a strict ISO 8601 time with its offset on a calendar day, a date alone among them, or names a leap second, whose series is not text of 1 to 256 bytes, or whose value is not a finite number, adds to no row: the dataset counts it in `passed_over` and the host notes `record_passed_over` with the reason `refused`, at most ten a dataset a page. A data test that raises passes its event over the same way, since the event caused it. One that runs out of time or memory meets a limit no event causes, as the engine treats a deadline or a memory stop, so it never passes its event over: the page ends before that event, the dataset's `tries` on its row count one more, and it rests at its checkpoint until the projector's next sweep, a second by default, which tries it again, as the views of recall functions are tried; a page that folds without a stop sets `tries` back to 0, and at `triesBeforeStall` tries, which the server gives as the bound the views of recall functions stall at, `overtimesBeforeStall` of `@on.auto/recall`, twenty, the dataset stalls, phase `stalled`, its stall naming the event's record and the stop's words, the host notes `dataset_stalled` with the record and the words, and it folds nothing more until no active definition names its data part. A page reads, of the rows it changes, only those of the series and periods its events touch, one indexed row a pair, and learns which of its series are new with one probe a series over a `VALUES` list, `(SELECT 1 … LIMIT 1) IS NOT NULL`, which both stores run once a series and which reads at most one indexed row of it, so a page reads at most twice as many rows as it has events, 2,000 for 1,000, however many periods its series hold. The probe is not an `EXISTS`: PostgreSQL turns that into a join, and over a table it has no statistics of yet, as after a rebuild, into one that reads every row of the dataset, 126 ms for a page of 1,000 series over 1,000,000 rows against 11 ms for the probe and 47 to 52 ms for the `SELECT DISTINCT series … IN` it replaces; SQLite took 1.7 ms against 43 ms. These reads and the upsert of the page's rows bind a value per row, so each is built in chunks within the `mostBoundValues` of the host's database ([`@on.auto/workflow-host`](../../packages/workflow-host/README.md#where-a-run-is-kept)): 2 values beside the dataset's 2 for each series and period read, 1 beside the 2 for each series probed, and 9 for each row written; the chunks of a read answer as one, and the upserts run in the page's one transaction (`src/dataset/dataset-merging.ts`, `src/dataset/dataset-writing.ts`). On a testing database that allows 15, the least the fold's own statements fit within, the 15 of the dataset's advance, a page over 16 series already stored and 4 new ones, whose read of stored rows and probe of known series each span several statements with stored rows and known series in each, folds the same rows, rows and series as on the engine's own bound, on SQLite and, where the PostgreSQL suites run, on PostgreSQL (`src/dataset-testing/dataset-bound-suite.ts`). A page's rows, its counts, its tries and its checkpoint are written in one transaction, which commits only if the checkpoint it read is still the row's.

`datasetsPortOn(database)` reads a dataset's row for `get_definition`'s `standing` (`src/standing/prediction-standing.ts`): `kind`, `data_sha256`, the phase, the checkpoint, the counts, the first and last period and `stall`, null until the dataset stalls and then `{ record, detail }`, as `{ dataset }`.

## The window a question reads

`seriesWindow(database, { playbook, document, series, now })` (`src/window/series-window.ts`) reads, in one consistent read of the host's database, the dataset's row and the series' rows over the last `data.window` periods, the one before the period `now` falls in last, which is the question's origin. A dataset that is not live is `unavailable` with the kind `dataset_not_ready`, its `because` `stalled` for one that stopped at an event; a series the window holds no row of is `invalid_input` at `/series`. The window holds a value for each period: nothing before the series' first row in it, so its history starts where the series does, unless the series has a row before the window, which one read of a single row looks for, when it holds a value from the window's first period; and from there the target as its aggregation reads it, a `count` or a `sum` of a period with no event being 0, and a `last` or a `mean` of one nothing. The run records the dataset's checkpoint the read was made at.

## The forecaster

`forecastOf({ observed, season, coverage, asked })` (`src/forecast/ladder.ts`) answers a forecast or `history_too_short` from the observed values alone: it keeps no state, reads no clock and names no series. `asked` is `steps`, the longest horizon, or a `total`, the periods to the end of the enclosing period, the periods of it observed so far, and the past enclosing periods it is backtested at, each its origin and its periods to the end, which the question computes from the calendar (`src/question/question-asked.ts`, `src/periods/enclosing.ts`).

- **The methods** (`src/forecast/methods.ts`) are tried in order, and the first that backtests enough answers. With a season: `level-adjusted-seasonal-naive`, the periods a season earlier scaled by the ratio of the mean of the last `k` periods to the mean of the same `k` a season before, `k` the smaller of the season and the longest horizon, which needs a season and `k` periods; and `seasonal-naive`, the periods a season earlier, which needs a season. Then `persistence` for steps, the last observed value carried forward, and `run-rate` for a total, the mean of the periods so far run to the end, each of which needs one period. A seasonal method's total is the sum of its steps.
- **The backtest** (`src/forecast/backtest.ts`) forecasts the history from each earlier origin the method has the periods for, every step up to the longest horizon, or each past enclosing period's total from the origin it had, and keeps each miss, the truth less the forecast, by step or for the total. A method backtests enough with at least 3 misses at every step; a step or a total whose truth was not observed in full adds none.
- **The range** (`src/forecast/quantiles.ts`) is the value plus the quantiles of the method's own misses at that step, at `(1 - coverage) / 2` and `(1 + coverage) / 2`, interpolated between the sorted misses, and always holds the value; the low of a series that was never negative is held at 0.
- **Too short a history** is the answer when no method backtests enough, with the periods of the history.

`src/forecast/history.ts` holds the history the methods and the backtest read: the observed values, the first period observed, the origin and the periods from the first to the origin.

## A question

`makePredictionFunctionAdapter(ports)`, a `CapabilityFactory` of [`@on.auto/workflow-host`](../../packages/workflow-host/README.md#entry), answers the forecaster's `ProvidedCapability` from the ports the server hands a capability: the capability, type `prediction`, media type `text/markdown`, guide `prediction-function`, made from the pool of workers, for the check, and the database of the host, for the window's read and, through `datasetsPortOn(database)`, the standing; and the folder of its datasets, through `datasetsProvidedOn(ports)`. The server composes it where no extension fills `capability/prediction` (see [Extensions](../../packages/server/README.md#extensions)). `predictionFunctionOf` (`src/capability/prediction-function.ts`) makes the same capability with a `DatasetsPort` of its own, which the package's tests give in memory. Its `mostActive` is `mostFunctions`, which the server gives as `PREDICTION_MAX_FUNCTIONS`, 32 unless it names another, and its `longestAnyRunMs` the 10 seconds of a question. A run checks its input against the question's schema and answers it within its call (`src/question/question-run.ts`): it reads the window, asks the forecaster, and answers the series, the `observed` line of the last `predicts.history` periods, twice the longest horizon, or twice the most periods the enclosing period holds, unless the document names another, and the `predicted` line of the horizons asked, or the `total` line of one point at the last period of the grain the enclosing period holds, with the question's `marks`. The answer is checked against the series shape before it is answered, and one that does not match is a defect of the runtime, never the caller's.

The run's record is `{ method, coverage, season, k?, history: { periods, first_period, last_period, origins }, decimals, total_to?, origin, dataset_checkpoint }`: the method that answered, the coverage, the season it used, `null` for a method without one, `k` for the level-adjusted method, the history it backtested, its periods, the first and the last of them and the origins it was backtested at, the decimals the observed values carry, which the run's words print the figures with, the enclosing period of a total, the question's origin and the dataset's checkpoint. A series whose history is too short ends the run `conflict` with the kind `history_too_short` (`src/question/history-refusal.ts`), its detail naming the series, the periods it has up to the origin, those of them without a value, and the first period from which the same question answers, which the refusal finds by asking the forecaster again at each later origin, the window moved on by as many periods and each period after the origin holding a value, at most the window's length of them; for a `last` or a `mean`, which holds no value in a period without a reading, it says the question answers then if every period until then holds a value. The run's words (`src/capability/prediction-words.ts`) say the values and their ranges, the share of truths the ranges are meant to hold, as `8 times in 10`, `19 times in 20` or a percentage, and the method in words, such as `From 2026-W41, it predicts 7 orders in 2026-W42 (6 to 8 orders), its range meant to hold the truth 8 times in 10, by carrying the last observed value forward.`

## What an extension builds on

An extension that fills `capability/prediction` and answers over the datasets of prediction functions builds on what this package exports for it, part of contract 1 beside the types of [`@on.auto/workflow-host`](../../packages/workflow-host/README.md#entry): `datasetsProvidedOn(ports)`, the folder of the datasets, which the forecaster provides and such an extension provides among its own, since the fold runs only for a capability that provides it, over the tables the runtime carries forward whichever capability answers; `datasetTables`, the names of the two tables the fold writes, `prediction_datasets`, one row a dataset, and `prediction_rows`, one row a period of a series; `DatasetRow`, a dataset's row as the fold keeps it, with `DatasetStanding`, its phase, checkpoint and counts; and `DatasetDetailsSchema`, the `details` of a definition's summary that the fold reads a dataset from, `{ data }`, with `DatasetDetails`, its type, and `DatasetData`, that of its `data`: the `period`, `events`, `time`, `series` and `target` of the dataset. An extension with datasets of its own provides its own folder and its tables instead, a change of their shape taking new tables, and the fold of this package then runs for no capability.

## Bounds

`predictionBounds` holds them: 1 to 8 horizons of 1 to 52 periods, a history of 0 to 500 periods, a season of 2 to 366, a window of 1 to 1,000 periods and at least what the forecaster needs to backtest an answer, a coverage of 0.5 to 0.95, 0.8 by default, 1 to 8 filters, the target's name and the unit of 1 to 64 characters, a series of 1 to 256 bytes, 10 seconds a question, 32 active functions a playbook, two rebuilds at once a playbook and ten notes of passed-over events a dataset a page; the tries before a dataset stalls are the server's, the views' twenty. Each has a test that reaches it here, and the server's `src/prediction` reaches the playbook's over HTTP.

## Measurements

`pnpm --filter @on.auto/prediction measure` measures, outside the tests (`measure.ts`, with its parts in `measure/`), on SQLite and, with `LEDGER_MEASURE_POSTGRESQL_URL` set, on PostgreSQL: 200,000 events of 1,000 series over 200 days, one an order a series a day, folded by the projector into the dataset of a daily function, then 10,000 more appended while it is live; then, on a database of its own, the same history folded into the dataset of the same function with a `data` test on its filter, `${ $data.total > 0 }`, which every event passes, tested on the filter thread of a pool, as the server tests it. Measured on 2026-10-12, when the function also folded two features, on Node 26.10.0 on a development machine of 16 cores, PostgreSQL 18.4 on the same machine, an x86_64 build run under translation, at a load average of 4 to 7.5:

| What                                                          | SQLite                                       | PostgreSQL                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| 200,000 events recorded, in 200 appends of 1,000              | 9.0 s                                        | 7.6 s                                        |
| the dataset rebuilt from them, in 201 pages, to 200,000 rows  | 8.8 s, 22,821 events a second, 44 ms a page  | 17.2 s, 11,621 events a second, 86 ms a page |
| the same with a data test                                     | 21.5 s, 9,314 events a second, 107 ms a page | 22.4 s, 8,930 events a second, 111 ms a page |
| 10,000 more events, in 10 appends, folded live from the first | 0.85 s, 11,716 events a second               | 0.55 s, 18,085 events a second               |
| the same with a data test                                     | 1.39 s, 7,205 events a second                | 1.20 s, 8,363 events a second                |

A data test costs a page of 1,000 events about 63 ms on SQLite, about 0.06 ms an event on the filter thread, since the page prepares the dataset's tests once and tests each event in that session. When each event opened a session of its own and froze its context, the same rebuild with a data test folded 219 events a second on SQLite, 4,359 ms a page, measured over 20,000 events, so a page held the playbook's other folders, which the projector folds one after another, back by more than 4 s, and a rebuild of 200,000 events took about 15 minutes. The PostgreSQL rebuild without a data test varied from 13.1 s to 20.5 s over four runs at that load, and a rebuild that probed with an `EXISTS` from 9.9 s to 22.7 s over three. The live figure counts from the first append and includes the ten appends, which at the recording's rate take about 0.45 s on SQLite and 0.38 s on PostgreSQL.

## Testing

`@on.auto/prediction/testing` exports `weeklyOrders`, the example document, `weeklyOrdersLines` and `weeklyOrdersWith(...replaced)`, which changes its lines, and the daily documents, `dailyVisits`, `dailyTotals` and each `...With`. `src/testing/forecast-questions.ts` holds the forecaster's questions the tests ask, `stepsAhead` and `theRestOfTheWeek`. The dataset's suites (`src/dataset-testing`) and the questions' suite (`src/question-testing/projected-questions-suite.ts`) run on SQLite and on PostgreSQL, where `LEDGER_TEST_POSTGRESQL_URL` is set, over the host's projector and a playbook of the operations' testing harness, on a clock the suite moves.

## Source

`src/index.ts` is the entry point and `src/testing/index.ts` the entry point of the test support. `src/document` holds the document: its type, the front matter, the parts and parsing, and the derived schemas. `src/data` holds the data part, its filters, pointers, measures and digest. `src/periods` holds the calendar: civil days, grains, enclosing periods and the moments an event's time is read as. `src/dataset` holds the folder, its tables and statements, the fold and the port; `src/window` the window a question reads; `src/forecast` the forecaster, its history, methods, backtest and range; and `src/question` a question, its run, its record and its refusal. `src/capability` holds the capability, its bounds, its words and the figures they print; `src/checks` its check at save; and `src/standing` the standing `get_definition` shows. `src/dataset-testing` and `src/question-testing` hold the suites and the harness the tests share.
