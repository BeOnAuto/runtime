# @on.auto/prediction

The implementation of prediction functions. A prediction function answers a question with numbers learned from the playbook's own history: a quantity over a grain of time, one or more periods ahead, for one series at a time, as a likely value with a low and a high, in the `series` shape the runtime owns beside the last periods observed. Keep the reusable function, the model a version of it holds, and the prediction a run returns distinct, as [Playbook terminology](../../docs/concepts/terminology.md) says. A model is a file in a format one of the server's model runtimes executes: one that a trainer builds outside the playbook from the rows the playbook keeps, or one that a runtime which fits its own models fits from them. The capability speaks to them only through [`@on.auto/prediction-contract`](../../packages/prediction-contract/README.md), and never names a format: the release's runtime, of its own models, is the extension [`@on.auto/prediction-series`](../../packages/prediction-series/README.md), and a server loads any other an extension it lists provides.

This package holds the function's document, its derived schemas, its checks at save, the dataset the playbook keeps for it, the files a build reads and the upload of a dataset; a build, from its refusals and its request to its version, the trainer's three routes, the candidates a runtime fits for a build no trainer answers, the scoring of the candidates, the shadow and the activation of a version; the question the active version answers, or the default forecast before one is; and the model processes, and the jobs served in the server's own process for a runtime bound inline, that load the models through the runtime of their format, with the files they read.

User documentation is [Prediction function format](../../docs/reference/prediction-format.md), published at [on.auto/docs](https://on.auto/docs/): the document, the fields, a total to the end of a period, the dataset and its grammar, the brief, a build and the trainer's path, the signature of a candidate, its scoring, a question and its answer, how a run ends, the bounds and a prediction function in a workflow; [HTTP API](../../docs/reference/http.md#prediction-functions) lists the five routes served over HTTP alone, and [Model runtimes](../../docs/reference/model-runtimes.md) says what a candidate's format is, what a candidate records of the runtime that executes it and what an extension that provides one exports. Update them alongside behaviour changes; `src/document/reference-page.test.ts` checks that the page's example is the document the tests run, that its rows are taken by an upload, that its uploaded data part parses, that its bounds are the ones `predictionBounds` holds, and that its totals are the pairs of periods `longestSpanOf` sums, with their spans, `src/document/reference-trainer.test.ts` that the bounds of a build's trainer are the ones the code holds, and `src/document/example-answers.test.ts` that the page's answer to a question is a series of the example's grain, default history and horizons, and that the forecast of the developer example is a series whose observed weeks are the last of the data it uploads; the server's `src/prediction/documented-example.test.ts` runs the page's workflow before and after a build of `weekly-orders`, and sends a build through the page's interaction function, whose request names the build and its routes.

## The document

`parsePredictionDocument(source)` reads a document with the shared reader of [`@on.auto/definitions/document`](../../packages/definitions/README.md#reading-function-documents) and gives a `PredictionFunctionDefinitionDocument`: the optional `description`, `predicts`, `data`, `evaluation`, `source` and `rules`, the brief compiled as a Liquid template, the digest of the data part, `dataSha256`, the `data` expressions of the filters with their pointers and lines, and the derived schemas. The front matter's keys are `description`, `predicts`, `data`, `evaluation`, `source` and `rules`; every other key, `input` and `output` among them, is an issue at its line. Each part is checked in its own module (`src/document/predicts-part.ts`, `evaluation-part.ts`, `source-part.ts`, `src/data/data-part.ts`), and every problem is reported at once, each at the line of the value it names: a repeated horizon, a history that is not a whole number from 0 to 500 or that is past the window, a holdout or a window outside the bounds the horizons and the season set, `seasonal-naive` without a season, a `given` name that is not a feature, a feature or the target named `series` or `period`, a feature named as the target, a `time` or `series` pointing where none of the named event types holds a value, `count` with `of` or another aggregation without it, `to` beside `through`, a rule past its bounds. The brief (`src/brief`) reads `dataset` and `function` alone: parsing compiles it and refuses a brief that does not compile and a name outside them at their lines; the compile is cheap and deterministic, held by the template engine's bounds on characters and names and by no clock, 0.11 ms for the example's brief and 4.7 ms for one of 65,535 characters at the median, so every `prepare` of a run pays it. Rendering is the check's, below, since its 200 ms bound is a clock's.

The digest of the data part, `dataSha256`, is the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON of what the rows of its dataset depend on: the data part without its `window` and its `given`, with the grain beside it (`src/data/data-digest.ts`), since a row is folded from the filters, the pointers and the aggregations at the grain, and neither the window a build reads nor the features a question supplies changes one. Two functions whose rows are folded alike share one dataset, and an edit that widens the window or names other given features rebuilds nothing.

`summarize` answers the description, the input schema, the series shape as the output schema, and the `details` the registry keeps with the definition, through `publishedOutputOf` of `@on.auto/definitions`, as a reasoning function that declares `output.shape: series` publishes them: the details hold `shape: series`, then `data_sha256` and `data`, `data` being what the digest covers, so the host's projector folds a dataset without this package's parser, and `calls`, the interaction functions it calls, its trainer `source.through` with the role `trainer` and then the function of `evaluation.activate.approved` with the role `approver`, each when the document names it, so the read of each says in `used_by` that the prediction function uses it. The input schema is `oneOf` a question, `{ series, horizon?, given, marks? }` with `series` text of 1 to 256 bytes, whose `maxLength` of 256 a run's own byte count completes, `horizon` one of the horizons and none with `accumulates`, `given` required when the document lists it and holding those names alone, and `marks` the series shape's own `seriesMarksSchema`, at most 8, and a build, `{ build: {} }` (`src/document/derived-schemas.ts`). A build's outcome, `{ version, winner, candidates }`, is no series, so the output schema says the answer of a question, the run an agent reads a series from, and the format guide says the build's.

The capability's `check` (`src/checks/prediction-check.ts`), which runs at a save and never at a run, renders the brief over the largest dataset the bounds admit (`src/brief/brief-dataset.ts`) and refuses, at its line, a brief that writes a list or a mapping where text goes, reads an item the largest dataset does not have, renders past 64 KiB or takes longer than the template engine's 200 ms to render. It reads the other definitions of the playbook through the `SavedDefinitions` port of `@on.auto/definitions`, which answers the active version of a definition with its schemas: an approver named under `evaluation.activate.approved` is an active interaction function whose input schema takes `{ function, version, card }` and whose output schema is an object whose one required property `decision` is `approve` or `reject`; a function named under `source.through` is an active interaction function whose input schema takes `{ build: {} }` and whose output schema is an object whose one required property `candidates` is a list of text (`src/checks/called-functions.ts`). A function that names an uploaded dataset is checked against what the port's `content(sha256)` answers (`src/checks/uploaded-datasets.ts`): the playbook keeps content under the digest, as `text/csv`, whose header and first row (`src/upload/kept-head.ts`) are of the grain `predicts.period` names and name `predicts.target` and then the features in `data.features`' order; each fault is refused at its line, so the standing never names a digest that was never uploaded. The filters' `data` expressions are then checked by the pool's check worker against `$data`, as a recall function's are.

## The dataset

`datasetFolder({ filters, triesBeforeStall, rebuildsAtOnce })` is a folder of the host's projector ([`@on.auto/workflow-host`](../../packages/workflow-host/README.md)), beside the recall views, over two tables it creates in the ledger's database: `prediction_datasets`, one row a dataset of a playbook, keyed by `playbook_key` and `data_sha256`, with its `phase`, `checkpoint`, `checkpoint_at`, `rows`, `series`, `first_period`, `last_period`, `events_folded`, `passed_over`, `tries`, the stall's `stalled_record` and `stalled_detail`, and the `data` part it folds; and `prediction_rows`, one row a series and period, keyed by `playbook_key`, `data_sha256`, `series` and `period_index`, the whole number that orders the periods of its grain, with the `period` in its ISO 8601 form, the `target` as `DOUBLE PRECISION`, the `target_count` and `target_at` that `mean` and `last` need, the `events` folded into it and the `features` as JSON, each feature as its value, count and time. The two tables are part of the layout of the ledger, so a change to them raises `ledgerLayout` of `@on.auto/ledger` and ships the step that carries a database of the layout before forward, registered beside them in `datasetLayoutSteps` (`src/dataset/dataset-statements.ts`), which the server gives the ledger with the steps of the other packages (see [A layout step](../../packages/ledger/README.md#a-layout-step)). A dataset is added when an active definition first names its data part, waits for one of `rebuildsAtOnce` slots, 2 a playbook, rebuilds from the first event of the history, goes live, and is dropped with its rows once no active definition names it.

The fold runs in the server's process over the page's decoded events (`src/dataset/dataset-fold.ts`): the filters are matched as a recall function's are, the `data` tests through the engine's filter sandbox, which in the server is the pool's one filter thread, shared with the workflows' trigger and listener filters. A dataset's tested filters are prepared once a page, in one session of the sandbox (`filterTestingOf` of `@on.auto/workflow-engine`), and each event of the page is tested in it, each filter's `type` matched before its test, so an event meets the tests of its own type's filters alone (`src/dataset/dataset-matching.ts`); a test the sandbox stops lets that session go, the event's other filters are tested in sessions of their own, and the next event takes a fresh one. Then for each event the time is read, in UTC, and its period found (`src/periods`: weeks from Monday as ISO 8601 numbers them, calendar quarters, days through the civil calendar), the series read, and the target and each feature merged into the row of that series and period with `count`, `sum`, `last` by the event's time, whatever order the events were recorded in, or `mean`; a missing or null value adds nothing. An event whose time is not a strict ISO 8601 time with its offset on a calendar day, a date alone among them, or names a leap second, whose series is not text of 1 to 256 bytes, or whose value is not a finite number, adds to no row: the dataset counts it in `passed_over` and the host notes `record_passed_over` with the reason `refused`, at most ten a dataset a page. A data test that raises passes its event over the same way, since the event caused it. One that runs out of time or memory meets a limit no event causes, as the engine treats a deadline or a memory stop, so it never passes its event over: the page ends before that event, the dataset's `tries` on its row count one more, and it rests at its checkpoint until the projector's next sweep, a second by default, which tries it again, as the views of recall functions are tried; a page that folds without a stop sets `tries` back to 0, and at `triesBeforeStall` tries, which the server gives as the bound the views of recall functions stall at, `overtimesBeforeStall` of `@on.auto/recall`, twenty, the dataset stalls, phase `stalled`, its stall naming the event's record and the stop's words, the host notes `dataset_stalled` with the record and the words, and it folds nothing more until no active definition names its data part. A page reads, of the rows it changes, only those of the series and periods its events touch, one indexed row a pair, and learns which of its series are new with one probe a series over a `VALUES` list, `(SELECT 1 … LIMIT 1) IS NOT NULL`, which both stores run once a series and which reads at most one indexed row of it, so a page reads at most twice as many rows as it has events, 2,000 for 1,000, however many periods its series hold. The probe is not an `EXISTS`: PostgreSQL turns that into a join, and over a table it has no statistics of yet, as after a rebuild, into one that reads every row of the dataset, 126 ms for a page of 1,000 series over 1,000,000 rows against 11 ms for the probe and 47 to 52 ms for the `SELECT DISTINCT series … IN` it replaces; SQLite took 1.7 ms against 43 ms. A page's rows, its counts, its tries and its checkpoint are written in one transaction, which commits only if the checkpoint it read is still the row's.

`datasetsPortOn(database)` reads a dataset's row for `get_definition`'s `standing` (`src/standing/prediction-standing.ts`): `kind`, `data_sha256`, the phase, the checkpoint, the counts, the first and last period and `stall`, null until the dataset stalls and then `{ record, detail }`; a function that names an uploaded dataset stands as `{ kind: uploaded, sha256 }`.

## The files a build reads

`writtenDatasetFiles({ database, content, playbook, document, now })` writes the two files a build will read, through the content port, each kept under its SHA-256 (`src/files`): the scoring rows, the dense grid of the `window` periods that ends at the last period closed at `now`, and the training rows, the same without the last `holdout` periods. The header is `series,period`, the target and the features in the document's order; the rows are sorted by series, as their UTF-8 bytes sort, then by period, in CRLF records of RFC 4180; a period with no event is 0 for `count` and `sum` and empty for `last` and `mean`; a series observed in fewer than `holdout + 1` periods of the window is left out. It reads the dataset's row, the counts, the series and every row of both files in one snapshot of the host's database, `readConsistently`, so the two files are of the one checkpoint it names however far the fold moves on while they are written, and it never reads again. It refuses a dataset past 200,000 rows or 1,000 series, with the figures it counted, every series of the window observed often enough counted, or a file past 64 MiB, `too_large`, a dataset not yet live, not kept or stalled, `not_ready`, and a function that names an uploaded dataset, `uploaded`, each as a `DatasetFilesRefused` with its words, and a refusal keeps nothing: the scoring rows are written first, and the training rows are fewer, so the bytes can only be refused before anything is kept. A build calls it as it starts, and a shadow at each close it predicts at, through a content port of its own that writes the scoring rows into the model processes' rows cache. A function that names an uploaded dataset is read by `uploadedDatasetFiles` (`src/build/uploaded-files.ts`) instead: the upload kept under its digest, parsed as it streams, its last `window` periods up to its own last period the scoring rows and the same without the last `holdout` the training rows, in the same grammar, a period no row names empty, with no checkpoint.

## The upload

`uploadDataset(content)` is the operation `upload_dataset`, `PUT /v1/orgs/{org}/playbooks/{playbook}/datasets/{sha256}` under `playbook:write`, served over HTTP alone, so it is no tool on any MCP endpoint. Its body is `text/csv` with a `Content-Length` of at most 64 MiB; it is read as a stream (`src/upload`), checked as UTF-8 and against the grammar of the files above with any target and feature names, CRLF or LF records and a leading byte order mark allowed, and kept through the content port with the path's digest expected. A body that breaks the grammar is refused `invalid_input` with the line of its first fault, one whose digest is not the path's with both digests, the body's the one the port computed as it kept the stream, and nothing is kept then. The answer is the digest, the bytes, the rows, the series, the columns, the grain and the first and last period.

## A run

`makePredictionFunctionAdapter({ pool, datasets, ports, mostFunctions })` makes the capability, type `prediction`, media type `text/markdown`, guide `prediction-function`; the server gives it the computation pool, for the check, the host's `DatasetsPort`, and the `PredictionPorts` a build and a question use: the runtime's ledger with its content, the host's database for the consistent reads of the files, the model runtimes, `runtimes`, the implementations of `prediction/model-runtime` the server loaded, in their order of precedence, the model processes, the two file caches and the scoring rows they parsed, the scoring permits, the bounds of a build (`buildsPerDay`, `buildsAtOnce`, `mostOpenRequests`) and the count of the playbook's open requests. Its `mostActive` is `mostFunctions`, 32 unless the server's `PREDICTION_MAX_FUNCTIONS` says otherwise. A run (`src/run`) refuses an input nested past 512 levels, one the question's or the build's schema refuses and a question whose series takes more than 256 bytes, a further check of the question's schema (`src/document/derived-schemas.ts`), `invalid_input`, with `checkedInput` of `@on.auto/definitions`, one sentence for each issue at its pointer, and is then a build, `{ build: {} }`, or a question. `finishesLaterFor` is false for a question, so a question's run cannot be cancelled from outside while its call is in flight, and true for anything else, and `longestRunFor` is 10 seconds for a question and, for a build or an input it cannot tell, the build's wait, `source.expires`, plus eight candidates scored at ten minutes each and a minute to start (`src/run/run-lengths.ts`). The capability declares, on a build's run, `dataset_assembled`, `candidate_uploaded`, `candidate_refused`, `candidate_scored`, `candidate_disqualified`, `baseline_scored` and `baseline_not_walked`, and the stream kind `models`, whose stream `models/<function>` holds `version_built`, `shadow_predicted`, `shadow_graded`, `shadow_passed`, `shadow_failed`, `version_activated` and `activation_refused`, and the claims of its builds, `build_asked` and `build_ended` (`src/facts`); `record_passed_over` is a note of the host, not a fact.

## Model runtimes

The capability declares the extension point `modelRuntimePoint` (`src/runtimes/model-runtimes.ts`), `prediction/model-runtime`, an `ExtensionPoint` of `@on.auto/definitions`: it accepts the contracts `acceptedModelRuntimeContracts` of the contract package names, and takes as an implementation a value `ModelRuntimeSchema` decodes whose binding's module is a file. The formats offered a function, `formatsAcceptedFor`, are those whose runtime's capabilities take what the function feeds, `fedShapeOf`: its features, its given features and a total with `accumulates`, and its longest horizon. ## A build

A build's run (`src/build/build-run.ts`) refuses, as its first act and recording nothing more than its start and its rejection, a build while the server scores `buildsAtOnce` builds across every playbook, those standing `scoring`, counted on the projection's `builds_scoring` index, `unavailable`, `builds_full`; and, for a build that asks through the inbox, a playbook with its `mostOpenRequests` open, `unavailable`, `requests_full` (`src/build/build-refusals.ts`). A build waiting for its trainer or for an approval holds no model process, so it is not counted. Both bounds are read and then decided on, so builds asked at the same moment may pass them by as many, as the interaction capability's `roomFor` allows its own. It then reads the function's claims on `models/<function>` and refuses what the claim below would refuse, with the claim's own decision (`claimable`), so an ask refused for a build in flight or for the builds of the day writes no rows and no brief. It then writes the two files, refused `unavailable`, `dataset_not_ready`, with the because `stalled` once the dataset stalled, or `conflict`, `dataset_too_large`, with the figures of `writtenDatasetFiles`; walks each baseline the function names over the scoring rows in a model process, a `baselines` job within 30 seconds, a deadline of its own, that runs as a long job of the pool, as a walk does, and reads the target of each row alone (`src/build/perfect-baselines.ts`, `src/model-jobs/baselines-job.ts`), and refuses `conflict`, `baseline_perfect` when a baseline loses 0 on `evaluation.metric` at every pair whose loss is defined and at one at least (`losesNothing` of `src/scoring/metrics.ts`), since no loss is below 0 and `p_better` counts the resamples whose mean favours a candidate strictly, so every candidate would be `not_better`, which a flat holdout gives `persistence` under `mase`; a baseline whose loss is defined nowhere is not called perfect; refuses `conflict`, `unscorable` when the metric judges no period held back, which with a truth held back only `mase` does, no series that holds one having a scale, every one still over the training periods or too short for one (`judgesNoPeriodHeldBack` of `src/scoring/metrics.ts`), and a baseline walks a series, since every candidate would then be `not_better`, `p_better` being 0 over no differences, though `winkler` and `quantile`, which scale nothing, judge the same rows; a build whose baselines walk no series is judged by its coverage alone and goes ahead; a check that finds every process a long job may hold taken waits for one within those 30 seconds, as every job of the pool waits for its permits within its deadline, and answers `unavailable`, `model_process_busy` when none comes free, and a process that ends before it answers `unavailable`, `model_process_failed`, with the because `baseline_check`, whose words say the process that checks the build's baselines ended and ask for the build again; renders the brief over them, closed with a section `## Candidates` that names the formats offered the function, or says that none is (`src/build/build-brief.ts`), `conflict`, `unworkable` past 64 KiB, and keeps it through the content port as `text/markdown`; and claims the build on `models/<function>` (`src/models/build-claims.ts`), after its files, that check and its brief, so a build refused for any of them takes none of the day's builds. `claimsDecider` records `build_asked { build, asked_at }`, after `build_ended` for each earlier claim whose run has ended, or that never came to wait for its trainer within the minute a build has to start, and refuses a build while another of the function is in flight, its run not ended, `conflict`, `build_in_flight`, naming its run, and once the function's `buildsPerDay` builds were asked over the trailing 24 hours, `unavailable`, `builds_full`, with the because `builds_of_the_day`, naming when it may ask again. The stream's versioned append serialises the claims, so these two bounds are exact: of two builds asked at once, both may pass the first read, and the second is decided again over the first's claim and refused, its rows and brief kept under their digests and named by no record. A build whose version is in its shadow or awaits its approval is not in flight, since its run has ended. The run then records `dataset_assembled` with the files' digests, sizes and figures and defers with its request record (`src/build/build-request.ts`). With `source.to` the record is the inbox's: `to`, a `message` within 8 KiB that names the run, its expiry, the three routes, the formats an upload takes and how to answer, then the brief's opening, cut at a code point, the `answer_schema` `{ candidates, recipe?, limitations? }`, `expires_at`, `requested_at` and `build: { brief_sha256, brief_bytes, training_sha256, training_bytes, routes, formats }`. ## The trainer's routes

`readBuildBrief`, `readBuildRows` and `uploadCandidate` (`src/trainer`) are the operations `read_build_brief`, `read_build_rows` and `upload_candidate`, `GET /runs/{run_id}/build/brief` and `/rows` under `playbook:read` and `POST /runs/{run_id}/build/candidates` under `playbook:write`, served over HTTP alone. Each loads the run's stream and answers only a build that waits for its answer (`src/trainer/waiting-builds.ts`): a run the playbook does not hold is `not_found`, a run of another kind `conflict`, `not_a_build`, a build that asks no trainer `conflict`, `no_trainer`, and a build that took its answer, whose request or child ended, or whose run ended, `conflict`, `build_ended`. The brief is the content its record names, as `text/markdown`; the rows the training rows, streamed from the content port as `text/csv`.

An upload names its `format` in the query string, `?format=`, which the HTTP layer reads beside a streamed body as it reads the path, and the format is chosen before the body is read (`src/trainer/candidate-formats.ts`): left out, it is the one format offered the function when one is; a format not offered it, or none named where several are, is refused `invalid_input` at `/format`, naming the formats offered, and so is any upload to a build of a function no runtime of the server executes, each with the body unread and nothing recorded. An upload's body is an `application/octet-stream` with a `Content-Length` of at most 64 MiB, which the HTTP layer requires before it reads the body. It is written as it streams into the models cache, under the name its format gives it, its SHA-256 computed as it is written, and checked in a model process by the runtime of its format, a `check` job within 30 seconds (`src/trainer/candidate-checks.ts`) that runs as a long job of the pool, as a walk does, so uploads never hold the processes a question needs. The upload takes one of the scoring permits before it reads the body, and answers `unavailable`, `model_process_busy`, with the body unread when none is free. The check: the runtime loads the file, answers the signature it found in it, which the capability records as given, and says whether the model takes what the function feeds, `{ features, given, horizon, totals }`, or why not; what the release's `series` runtime checks is in [its package](../../packages/prediction-series/README.md#a-recipe). A file refused records `candidate_refused { sha256, bytes, because, detail }`, `because` `failed_to_load`, `wrong_signature` or `too_many`, and answers `invalid_input`; a check that finds every process a long job may hold taken answers `unavailable`, `model_process_busy`, recording nothing. A file accepted is recorded `candidate_uploaded { sha256, bytes, format, runtime, signature }`, `runtime` the `name` and `version` of the runtime that checked it, under a rule that refuses a ninth or a repeat, the record tried again up to three times while the run's stream changed meanwhile, and then staged under the build's run through the content port, so nothing is staged that no record names and the unstaging at the run's end misses; a stage the run's end overtook is refused by the content store, which keeps nothing of it, and answers `conflict`, `build_ended` (`src/trainer/candidate-staging.ts`). The same bytes again answer the same digest and record nothing, and stage the file when nothing is staged under that digest, as when the server stopped between the record and the stage, so a retry repairs it. The answer is `{ candidate, bytes, format, signature }`.

The answer to a build is `request_answered` on its run, by `answer_interaction` for an inbox request or, through a function, the `/request` child's `run_succeeded`. `buildAnswerIssues` (`src/checks/build-answers.ts`) is the capability's `answerIssues`: a digest no `candidate_uploaded` of the build carries is refused `invalid_input` at its pointer, and a `recipe` or `limitations` past 8 KiB at its own. The answer of a `/request` child is checked the same way before anything of it is scored, and one with issues ends the build `rejected`, `invalid_input`, its issues under `/answer` (`src/build-work/request-child.ts`).

## The builds projection and its due work

`predictionBuilds` (`src/builds/builds-projection.ts`) is the keyed projection `prediction_builds`, version 1, over the run streams and the version facts of the `models` streams, one row a build keyed by its run id, a child's facts keyed by the run that called it and a model fact by the build it names: the `function`, its `definition_version`, the `standing` (`starting_request`, `waiting_for_trainer`, `scoring`, `in_shadow`, `awaiting_approval`, `ended`), `through`, `requested_at`, `expires_at`, the candidates `uploaded` and `scored`, the `winner`, the `version`, the `rule`, the `shadow_origins` predicted, `next_due_at`, `open`, `scoring_since`, set when the build's answer arrives and cleared when its version is built or its run ends, `ended`, `staged` and the `cause`, the message whose fold last changed it: every fold that changes the row sets it, the start of a build's `/request` child that moves the row to wait for its trainer among them. Its indexes are `by_open` for the standing and `builds_due` and `builds_scoring` across playbooks. The due work advances `next_due_at` and `staged`, and the projection says which folds set each: `next_due_at` every fact it folds but `candidate_uploaded`, and `staged` that fact alone, so the ledger's upsert of any other fact leaves an advance of `staged` as it stands, and that of an upload an advance of `next_due_at`.

`buildsDue({ ports, runs })` (`src/builds/builds-due.ts`) is the host's due work in the lane that does not call out: each row whose `next_due_at` has passed is performed, and each perform reads the build's run, its facts and the function's models and does one thing, recording one fact or changing one row (`src/build-work/run-steps.ts`, `run-ended.ts`). A perform changes the row only while it still holds the `cause` the perform read, so a fact folded into the row meanwhile, such as the answer of a trainer or of an approver, leaves it due, and the next perform reads that fact. While the run waits: a build through a function starts its child once under the derived id, through `runs`, with `calledBy: { runId, reference: '/request', run: 1 }`, and a child refused, or one that ends without an answer, ends the build `rejected`, reason `unanswered`, with the child's kind or `undelivered`; an inbox build nobody answers is ended by the playbook's requests at its `expires_at`, `rejected`, reason `unanswered`, kind `expired`, which closes its row; an answered build scores its next unscored candidate, then records the baselines, then ends; and a build that asks no trainer, or whose trainer answered no candidate, first fits its candidates (`src/build-work/fitted-candidates.ts`). That step takes a scoring permit, puts the training rows in the rows cache and asks the fitting runtime for its models in a `fit` job within 30 seconds, a long job of the pool; it uploads each model it answers as the trainer's upload would, checked, recorded `candidate_uploaded` or `candidate_refused` and staged under the build's run (`src/trainer/fitted-uploads.ts`), and records `candidates_fitted { runtime, candidates, detail? }`, `runtime` null when no runtime fits the function any more and `detail` the words of a fit that ended without an answer, after which the build scores those candidates as it scores a trainer's, the playbook named as the caller who answered. A perform that finds no scoring permit free, or every model process taken, rests the row a minute. Once the run has ended, however it ended, the candidates staged under it are unstaged, a child still in flight, as a build cancelled through `cancel_run` leaves it, is cancelled, and a build that succeeded with a version runs its shadow and its activation; once its row closes, by an activation or a refusal, its `/approval` child, if one is still open, is cancelled. A restart carries on from the last fact recorded, since every perform reads what was recorded rather than what it remembers, and servers sharing a database perform the work of the one that holds the host's lease.

## Scoring

A candidate's scoring (`src/scoring/scoring-step.ts`) takes one of `buildsAtOnce` scoring permits, puts the staged file in the models cache and the scoring rows in the rows cache, and walks the candidate in one `walk` job within 10 minutes, by the runtime that executes its format now, a candidate whose format none executes any more being `not_loaded` (`src/scoring/scoring-step.ts`, `candidate-walk.ts`): the child streams the rows file, keeping each series' values and features and never the text, and, for each series and each of the `holdout` origins, feeds the window up to the origin and answers its `q` (`src/model-jobs/walk-job.ts`). A walk whose build's run ends meanwhile, as one cancelled through `cancel_run`, is let go within a second, its job ended and its process and permit freed (`src/build-work/run-watch.ts`). The server (`src/scoring/candidate-scoring.ts`, `metrics.ts`) reads each step the holdout holds and scores it per horizon, over the scoring rows it parses once a build and shares between the candidates and the baselines (`src/scoring/scoring-inputs.ts`): `mase`, the absolute error over the series' in-sample naive error, one period or one season back; `winkler` at 80%, the interval's width plus ten times the distance of a truth outside it; `quantile`, the pinball loss of the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles; and the coverage. The baselines `persistence` and `seasonal-naive` are forecast by the playbook's own code over the same origins (`src/scoring/baselines.ts`), each with an interval from its own errors over the training periods, and recorded as `baseline_scored` with their scores and the series they walked, or `baseline_not_walked` when no series is long enough. Against each baseline the moving-block bootstrap (`src/scoring/bootstrap.ts`) resamples the differences of the primary metric at each origin 1,000 times, from a seed of the run id's SHA-256, and gives `p_better`, the share of resamples whose mean favours the candidate. Its block is the season when three blocks of it fit the origins, and otherwise the cube root of the origins rounded up, at least 1: a block as long as the origins draws the same resample every time, so `p_better` would be 0 or 1 and `confidence` would mean nothing. A candidate whose percentiles at any origin put a low above its likely value or a high below it is `answered_badly`, by the rule a question's answer is checked by, `rangeHolds` of the series shape (`src/scoring/quantile-order.ts`), so no version that passes can be refused by every question at such an origin and no Winkler score is negative; the shadow counts such a series among those with no number. The candidates a runtime fitted are scored as a trainer's are, against the same baselines by the same `confidence`, so every version beats them; a runtime that fits its own leaves out the models that forecast as a baseline does, which would be `not_better` against themselves, and a build whose fitted candidates all lose to the baselines keeps no version, the function answering from the default forecast still. The recipes are not put among a trainer's candidates, so a version's `trained_by` and the run's `candidates` always name the candidates of the caller that answered. A candidate is `candidate_disqualified` with its `because`, `timed_out`, `out_of_memory`, `crashed`, `not_loaded`, `answered_badly`, `coverage_below_floor` or `not_better`, or `candidate_scored` with the `runtime` that walked it, its scores, coverage, `p_better` and duration. The winner is the scored candidate with the lowest mean of the primary metric over the horizons, the first answered on a tie.

With `accumulates`, each origin is the end of a period and the quantity the total to the end of its enclosing period, fed `h`, the periods left; `persistence` is the run-rate, the mean of the enclosing period so far times the periods left, `seasonal-naive` the same periods' sum one season earlier, and `mase` is scaled by the run-rate's error over the training periods (`src/scoring/accumulated-baselines.ts`).

## The ending of a build

Once every candidate is scored and every baseline recorded (`src/scoring/ending-steps.ts`), a winner is kept as the next version: `version_built` on `models/<function>`, decided by `modelsDecider` (`src/models/model-decider.ts`) once a build, after `activation_refused { because: superseded }` for every earlier version still in its shadow or awaiting its approval, since the newest version is the one challenger, with the version's card (`src/scoring/version-card.ts`): the build, `trained_by`, the caller whose answer brought the candidates, by `id` and `name`, the dataset's digests and figures, the `file`, its digest, size and `format` and the `runtime` that scored it, the `signature` its runtime answered at the upload, the features, the scores, the coverage, `p_better` against each baseline, the baselines' figures and the rule, at most 4 KiB as JSON, the features kept as content when the card would pass it, and the answer's `recipe` and `limitations` kept as content and named by their digests. The run then succeeds with `{ version, winner, candidates }`, each candidate with its verdict, as `playbook:<id>`; `version` and `winner` are null when none won, and the build ends there.

## Shadow and activation

A build with a version moves to its shadow (`src/shadow`). The first version of a function, a function over an uploaded dataset and `shadow_periods: 0` pass at once, `shadow_passed` with no figures. Otherwise, at each of the `shadow_periods` closes of the grain after the version was built, or with `accumulates` of the enclosing period, its origin the last period that period holds and its target the periods to the end of the next, found by `periodsToTheEnd` (`src/shadow/shadow-schedule.ts`, `src/periods/enclosing.ts`), the due work writes the dataset's scoring rows at that close into the rows cache and walks the new version and the active one from that origin, one `shadow_predicted` a version with the `runtime` that walked it, null when none of the server executes the version's format, the series it predicted, `count`, and those its walk answered with no number or one that is not finite, `missing`, its predictions kept as content. Once a predicted period closes, it grades both against the dataset's rows over the series both versions predicted, `shadow_graded` with each metric, its pairs, the series it answered and those with no truth, `missing`, a stored prediction decoded so that one with no number is no prediction (`src/shadow/shadow-grades.ts`). Once every grade is in, `shadow_passed` or `shadow_failed` with the two versions' figures of the primary metric over those pairs, the new version passing when its figure is no higher than the active one's. A new version that answered nothing fails when the active one answered, and passes when only it answered; when neither has a graded pair, as before any truth arrives, the shadow is not decided and waits for one more close, up to `mostShadowPeriods`, 52, closes in all: a shadow that reaches them with no graded pair fails, `shadow_failed` with `ungraded_closes`, and the version is refused `shadow_failed`, so a target whose events stop does not keep both versions walking at every close and the build's row open. A verdict that arrives for a version a newer one superseded meanwhile records nothing, and the models projection takes the verdict of its challenger alone. Between closes the row rests until the next.

A version that passed activates by its rule (`src/shadow/model-lifecycle.ts`): `automatic` records `version_activated { rule: automatic }`; `{ approved: <function> }` starts the `/approval` child with `{ function, version, card }` and records `version_activated { rule: approved, approved_by }` when it answers `approve`, or `activation_refused { because: approver_rejected, refused_by }`, `approved_by` and `refused_by` the caller who answered the approval, by `id` and `name`, as its answer holds it, `unanswered` when it ends unanswered; a version that failed its shadow records `activation_refused { because: shadow_failed }`; and a version still in its shadow or awaiting its approval when a newer one is built records `activation_refused { because: superseded }`, its `/approval` child cancelled, and the newer version runs a shadow of its own. `activateVersion` (`src/models/activate-version.ts`) is the operation `activate_version`, `POST /definitions/prediction/{name}/activate` under `playbook:write` over HTTP alone, which records `version_activated { rule: requested }` for a built version whatever its shadow decided, `not_found` for a version not built and `conflict`, `already_active` for the active one; a requested activation of a version whose build had not settled settles it.

`predictionModels` (`src/models/models-projection.ts`) is the keyed projection `prediction_models`, version 1, over the `models` streams, one row a function: the `active` version with its data and file digests, its file's format, which `version_activated` carries as `file_format`, and the definition version it was built from, the `latest`, how many `versions`, the `challenger` in its shadow with its build, the `shadow_predicted` and `shadow_graded` counts and the `shadow` result. `get_definition`'s standing (`src/standing/prediction-standing.ts`, `model-standing.ts`) adds `models`, read from it, and `build`, the open build of the builds projection with its standing, `requested_at`, `expires_at` and its candidates uploaded and scored, beside the dataset.

## A question

A question's run (`src/question`) reads the function's row of the models projection, and before a version is active answers from the default forecast (`src/question/default-forecast.ts`): the fitting runtime's `defaultModel` for the function's shape, asked in a `default` job within 10 seconds, kept in the models cache by its digest and run as a version's file is; the run's record then says `version: null` and `default_forecast: true`, and its words that no version of its model is active. With no runtime that fits the function, a question before a version is active is refused `conflict`, `no_active_version`. Once one is, it refuses `data_changed` once the document's `dataSha256` is not the active version's, or once its `horizons`, its `given` names or its `accumulates`, on which the model's signature depends, are not those of the definition version the active version was built from (`src/question/active-version.ts`). It reads the series' window: of a projected dataset at its checkpoint, the last `L` periods up to the last closed one, refusing a dataset not live `unavailable`, `dataset_not_ready`, with the because `stalled` once it stalled; of an uploaded dataset in a model process, a `window` job within 10 seconds over the upload in the rows cache, which streams the file and keeps the one series asked, its last `L` periods up to the upload's own last period (`src/question/series-window.ts`, `src/model-jobs/window-job.ts`), so the server parses nothing of an upload for a question and holds none of it; and refuses a series the window does not hold `invalid_input`. It feeds `y`, `x`, `given`, its values repeated over the steps ahead, with `accumulates` `h`, the periods left in the enclosing period, and to a runtime that states `calendar` `at`, the index of the origin's period; and runs the active version's file, from the models cache, by the runtime that executes its format, in a `run` job within 10 seconds, on a process that holds it when one does; a version whose format no runtime of the server executes is refused `unavailable`, `model_process_failed`, with the because `no_model_runtime`. It reads the three numbers of each step asked from `q`, every horizon or the one the question names, the nearest first, or the one total with `accumulates`, and succeeds with the prediction, a series of the function's grain and unit with its `origin` and the question's `marks`, and two lines: `observed`, the last `predicts.history` periods of the window it fed, which the window carries as doubles, the dataset's own values, and feeds to the model as float32, a period with no value left out; and `predicted`, a point for each step with its `value`, `low` and `high`, or, with `accumulates`, `total`, one point at the last period its enclosing period holds, since every `at` of a series is of its grain, with the enclosing period as the record's `total_to`, which the run's words say the total by the end of (`src/question/question-run.ts`). The series shape closes its records, so `version` and `coverage` are in the record, `{ version, default_forecast?, runtime, coverage, decimals, total_to?, origin, dataset_checkpoint, duration_ms }`, `runtime` the `name` and `version` of the runtime that answered, and `decimals` the most decimals any value the series observed in the window carries, at most 2 (`decimalsObserved` of `src/capability/figures.ts`), to which the run's words write each figure it predicts (`observedFigure`), so a series of whole numbers is answered in whole numbers. Every process taken answers `unavailable`, `model_process_busy`; a process that dies or runs out of memory answers `unavailable`, `model_process_failed`, as does an answer of another shape; and a number that is not finite, which the process answers as a failed job so that the pool lets it go, answers the same with the because `not_finite`, which no retry resolves. The answer is then checked against the series shape of `@on.auto/definitions`, the check every output of the shape takes, before it is answered (`src/question/answer-check.ts`): a low above its likely value or a high below it, the one fault a model's finite numbers can give, answers `unavailable`, `model_process_failed`, with the because `not_in_order` and the shape's own words for the fault, which no retry resolves either; any other mismatch is of the answer the runtime built around those numbers, which the model did not write, so it is a defect of the runtime: the call fails with an incident and the run is `failed`. The points ahead are sorted nearest first however the document orders its horizons, which the same check would refuse otherwise.

## The model processes

`modelPorts({ pool, scoringAtOnce, directory })` (`src/model-processes/model-ports.ts`) gives the model processes, the two file caches, the scoring rows parsed for the builds being scored and the scoring permits from the server's process pool. `modelProcesses` sends each job to `modelProcessModule`, the `ModuleAddress` of `src/model-jobs/model-process.ts`, which the pool of processes calls as it makes the job, so the main entry reads no address as it loads (`src/model-processes/model-processes.test.ts`), and which serves the engine's process loop with the jobs of `src/model-jobs/model-jobs.ts`. A `check`, `run` or `walk` job names, as `runtime`, the module of its runtime's binding, which the process imports and whose `loadModel(path, threads)` loads the file (`src/model-jobs/runtime-modules.ts`), so the process loads a runtime only when a job asks for one: `check` answers the signature the runtime found and the issue of the model with what the function feeds, or the first line of what the load threw, `run` answers a question's `q`, `walk` answers every origin's `q` of every series of a rows file, `window` answers the last window of one series of an uploaded rows file, and `baselines` answers the first baseline of a build that already loses nothing over the scoring rows, or none; the last two read rows and load no runtime. The child parses a rows file as it streams (`src/files/series-rows.ts`), so its heap holds the series' values and features and never the file's text. The deadlines are `modelDeadlines`, 30 seconds, 10 seconds, 10 minutes and 30 seconds for the check of a build's baselines, a window's a run's, at which the pool kills the child; each counts from the moment its job holds a process, so a job's wait for one never shortens its own time. A model's job is keyed by its runtime's module and the model's digest, a window by its rows', so a process that loaded a model serves its next job; the child keeps one loaded model a runtime and file, its intra-op threads the larger of 1 and the cores divided by the processes (`threadsOf`), and answers an allocation the runtime could not make, a `RangeError` whose message holds `allocation failed`, as the process loop's memory stop. `walk`, `check`, `baselines` and `fit` jobs are long jobs of the pool, at most `PREDICTION_BUILDS_AT_ONCE` of its processes, so a question always finds one. A runtime that states `fits` also serves `fit`, which reads the training rows' targets and answers the models `fitModels` fits, `{ format, bytes }` each, within 30 seconds, and `default`, which answers the model `defaultModel` gives for a function's shape within 10. A walk yields to the event loop between series and stops at its signal, so a walk served in the server's own process never holds the loop for a whole rows file and stops within a series of its deadline.

A runtime whose binding is `inline` runs its jobs in the server's own process: `inlineModels` (`src/model-processes/inline-models.ts`) is the engine's `inlinePool` over the same jobs, as many at once as the pool has processes and as many long ones as it has long jobs, each answered `timed_out` at its deadline or `cancelled` at its caller's signal, which it signals to the job, an allocation that fails answered as out of memory and anything else it throws as a crash; `modelProcesses` sends each job of such a runtime to it in place of the pool, admitted alike, and the server closes it with the pool. Only a job that yields can stop: the walk yields between series and stops at the signal, and the other jobs run to their end. So the point reserves the binding for the release, `reservedForTheRelease`, which the server applies to every extension that is not one of the release's own.

`fileCache` (`src/model-cache/model-files.ts`) keeps the files a model process reads under `PREDICTION_CACHE_DIR`, `/var/tmp/auto-runtime` by default, the models in `models` and the rows in `rows`, at most 1 GiB each, each file in a folder of its own named by its digest, `models/<sha256>/model.<format>`, the format of the file (`modelFileName`), and `rows/<sha256>/rows.csv`, so the folder a model's runtime reads external data from holds that one file and a candidate that names another file by its digest finds none. A file is written in a folder of its own, `.<uuid>`, as it streams, its digest computed as it is written, and that folder renamed to the digest once the file is whole, so a process never reads half a file; the files used least recently are removed past the bound, never the one just written; a file the cache does not hold, or holds no longer, is written again from the content port, and one whose bytes are not its digest is removed and refused. Every folder is made with mode 0700. As the server starts, it makes the cache folder with mode 0700 if it is not there, and refuses to start, naming `PREDICTION_CACHE_DIR`, when the folder cannot be made, is not a folder, belongs to another user or others may write to it (`cacheFolderProblem` of `@on.auto/operations`), so no other user can make the folder first and own what the server trusts; it then reads each folder found in the cache again and keeps it only when its file's bytes are its digest, removing anything else (`src/model-cache/cached-files.ts`). `parsedRows` (`src/model-cache/parsed-rows.ts`) keeps in the server's memory the scoring rows a build's candidates and baselines are scored over, parsed once a build, 63 and 66 MiB of heap and buffers in two parses of the widest rows the bounds admit on 2026-10-13: for at most `buildsAtOnce` builds, the one used least recently let go, and only until the build's run ends, when its due work lets them go (`src/build-work/run-ended.ts`).

`measure-models.ts` takes a fourth argument, `inline`, which serves the same jobs through `inlineModels` in the measuring process, as the server serves a runtime bound inline; `pnpm --filter @on.auto/prediction-series measure` gives it the runtime's own module, its default recipe and its linear recipe, whose figures are in [that package's README](../../packages/prediction-series/README.md#memory-and-time).

## Testing

`@on.auto/prediction/testing` exports `weeklyOrders`, the example document, `weeklyOrdersLines` and `weeklyOrdersWith(...replaced)`, which changes its lines, `uploadedWeeklyOrders(sha256)`, the example over an uploaded dataset, the daily documents and rows the build tests use (`dailyVisits`, `dailyTotals`, `dailyRows`, `steadyVisits`, each `...With` and `uploaded...`), `trainerlessDailyVisits(sha256)`, a daily function that names no trainer, and `testPorts`, the `PredictionPorts` over a memory ledger with both projections, a real process pool closed after the test and caches in a scratch folder, its model runtimes the scripted one of `src/runtime-testing` unless the test names others, its module, deadlines, processes and bounds the test's to choose, and its content and model processes the test's to wrap. The dataset's tests run on SQLite and, with `LEDGER_TEST_POSTGRESQL_URL` set, on PostgreSQL, each on a database of its own, through the suites in `src/dataset-testing`, which drive the host's projector with `projectedFolders` of `@on.auto/workflow-host/testing`; the builds and the questions over a projected dataset run the same way through the suites in `src/build-testing`, with their events laid out around one moment the suites own, `startedAt` in `src/build-testing/suite-clock.ts`, and a clock that starts there and runs at the pace of the real one, on a monotonic time that stands still, `steppingClock(0)` of `@on.auto/definitions/testing` over the shifted time of day, which the playbook's calls and its due work read as Effect's `Clock`, so they pass alike on whatever day they run and no render of a brief is stopped by a slow machine. The run's tests use a fake datasets port, a fake `SavedDefinitions` that keeps content too and a pool whose checks at save may take 30 s, so a check that a busy machine slows is not refused by the 2 s a save allows (`src/testing/prediction-runs.ts`); the brief renders at save and at a build's start on Effect's `Clock`, which the run's tests and the build playbook give as `standingClock` of `@on.auto/definitions/testing`, the real time of day on a monotonic time that stands still, and the brief's tests as `steppingClock`, standing still or moving by a step each time it is read, so a brief is refused for its time by the clock of its save and never by a slow machine; and `src/testing/cancels-in-a-call.ts` cancels a run from outside while its call is in flight, through `cancel_run`, just before its call records its end.

A build's tests drive a playbook of the operations' testing harness (`src/build-testing/build-playbook.ts`, `playbook-calls.ts`) and the due work by hand, one perform at a time or until nothing is due (`due-driving.ts`), with the candidates of `src/runtime-testing/scripted-candidates.ts`, files of the scripted runtime (`scripted-runtime.ts`), a model runtime of the contract whose format is `scripted` and whose module, `scripted-recipes.ts`, loads a JSON recipe: each a recipe whose answer the test knows, among them a mean, a last value, an interval too narrow or too wide, a model that answers `NaN` or whole numbers, one with an input the function does not feed, one whose allocation fails, a running total and a file that is not a recipe, so the capability's tests load no runtime of the release. The scripted runtime also fits its own models, the last value and the mean, or a run-rate for a total, each with a known interval, and answers the last value as its default forecast, so the tests of a build no trainer answers and of the default forecast run on it too; `scriptedRuntimeThatFitsNothing` fits none, and `inlineScriptedRuntime`, bound inline and reading the calendar, serves the same jobs through the inline pool and is fed `at`. The tests that score, check and answer run the real model process, which loads the scripted runtime's module as it loads any runtime's; those that must not spend a deadline, or a process that ends, sleeps, waits until the test lets it answer, allocates, cannot read its rows, answers what a model does not or answers a number that is not finite in a walk, run the scripted model process of `src/build-testing/scripted-models.ts`, a module written to a scratch folder that serves the real jobs or the next script a test names. A test names the next script by writing it beside the script and renaming it over the script, so a process that reads the script while a test names one reads the script before or the one after, never the empty file a write in place leaves for a moment: a held check that read it empty would end, free its lane, and let a walk the test means to find every lane held run instead of being told the processes are busy. The child's own module is tested in the test process, since the pool's children are ended with `SIGKILL`, which writes no coverage.

The tests of the scoring keep the 1.5 s of a walk in `src/scoring/scoring-runs.test.ts` on purpose: a scripted walk that sleeps is timed out at it, and a walk that finds the two lanes of long jobs held waits it for a lane and is told the processes are busy, both of which a slow machine only lengthens; the walks that crash, exhaust their memory or answer badly end in a process already warm, and the real walk scored last runs in the warm process of its candidate.

## Source

`src/index.ts` is the entry point and `src/testing/index.ts` the entry point of the test support. `src/document` holds the document: its type, the front matter, the parts and parsing, and the derived schemas. `src/data` holds the data part, its filters, pointers, measures and digest, and `src/brief` the brief's variables, template and largest dataset. `src/periods` holds the calendar: civil days, grains, enclosing periods and the moments an event's time is read as. `src/dataset` holds the folder, its tables and statements, the fold and the port; `src/files` the files a build reads and their streaming parse; `src/upload` the upload's operation, its CSV reader and grammar, and the reading of a kept dataset's head. `src/capability` holds the capability, its ports, its bounds, its words and the figures they print, and its guide, the public reference page, served to agents as `prediction-function`; `src/checks` its checks at save, of the functions it calls and of an uploaded dataset, and of a build's answer; and `src/standing` the standing `get_definition` shows. `src/run` holds a run, its two kinds and its lengths; `src/build` a build's start, its refusals, its files, its brief, its request and the record it defers with; `src/trainer` the trainer's routes, a candidate's format, its checks, its records, its staging and the upload of a fitted model; `src/facts` the facts of a build and of the models; `src/builds` the builds projection, its due work, a build's state and the document it was built from; `src/build-work` the steps of that work, from the request's child and the fitting of candidates to a run's end and the watch of a walk's run; `src/scoring` the scoring step and the walk of a candidate, the scoring grid, the metrics, the baselines, the bootstrap, the verdicts, the ending and the card; `src/models` the models stream's decider, the claims of builds, its state, its projection and the activation operation; `src/shadow` the shadow's schedule, predictions, truths, grades and the activation that follows; and `src/question` a question, the version or the default forecast that answers it and its refusals. `src/model-processes` holds the model processes, the inline pool, their ports and the scoring permits; `src/model-jobs` their module and jobs, the import of a runtime's module and its fitters, the feeds, the walk and the window; `src/runtimes` the extension point of the model runtimes, the runtime that executes a format, the runtime that fits a function's models, the formats offered a function and their words; and `src/model-cache` the file caches, their folder's checks and the parsed rows. `src/dataset-testing` and `src/build-testing` hold the suites and the harness the tests share, and `src/runtime-testing` the scripted runtime and its candidates.
