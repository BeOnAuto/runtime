# @on.auto/content

The content model every store of the runtime shares: the bytes a playbook keeps by their SHA-256 digest beside its messages, the port that keeps and reads them, the refusals of a put, the reading of a stream in chunks through the digest, the check of what a store reads back, and the digest itself. It is a package of its own because the ledger, the object store of [`@on.auto/object-storage`](../object-storage/README.md) and the operations all build on it, and it needs nothing of the runtime but `effect` and Node's digest; the operations bind the port to the playbook of a call (see [Recorded content](../operations/README.md#recorded-content)).

## The port

A tool call's arguments and a tool's answer are kept whole, whatever their size, beside the messages rather than in them, since every decision on a stream and every projection rebuild reads its facts back, and so is any content a capability keeps by its digest. `RecordedContent`, the port the ledger implements, keeps content under a `ContentOwner`, `{ org, playbook }`, the playbook it is kept for, which a `PlaybookAddress` of `@on.auto/operations` is, takes bytes as a stream and computes their digest itself (`src/content/recorded-content.ts`):

- `put(playbook, { stream, mediaType, expected? })` reads a web `ReadableStream<Uint8Array>` in chunks of `mostContentChunkBytes`, 1 MiB, digests it with SHA-256 as it passes and measures it, keeps it under the playbook and its digest, and answers `ContentKept`, `{ sha256, bytes, mediaType }`. `expected`, `{ sha256?, bytes? }`, is what the caller asserts of the bytes: a stream whose digest or size is not the one expected is refused with `ContentRefused` of `because: 'not_its_digest'` or `'not_its_size'`, carrying as `found` the digest and size the port read, so a route words the mismatch without digesting the bytes again, and a stream that fails before its end with `because: 'stream_failed'`, carrying the stream's own error as `cause`; each carries a `detail` sentence, so a route answers every refusal as one `invalid_input`. Nothing of a refused put is kept: no read finds it, and the ledger deletes the chunks it wrote, as its README says.
- A put of a digest the playbook already holds keeps the content it holds, with the media type it was first kept with, and answers that one, so an identical answer is kept once however often it comes and a second media type never replaces the first, with one exception: on an object store that ignores `If-None-Match` on a copy, two puts of one digest made at the same moment with two media types leave the later one's media type over identical bytes, after the earlier put answered a media type the store no longer holds, as the [object storage README](../object-storage/README.md#a-put) says.
- `get(playbook, sha256)` answers a `StreamedContent`, `{ stream, bytes, mediaType }`, or nothing for a digest the playbook does not hold; a read whose bytes are not its digest's fails as a defect before it hands over the last of them (`notTheBytesOfItsDigest`). `verifiedStream(sha256, bytes, source)` is that check for every adapter: it reads the stream an adapter has, digests and counts each piece, hands over the piece that reaches the size the adapter knows only once the size and the digest are checked, and fails on a stream that ends short of the size, goes past it, or gives a piece that is not bytes.
- A `ContentStore`, `{ content, sweepContent }`, is an adapter of the port with the sweep of what its puts left behind, `sweepContent(before)`, which deletes the pieces of puts that began before that time and never finished; the ledger runs the sweep of its own store or of the store it is given.

## The digest and the streams

The digest is Node's, behind the one module of the repository for it (`src/content/content-digest.ts`), which serves the whole repository, the hex SHA-256 of a license key, of a fixture or of an answer the server keeps among them: the digest of what is in hand, `sha256Of(text or bytes)`, is the one-shot form, `hash('sha256', content, 'hex')`, and the digest of a stream, `sha256Digest()`, is the incremental one, `createHash('sha256')`, which takes the stream in parts; a text is digested and kept as its UTF-8, so its digest is the SHA-256 of the text. `takenInChunks(stream, chunkBytes, take)` reads a stream through the digest in chunks, answering its digest, size and number of chunks or a `stream_failed` refusal, `requireExpected(expected, taken)` refuses a digest or a size that is not the one expected, and `leftUnread(stream)` cancels a stream no one reads; the ledger, the object store and the in-memory content keep through the first two, and the ledger and the object store read back through `verifiedStream`. `textToKeep(text, mediaType)` makes the content of a text to put, `contentOfText` and `contentOfBytes` a `StreamedContent` of a text or of bytes in hand, and `textOfContent(content)` reads one back as text, failing as a defect on bytes that are not UTF-8.

## Testing

`@on.auto/content/testing` keeps recorded content whole in memory, `memoryRecordedContent()`, by the rules of the port the ledger keeps, its refusals and its first media type among them, which the in-memory ledger of `@on.auto/operations/testing` keeps its content in and the tests of other packages take alone too, and `keptTextOf(content, playbook, sha256)` reads a kept text back as text.

`recordedContentBehaviour(entry)` is the behaviour suite of the port, in `src/testing`: a content of no bytes to 64 MiB kept and streamed back, a text kept once with its first media type in its playbook alone, a content put again whole or with its digest, the refusals of a wrong digest, a wrong size and a stream that fails, wrong bytes put with the digest of a content held, and a wrong put racing a right one. An adapter runs it with a `ContentEntry` whose `aStore()` answers `{ content, elsewhere, unnamedLeft }`: the port, the port of a second client of the same store, and how many pieces of puts no kept content names the store holds, which is 0 after every test. This package runs it on `memoryRecordedContent`, the ledger on SQLite and PostgreSQL, and `@on.auto/object-storage` on its fake bucket and on a real S3-compatible server. The samples it is made of are exported with it: `alpha` and `beta`, two playbooks of one org, `octets` and `json`, two media types, `generated(bytes, seed)` and `generatedStream`, bytes made the same for a seed, `answerOfAbout(bytes)`, a JSON answer of about that size, `readBack` and `readThrough`, which digest what a read streams, `deliveredBeforeFailing(stream)`, how many bytes a failing stream handed over, `brokenAfter(bytes)`, the content of a stream that fails once it handed over that many bytes, and `resultOf`, `refusalOf` and `present`, which run an effect for its value, its failure or its value present.
