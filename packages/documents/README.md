# @on.auto/documents

The text of a document the runtime reads, read in a process of its own or in a pool a host serves itself. Its entry exports `documentsReadIn(pool)`, `unreadDocuments`, `documentBounds` and `documentTooLarge`; `textOfDocument({ bytes, mediaType, name })` (`src/reading/text-of-document.ts`), which the process runs and the entry does not export, since pdf.js takes over the buffer of the bytes it is given, answers `{ read: true, text, mediaType }` for a document of a type it reads, or `{ read: false, because, mediaType }`, the words of why it does not, for any other and for one it cannot read, `mediaType` being the type it read the document as. `documentsReadIn(pool)` answers a `DocumentReading`, which runs it as a job of the pool, in a process of a `ProcessPool` of `@on.auto/workflow-engine/dsl` under the pool's memory limit and a deadline, or in any pool that answers the same jobs, taking the pool as `DocumentProcesses`, the one method of it the reading calls, `run(job, signal)`, which the engine's `ProcessPool` and `InlinePool` both have as they are, so the package names no type of the engine but those of its process loop, and `unreadDocuments`, the `DocumentReading` of a composition with no pool, which reads nothing. `@on.auto/documents/jobs` exports the jobs the process runs, for a host that serves them in a pool of its own (see [A pool of a host's own](#a-pool-of-a-hosts-own)). `@on.auto/tool-servers` hands a `DocumentReading` the bytes of every answer of an HTTP API that is not JSON, so a tool that fetches a file answers its text (see [An HTTP API as a tool server](../tool-servers/README.md#an-http-api-as-a-tool-server)); the server gives it `documentsReadIn` over a process pool of its own, of `DOCUMENT_PROCESSES` processes, 2 unless set, apart from the pool of its model processes, under the same memory, `PREDICTION_PROCESS_MEMORY`, and closed with it, so reads of documents and the jobs of models never wait on one another. The package imports no other package of the runtime but the process loop of the engine, `@on.auto/workflow-engine/process-loop`, which the module of its process runs.

## What it reads

The media type is read without its parameters and in lower case, and one that is not written as a type and its subtype, as RFC 6838 names them, is read as `application/octet-stream`, so the type a document answers is never anything else. Where it is `application/octet-stream`, the type that says nothing of the bytes, the extension of `name` stands for it: `.pdf`, `.docx`, `.txt`, `.md`, `.csv`, `.json`, `.xml`, `.yaml` and `.yml`, in any case, and the document answers the type it names.

| Type                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Read as                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Every `text/*` type; `application/json`, `application/xml`, `application/csv`, `application/markdown`, `application/yaml` and `application/x-yaml`; and every type that ends in `+json`, `+xml` or `+yaml` | Its bytes in the encoding of its byte order mark, UTF-8, UTF-16LE or UTF-16BE, which wins, or else of its `charset` where the platform knows it, or else UTF-8, the mark dropped and a byte that is not of the encoding read as U+FFFD (`src/readers/plain-text.ts`) |
| `application/pdf`                                                                                                                                                                                          | The text of each page, in turn, its lines as the PDF ends them, the pages joined by a blank line and a page with no text left out (`src/readers/pdf-text.ts`)                                                                                                        |
| `application/vnd.openxmlformats-officedocument.wordprocessingml.document`                                                                                                                                  | The text of its paragraphs, each followed by a blank line, as `mammoth` extracts raw text (`src/readers/word-text.ts`)                                                                                                                                               |

Any other type, an image, a spreadsheet file or an archive among them, is not read, in the words `The runtime does not read image/png as text; it reads text, PDF and Word documents`.

## Bounds

`documentBounds` (`src/reading/document-bounds.ts`) holds them, with their words:

| Bound                     | Value  | Past it                                                                                   |
| ------------------------- | ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The bytes of one document | 20 MiB | `It takes more than the 20 MiB the runtime reads of a document`, `documentTooLarge`       |
| The pages of one PDF      | 500    | `It has 612 pages, more than the 500 the runtime reads of a PDF`, read before any page is |

Beside them, a reading through `documentsReadIn` is bounded in time by the deadline its caller gives, which `@on.auto/tool-servers` gives as what the call has left of its `callMs`, 30 s, and in memory by the process of the pool it runs in, which the server sets by `PREDICTION_PROCESS_MEMORY`, 1,280 MiB unless set, or by the limits of the isolate a host's own pool runs it in; past either it answers in words (see [The process](#the-process) and [A pool of a host's own](#a-pool-of-a-hosts-own)).

The text a document answers is not bounded here: the caller bounds it as it bounds any answer, as `@on.auto/tool-servers` bounds the text of an answer of an HTTP API by `httpAnswerBytes`, and what a model reads of it by its room.

## The process

`documentsReadIn(pool)` (`src/process/pooled-documents.ts`) sends the bytes, the media type as written, the name and `textBytes`, the most bytes of text its caller takes, to a process of the pool that runs `src/process/document-process.ts`, whose job `text` (`src/process/document-jobs.ts`) runs `textOfDocument` and cuts the text at `textBytes`, at a whole character, with a last line, `[The text was cut to … of its … bytes, the most the runtime answers of a document here.]` (`src/reading/text-within.ts`), so no more text than the caller takes crosses to its process, with the deadline it is given and the signal of the call, and answers what the process answers. The pool kills the process at the deadline or when the signal ends the call, and ends it where it takes more than its memory: the data `prlimit --data` lets it reserve where the server finds `prlimit`, as the image does, and the heap of V8 everywhere. A document a process does not answer for is answered in words, never as a failure of the caller:

| The pool answers | The words                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `timed_out`      | `It took longer to read than the 2.5 s the call had left, so its reading was stopped`                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `out_of_memory`  | `It took more memory to read than a process that reads a document may take, so its reading was stopped`                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `cancelled`      | `The call ended before it was read`, or the words of `timed_out` where the signal ended the call as out of time, a `TimeoutError`, as the call's own deadline ends it; the pool counts its deadline from when a waiting read gets a process, so the call's signal is the bound of the whole call |
| `busy`           | `Every process that reads a document stayed busy for the time the call had left`                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `closing`        | `The server was closing, so it was not read`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `crashed`        | `The process that read it ended without an answer:` and what the pool says                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |

As many documents are read at once as the pool has processes. A reader that meets an allocation that fails, a `RangeError` whose message holds `allocation failed`, lets it through, never refusing the document as broken (`refusedUnlessOutOfMemory` of `src/reading/document-text.ts`, by `isAllocationFailure` of `@on.auto/workflow-engine/process-loop`), so the process loop answers it out of memory, as the pool answers a process the limit kills. Nothing of a document stays in the server's process but its bytes and its text: the readers, their libraries and what pdf.js sets on `globalThis` load in the process of the pool alone. `unreadDocuments` answers `The runtime reads no document here, since it was composed with no process to read one in, and it reads none in its own`, so a composition that gives no pool parses nothing in the process that serves requests.

## A pool of a host's own

`@on.auto/documents/jobs` (`src/process/document-jobs.ts`) exports `documentJobs()`, the handlers the module of the process serves, `ProcessHandlers` of `@on.auto/workflow-engine/process-loop`, and `documentJobKind`, `text`, the kind of their one job. It is an entry of its own for a host that runs its programs in isolated workers rather than child processes, where no process is spawned for `documentsReadIn` to read in: such a host serves these handlers in a pool of its own and composes `documentsReadIn(itsPool)`, so the reader has one implementation, which the module of the process serves in the Node shell and the host's pool serves elsewhere. The entry leaves them out, as it leaves out `textOfDocument`, so a composition reads a document only through the pool it gives, and one that gives none answers `unreadDocuments`, as before.

The job `text` takes the payload `{ bytes, mediaType, name, textBytes }`: the bytes, a `Uint8Array`, the media type as written, the name or `null`, and the most bytes of text the caller takes. It answers `{ read: true, text, mediaType }` or `{ read: false, because, mediaType }`, as `textOfDocument` does, a document past the 20 MiB or a PDF past the 500 pages of [Bounds](#bounds) refused in their words and the text cut at `textBytes` as [The process](#the-process) cuts it. A payload of another shape raises, as does an allocation that fails in a reader, which the reader lets through (`refusedUnlessOutOfMemory`). The handler takes the signal a `ProcessHandler` takes and reads none, since no reader yields while it reads (below). It takes over the buffer of a PDF's bytes, as `textOfDocument` does, so a pool that hands it the caller's bytes rather than a copy, as `inlinePool` does, leaves them read.

A host's pool runs a job `{ module, key, kind, payload, deadlineMs }` with the call's signal and answers a `ProcessOutcome` as the engine's pools do; `documentsReadIn` calls nothing else of it. It may leave the module and the key unread, as `inlinePool` does, and only a pool that calls the module's address reads one. Its endings take the words of the table of [The process](#the-process): a pool that ends a job at its memory limit answers `out_of_memory`, as it answers a handler that raises an allocation that fails, by `isAllocationFailure` of the process loop, and one that ends it at its time or CPU limit answers `timed_out`, so the reading answers the package's words of memory and of time and the host invents none.

A host bounds the isolate that runs the handlers, since nothing within it can: no reader yields to the timers of its isolate while it reads, so a pool that serves the jobs in the isolate that asks can neither stop one at its deadline nor catch the memory a reader exhausts. Measured on 2026-10-29 on the machine of [Measured](#measured), Node 26.11.1, at a load average over one minute of 2.7, through `inlinePool` of the engine: the PDF of nested drawings `pdfOfNestedDrawings({ fanOut: 10, depth: 7 })`, 2,096 bytes, given a deadline of 1.5 s, read for 21.5 s while a timer of 10 ms never turned, and answered its text; and the Word document that unpacks to 60 MiB, `wordDocumentRepeating('a', 1_800_000)`, given a deadline of 3 s, ended its process in 10.6 s at V8's heap limit, 4,045 MB, with `FATAL ERROR: Ineffective mark-compacts near heap limit Allocation failed - JavaScript heap out of memory` and no answer. `inlinePool` therefore serves a host whose isolate is bounded of itself, which answers the end of its isolate as the end of the job; the tests run the handlers through it with documents that end within their time.

## The readers

Each reader is given the bytes and nothing else: no path, no address, no fetch. A reader that throws on a broken document answers its first line in words, `It is not a PDF the runtime can read: InvalidPDFException: Invalid PDF structure.`, and never fails the caller. Each loads its library with the first document of its type, through a dynamic import, so a process that reads none loads neither library. The bytes are read where they are, never copied: pdf.js takes over the buffer of a PDF, which the caller does not read again.

- A PDF is read by pdf.js, `getDocument` of the build `unpdf` resolves, its own build of pdf.js for servers, which runs the code pdf.js runs in a worker in the same thread, so reading starts no worker thread. It is given no system fonts, no font faces, no WebAssembly, no fetch in a worker and no address of the standard fonts, the CMaps or the ICC profiles, so it reads nothing beyond the bytes, and it logs nothing below errors. Its page count is read first, then each page's text content in turn, each page's resources freed once its text is read. A PDF whose pages hold no text, as a PDF of scanned pages does not, answers `It holds no text, as a PDF of scanned pages holds none: the runtime reads no text in an image`. Loading pdf.js sets four names on `globalThis`: `DOMMatrix`, which `unpdf` defines where the platform has none, as Node has none, and `pdfjsLib`, `pdfjsWorker` and `_pdfjsTestingUtils`, which pdf.js sets itself; they stay in the process of the pool that read a PDF.
- A Word document is read by `mammoth.extractRawText` from a buffer over the bytes, which leaves out its formatting and reads no file a document links to, since `mammoth` reads one only when it is told to.

Both are pure JavaScript, install with `--ignore-scripts` and need no native build. They are pinned to exact versions.

| Package   | Version | Installed                                                     | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | What it does not read                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| --------- | ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `unpdf`   | 1.9.1   | 2,173,664 bytes, no dependencies                              | It ships pdf.js 6.4.299 built for servers in one module with no dependency, where `pdfjs-dist` 6.4.299 names `@napi-rs/canvas`, a native package, as an optional dependency, which an install of the npm package would fetch; the peer `unpdf` names, `@napi-rs/canvas`, for rendering pages, is optional and not installed | Text drawn as an image, as a scanned page is, since nothing here reads text in an image; a PDF that needs a password; and text in a font that needs one of the predefined CMaps pdf.js would load from the files it is given no address of, as some fonts of Chinese, Japanese and Korean text do |

The sizes are of the packages as installed, measured on 2026-10-29.

## Measured

On an Apple M4 Max with 16 cores and 128 GB, Node 26.11.1, measured on 2026-10-29 in a pool of four processes of 1,280 MiB, where `prlimit` is not found, so V8's heap is the bound of memory, at a load average over one minute of 4.5: a PDF of one page read in 182 ms in a pool with no process yet, starting one and loading pdf.js, and in 2 ms the next time; a PDF of 500 pages of 40 lines, 1.7 MB, written by `pdfOfPages`, in 268 ms; a PDF of 2,096 bytes whose page draws a drawing ten times, each drawing the next ten times, six levels deep, in 23.7 s, and one of 2,318 bytes seven levels deep stopped at its deadline of 5 s, in 5.03 s; and a Word document of 179,043 bytes whose part unpacks to 60 MiB, written by `wordDocumentRepeating`, ended out of memory in 9.8 s. Measured on the same machine in the process of the reader, before the pool: one of 501 pages refused in 2 ms, a Word document of 2,000 paragraphs, 195 KB, read in 58 ms, and a PDF of two pages with its fonts embedded, 15 KB, written by another program, read in 45 ms, loading pdf.js, and in 7 ms the second time.

## Testing

`@on.auto/documents/testing` (`src/testing/written-documents.ts`) writes the documents the tests read, so no binary fixture is kept: `pdfOfPages(pages)`, a PDF 1.7 of one page for each list of lines, in Helvetica, the font a reader of PDF holds without its file, with its cross-reference table; `pdfOfNestedDrawings({ fanOut, depth })`, a PDF of one page whose form draws the next `fanOut` times, `depth` forms deep, a few kilobytes that cost a reader time and memory ten times over at each level; `wordDocumentOf(paragraphs)`, a `.docx` of one paragraph for each text, its parts deflated in a zip file; and `wordDocumentRepeating(paragraph, times)`, a `.docx` of one paragraph many times, which unpacks to far more than it takes. `documentsReadInProcess`, a `DocumentReading` that reads in the process of the test and cuts the text as the process does, is for the tests of the packages that take one, never for a composition, and takes over the bytes of a PDF as the process does. `src/reading/text-of-document.test.ts` reads each type through `textOfDocument`, with a fetch that fails the test if a reader calls it; `src/reading/text-within.test.ts` cuts a text at a whole character and lets an allocation that fails through a reader; `src/process/inline-documents.test.ts` serves the handlers of `@on.auto/documents/jobs` through `inlinePool` and reads through `documentsReadIn` over it a PDF, a Word document named for its type, a text file, an empty text, a PDF that holds no text, a document past 20 MiB, a PDF past 500 pages and a text cut at its bound, and answers the words of memory where a reader meets an allocation that fails and of time at the pool's deadline; `src/process/pooled-documents.test.ts` reads through a pool of two processes of 768 MiB, cuts a text in the process, stops the nested drawings at their deadline and at the call's, answering the words of time for both, and answers each ending of the pool in its words through a fake pool; `src/process/out-of-memory.test.ts` stops the Word document that unpacks to 60 MiB with the words of memory, whether the limit kills the process or a reader meets the allocation that fails, through a module whose reader meets one, and reads the next document; and `src/process/document-process.test.ts` starts the module of the process and sees it ready.

## Source

`src/index.ts` is the entry point. `src/reading` holds `textOfDocument`, the types it reads and their readers, the bounds and the shape of what it answers; `src/readers` the reader of text, of PDF and of Word; `src/process` the reading in a pool, its jobs, which `@on.auto/documents/jobs` exports, and the module a process of the pool runs; `src/reading/text-within.ts` the cut of a text the process answers; `src/testing` the writers of the testing entry and the reading in the process of a test.
