# @on.auto/activity

The organization's activity in Auto Runtime: what the playbooks of an org did that a person may want to see, kept for the whole org so a client shows it with one read however many playbooks the org holds, and what each caller has seen of it. It owns the fact `activity_seen`, the comments people make on the things of a playbook and of the organization, with their facts `comment_made`, `comment_resolved` and `comment_reopened`, five keyed projections of the ledger, the org operations `list_activity` and `mark_activity_seen`, and four operations of comments, `make_comment`, `list_comments`, `resolve_comment` and `reopen_comment`, each served for a playbook and for the organization's own, defined on the application layer, [`@on.auto/operations`](../operations). [The organization's activity](../../docs/reference/http.md#the-organizations-activity) and [Comments](../../docs/reference/http.md#comments) in the HTTP reference document the routes in full, and [Addresses](../../docs/reference/addresses.md) the address a comment names what it is about by.

## The facts

A caller marks rows seen with one `activity_seen`, recorded through `OrgWriter` on `org/<org>/activity/<id>`, the stream of what that caller has seen. A caller's id, such as `person:<subject>`, holds characters a stream name does not, so `<id>` is the UUID version 5 of the caller's id; the raw id is the context's `by`, as every fact of an org carries it with `at` (`ActedContextSchema`).

| Field     | What it holds                                                                                                                  |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `items`   | The `new` and `comment` rows the caller has seen, each `{ address, at }` as the row gives them, `at` in ISO 8601; may be empty |
| `through` | Left out, or `{ at, playbook, key }`, the place in the activity everything at or before which the caller has seen              |

`ActivitySeenSchema` is its schema. Its fixture is `src/facts/0.56.0/activity_seen.json`, listed in `recorded-facts.json`, and the server reads it among its known facts.

A comment is a stream of its own, the id a UUIDv7 the runtime gives it, with `ActedContextSchema`: a playbook's is `playbook/<org>/<playbook>/comments/<id>`, written through `PlaybookWriter`, and one of the organization's own is `org/<org>/comments/<id>`, written through `OrgWriter`. `comment_made` holds `{ address, text, made_by: { id, name }, anchor? }`, the caller as the runtime names them, as `definition_created` keeps `created_by`, and `anchor`, `{ x, y }`, each a number from 0 to 1 with at most 4 decimals, where on the thing the comment was dropped, left out for a comment made without one; `comment_resolved` holds `{ how }`, `acted` or `dismissed`; and `comment_reopened` holds no data, `{}`, its context saying who reopened the comment and when. A `comment_made` recorded before `anchor` existed has none, and reads as a comment made without one. A comment is never edited. The decider records `comment_made` once on a new stream, `comment_resolved` only on a comment that is open and `comment_reopened` only on one that is resolved, so resolving twice or reopening an open comment records nothing, and a reopened comment can be resolved again. `CommentEventSchema` is their schema, and their fixtures are `src/facts/0.56.0/comment_made.json`, `comment_resolved.json` and `comment_reopened.json`.

## The projections

Each fact changes one row of a projection, so a run that succeeds, which both makes its own row and resolves its definition's broken row, is kept in two tables. Times are whole milliseconds since 1970-01-01T00:00:00Z, so a read pages and compares on them. A resolved request or broken definition stays in its table, closed, and is never served: every read and count asks for `open = true`.

| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `activity_1`          | A playbook's `runs` and `definitions`                                        | `runs/<run id>` for a run, `definitions/<type>/<name>/<version>` for a version saved | A run no other run started, from its `run_started`, which keeps who started it, its trigger and output shape, `listed` once its `run_succeeded` lands; and each `definition_created` and `definition_updated`, listed at once, with `change` `created` or `updated`. A run a workflow's call started keeps no row                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `activity_requests_5` | A playbook's `runs`                                                          | The run's id                                                                         | Who started the run, from `run_started`; `open` from a `run_deferred` whose request takes an answer, a step's included, with the first line of its message (at most 200 characters), its answer schema (at most 8 KiB as JSON, otherwise none), whom it waits on and when it expires; closed by `request_answered` or the run's ending                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |

A failure of an older version, a cancelled run and a request nobody answered write nothing                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `activity_seen_1`     | An org's `activity` streams, `scopes: ['org']`                               | The caller's id, the context's `by`                                                  | Where the caller marked everything seen through, `through_at`, `through_playbook` and `through_key`, which only moves forward, and `seen_at`, when it last moved; and `marked`, a JSON list of the 1,000 items the caller marked last after `through`, newest first, each `{ address, at }`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `comments_2`          | A playbook's `comments` streams, and an org's, `scopes: ['playbook', 'org']` | The comment's id, the stream's id                                                    | The `address`, `text`, `by`, `by_name` and `at` of `comment_made`, `anchor_x` and `anchor_y`, the anchor in whole ten-thousandths of the box, `null` without an anchor, `open`; closed by `comment_resolved` with `resolved_at`, `resolved_by` and `resolved_how`, and kept, so `list_comments` reads a resolved comment; opened again by `comment_reopened`, which sets those three to `null` and `reopened_at` and `reopened_by` to its time and caller, `null` until the comment is first reopened and kept when it is resolved again; and the time and the caller the activity lists it by, those of `comment_made` and then of the last `comment_reopened`. `about_run`, `about_version` (`<type>/<name>/<version>`) and `about_definition` (`<type>/<name>`), read from the address, a part's included, are what a read under a run, a version or a definition matches; a read under any other address matches `about_thing`, the address without its `/parts/<part>`, so it holds the address and its parts, and a read under a part matches the part alone. A request waits on the person its party names by id, `person:` and the id; otherwise on the lower-cased address its party names where the party holds an `@`, and on `any_member` otherwise. `activityProjections` lists the five for a composition; the server opens its ledger with them.

## The operations

Both are org operations served over HTTP alone, permitted by `org:read` or `playbook:read`; `activityOperations` lists them for a catalog.

| Operation            | Kind    | Route                 | Input                                                                    | Answer                                             | Rejections of the handler                |
| -------------------- | ------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| `list_activity`      | query   | `GET /activity`       | `kind`, `playbook`, `mine`, `since`, `limit` (50, at most 200), `cursor` | `{ rows, has_more, next_cursor, counts, through }` | `invalid_input`, `not_found`             |
| `mark_activity_seen` | command | `POST /activity/seen` | `items` (at most 200), `through`                                         | `{ counts, through }` after the mark               | `invalid_input`, `forbidden`, `conflict` |

The four operations of comments are playbook operations, each permitted by `playbook:read`, since commenting, resolving and reopening are a reader's acts, and again org operations for the organization's own comments, under `/v1/orgs/{org}`, each permitted by `org:read` or `playbook:read`, whose `operationId`s are `make_comment_in_org`, `list_comments_in_org`, `resolve_comment_in_org` and `reopen_comment_in_org`; `commentOperations` lists the playbook's for a catalog and `orgCommentOperations` the organization's, and `commentChanges` names a comment made, resolved or reopened, `{ kind: 'comment', comment_id }`, among the changes of its playbook, or of the org for the organization's own. `make_comment` and `reopen_comment` are served over HTTP alone, so an agent neither comments on its own work nor reopens a comment; `list_comments` and `resolve_comment` are tools too. Each answers a comment with `studio`, the `comment` page of Studio that opens its address.

| Operation         | Kind    | Route                         | Input                                                                                                                      | Answer                                | Rejections of the handler                |
| ----------------- | ------- | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| `make_comment`    | command | `POST /comments`, `201`       | `address` (at most 512 characters), `text` (1 to 4,000 characters, trimmed), `anchor` (optional, `null` taken as left out) | The comment, open                     | `invalid_input`, `conflict`              |
| `list_comments`   | query   | `GET /comments`               | `address`, `under`, `open` (`true`), `limit` (50, at most 200), `cursor`                                                   | `{ comments, has_more, next_cursor }` | `invalid_input`                          |
| `resolve_comment` | command | `POST /comments/{id}/resolve` | `id`, `how`                                                                                                                | The comment as it stands, closed      | `invalid_input`, `not_found`, `conflict` |
| `reopen_comment`  | command | `POST /comments/{id}/reopen`  | `id`                                                                                                                       | The comment as it stands, open        | `invalid_input`, `not_found`, `conflict` |

`src/positions/comment-addresses.ts` reads and writes the address grammar, the parts and the organization's things included: an address of a form a comment takes, of the route's playbook, or of the organization's things on the organization's route, is all `make_comment` checks, never that the thing exists. The org's operations of comments act on the org's ledger alone and refuse nothing as `retired`.

## The reads

`list_activity` folds the org's register of playbooks, `org/<org>/playbooks`, which no keyed projection can fold since its name has no id: it gives the active playbooks the caller may access and a `new` row for each of the newest 100 changes that created or updated a playbook. For each of those playbooks it reads the open rows of the four tables of a playbook through `OrgReader.readPlaybookRows`, newest first by `at`, and merges them with the playbook rows newest first by `(at, playbook, key)`. It makes up to four of those reads at once, and counts up to four playbooks at once (`readsAtOnce` of `src/operations/activity-tables.ts`), so a reader that reaches a playbook's rows over the network is not waited on one read at a time, while a listing holds at most four of the ten connections of a PostgreSQL ledger's pool. A cursor is the base64url of the JSON `{ at, playbook, key }` of a row. A read orders by `at` alone in SQL, reading strictly before the cursor's `at` and after the `at` of `since`, and the rows that share one of those times, or the oldest time of a full read, whole, so that the order of rows of one time is decided in code, the same on SQLite and PostgreSQL, whose collation orders text otherwise.

The counts are over every active playbook the caller may access, whatever the filters: the open requests that wait on `any_member` or on the caller's own id, each of them for the local developer, and none that waits on an address, since the caller is known by their id alone; the open broken definitions; and the listed `new` rows, and apart from them the open comments, after the caller's `through`, less the caller's own and those the caller marked, counted through `OrgReader.countPlaybookRows` with `{ column, after }` on the column of the time each row is listed by: a `new` row's `at`, and a comment's time it was made or last reopened, the caller's own comment being one the caller made and nobody reopened since, or one the caller last reopened. A caller who has never marked anything reads as if `through` were 7 days before the read. A comment row is the open row of `comments_2` keyed `comments/<id>`, its `at` the time it was made or last reopened, its `by` who made it, its `message` the first line of the text, its `about` the address, and its `definition` and `run_id` read from that address, not looked up. For a caller who may access every playbook of the org, a read that names no `playbook` also reads the open rows of the org's own `comments_2` through `OrgReader`, as rows of kind `comment` whose `playbook` is `null` and whose address is `org/comments/<id>`, merged by `(at, playbook, key)` with the organization before any playbook, and the counts count them as a playbook's; inside, and in their cursor, their playbook is `''`, `{ at, playbook: '', key }`, which no playbook's id is. A `new` row is seen when its `by` is the caller, and a `comment` row when the caller made it and nobody reopened it since, or last reopened it; either is seen when the caller marked its address at its `at`, or when it is at or before `through`.

## Testing

`pnpm --filter @on.auto/activity test` runs every test beside the code, against the in-memory ledger of `@on.auto/operations/testing`, with 100% coverage per file. Each fold transition has a test in `src/projections`, and `src/operations` drives both operations through the dispatcher over a scenario of two playbooks (`src/testing/scenario.ts`); `src/comments` drives the operations of comments the same way (`src/testing/commenting.ts`). The server's `src/activity` drives the routes of the activity, and its `src/comments` those of comments, over HTTP and MCP on a temporary SQLite ledger.
