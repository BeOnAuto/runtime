# @on.auto/activity

The organization's activity in Auto Runtime: what the playbooks of an org did that a person may want to see, kept for the whole org so a client shows it with one read however many playbooks the org holds, and what each caller has seen of it. It owns the fact `activity_seen`, the comments people make on the things of a playbook and of the organization, with their facts `comment_made`, `comment_resolved` and `comment_reopened`, five keyed projections of the ledger, the org operations `list_activity` and `mark_activity_seen`, and four operations of comments, `make_comment`, `list_comments`, `resolve_comment` and `reopen_comment`, each served for a playbook and for the organization's own, defined on the application layer, [`@on.auto/operations`](../operations). [The organization's activity](../../docs/reference/http.md#the-organizations-activity) and [Comments](../../docs/reference/http.md#comments) in the HTTP reference document the routes in full, and [Addresses](../../docs/reference/addresses.md) the address a comment names what it is about by.

## The facts

A caller marks rows seen with one `activity_seen`, recorded through `OrgWriter` on `org/<org>/activity/<id>`, the stream of what that caller has seen. A caller's id, such as `person:<subject>`, holds characters a stream name does not, so `<id>` is the UUID version 5 of the caller's id; the raw id is the context's `by`, as every fact of an org carries it with `at` (`ActedContextSchema`).

| Field     | What it holds                                                                                                                  |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `items`   | The `new` and `comment` rows the caller has seen, each `{ address, at }` as the row gives them, `at` in ISO 8601; may be empty |
| `through` | Left out, or `{ at, playbook, key }`, the place in the activity everything at or before which the caller has seen              |

`ActivitySeenSchema` is its schema. Its fixture is `src/facts/0.56.0/activity_seen.json`, listed in `recorded-facts.json`, and the server reads it among its known facts.

A comment is a stream of its own, the id a UUIDv7 the runtime gives it, with `ActedContextSchema`. One on a definition or a step lies under the definition's key, `definition/<org>/<definition>/comments/<id>`, written through `PlaybookWriter.ofDefinition`, as a run does, so it is read wherever the names table places the definition and nothing is written when the definition moves or is renamed: the comment references the definition by its identity (Vernon, "Effective Aggregate Design, Part II"), found by its type and name in the names table at the moment of the command (Axon, "Set based consistency validation"; Marten, "Natural keys"). Every other comment of a playbook is `playbook/<org>/<playbook>/comments/<id>`, written through `PlaybookWriter`, and one of the organization's own is `org/<org>/comments/<id>`, written through `OrgWriter`. `comment_made` holds `{ address, text, made_by: { id, name }, anchor?, definition? }`: the address as it was when the comment was made, the caller as the runtime names them, as `definition_created` keeps `created_by`, `anchor`, `{ x, y }`, each a number from 0 to 1 with at most 4 decimals, where on the thing the comment was dropped, left out for a comment made without one, and `definition`, the id of the definition a comment on a definition or a step is about, which the one lookup that chose its stream also wrote, left out for every other comment; `comment_resolved` holds `{ how }`, `acted` or `dismissed`; and `comment_reopened` holds no data, `{}`, its context saying who reopened the comment and when. A `comment_made` recorded before `anchor` existed has none, and reads as a comment made without one. Both fields are optional keys of the same fact, so no new fact type was needed (Greg Young, "Versioning in an Event Sourced System", weak schema). A comment is never edited. The decider records `comment_made` once on a new stream, `comment_resolved` only on a comment that is open and `comment_reopened` only on one that is resolved, so resolving twice or reopening an open comment records nothing, and a reopened comment can be resolved again. `CommentEventSchema` is their schema, and their fixtures are `src/facts/0.56.0/comment_made.json`, `comment_resolved.json` and `comment_reopened.json`, and `src/facts/0.68.0/comment_made.json`, the shape that carries `definition`.

## The operations

Both are org operations served over HTTP alone, permitted by `org:read` or `playbook:read`; `activityOperationsOf()` lists them for a catalog.

| Operation            | Kind    | Route                 | Input                                                                    | Answer                                             | Rejections of the handler                |
| -------------------- | ------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| `list_activity`      | query   | `GET /activity`       | `kind`, `playbook`, `mine`, `since`, `limit` (50, at most 200), `cursor` | `{ rows, has_more, next_cursor, counts, through }` | `invalid_input`, `not_found`             |
| `mark_activity_seen` | command | `POST /activity/seen` | `items` (at most 200), `through`                                         | `{ counts, through }` after the mark               | `invalid_input`, `forbidden`, `conflict` |

The four operations of comments are playbook operations, each permitted by `playbook:read`, since commenting, resolving and reopening are a reader's acts, and again org operations for the organization's own comments, under `/v1/orgs/{org}`, each permitted by `org:read` or `playbook:read`, whose `operationId`s are `make_comment_in_org`, `list_comments_in_org`, `resolve_comment_in_org` and `reopen_comment_in_org`; `commentOperations` lists the playbook's for a catalog and `orgCommentOperations` the organization's, and `commentChanges` names a comment made, resolved or reopened, `{ kind: 'comment', comment_id }`, among the changes of its playbook, or of the org for the organization's own. `make_comment` and `reopen_comment` are served over HTTP alone, so an agent neither comments on its own work nor reopens a comment; `list_comments` and `resolve_comment` are tools too. Each answers a comment with `studio`, the `comment` page of Studio that opens its address.

`src/positions/comment-addresses.ts` reads and writes the address grammar, the parts and the organization's things included: an address of a form a comment takes, of the route's playbook, or of the organization's things on the organization's route, is what `make_comment` checks of its form. For a definition or a step it also finds the definition the playbook holds by that type and name, retired or not, and refuses with `not_found` where there is none, in the words a read of a definition uses; it checks nothing else about whether the thing exists. `src/positions/definition-addresses.ts` turns a definition's id and the recorded address into the columns above, and the address as it is now. `list_comments` turns an address of a definition or a step into its definition's id the same way, and an unknown name reads no comments; it answers each comment on a definition with the address of the playbook and the name the definition holds now, read once for each definition on the page (`nameHeldBy`), and the recorded one where the definition holds no name there. `resolve_comment` and `reopen_comment` find the definition whose key holds the comment's stream among those the playbook holds (`definitionHolding`) and write there, or on the playbook's own stream, so a comment whose definition moved away is `not_found` where it was. The org's operations of comments act on the org's ledger alone and refuse nothing as `retired`.

## The reads

`list_activity` reads the org's register of playbooks, `org/<org>/playbooks`, as two keyed projections kept in the transaction of every append to it: the playbooks, `playbooks_1` of `@on.auto/playbooks`, which give the active playbooks the caller may access, and the changes, `playbook_changes_1` (`playbookChangeRows` of `src/projections/playbook-change-rows.ts`, ledger layout 32), a row for each fact that created or updated a playbook, of which it reads the newest 100 by their position, a `new` row each. It reads the activity of all of those playbooks in one read of an entity kind of the entity API (`playbookActivityKind` of `src/entities/playbook-activity.ts`, [The entity API](../operations/README.md#the-entity-api)): its keys are the playbooks, and for each it answers what the playbook's requests set aside, its counts, the rows of the page asked, read from the open rows of its tables newest first by `at` with the run that started a broken definition and the names of its definitions applied, and the names of its goals where its rows hold one, so a store that keeps every playbook of the org answers all of them in one call; `mark_activity_seen` reads the same kind for its counts, with no page. The org's side merges those rows with the playbook rows newest first by `(at, playbook, key)`. Inside the read it makes up to four reads at once and reads up to four playbooks at once (`readsAtOnce` of `src/operations/activity-tables.ts`), while a listing holds at most four of the ten connections of a PostgreSQL ledger's pool. The kind's name is `playbook_activity`, or `playbook_activity_with_goals` where goals are kept (`activityEntityKinds` of `src/composition/activity-composition.ts`), and its keys, what it is asked and its values cross a boundary as data, decoded by the schemas of `src/entities/activity-wire.ts`. A cursor is the base64url of the JSON `{ at, playbook, key }` of a row. A read orders by `at` alone in SQL, reading strictly before the cursor's `at` and after the `at` of `since`, and the rows that share one of those times, or the oldest time of a full read, whole, so that the order of rows of one time is decided in code, the same on SQLite and PostgreSQL, whose collation orders text otherwise.

The counts are over every active playbook the caller may access, whatever the filters: the open requests that wait on `any_member` or on the caller's own id, each of them for the local developer, and none that waits on an address, since the caller is known by their id alone; the open broken definitions; and the listed `new` rows, and apart from them the open comments, after the caller's `through`, less the caller's own and those the caller marked, counted through `OrgReader.countPlaybookRows` with `{ column, after }` on the column of the time each row is listed by: a `new` row's `at`, and a comment's time it was made or last reopened, the caller's own comment being one the caller made and nobody reopened since, or one the caller last reopened. A caller who has never marked anything reads as if `through` were 7 days before the read. A comment row is the open row of `comments_3` keyed `comments/<id>`, its `at` the time it was made or last reopened, its `by` who made it, its `message` the first line of the text, its `about` the address, and its `definition` and `run_id` read from that address. A comment on a definition or a step lies under the definition's key and is read with it, so after a move or a rename its `about` names the playbook and the name the definition has now: for the rows of a page, the read of each playbook's activity reads `OrgReader.namesHeldInPlaybook(playbook, type)` once for each type of such a row, finds the definition the row lies under among those names, and composes its `about` from them and the version, step and part of the recorded address (`addressedAsHeld` of `src/positions/definition-addresses.ts`, as `list_comments` does), its `definition` read from that `about`; where those names hold no such definition, as when it moved again between the two reads, the row keeps the recorded address. The names read is the playbook's own table of names, the one writer of a definition's name, so no copy of it is kept with the activity. For a caller who may access every playbook of the org, a read that names no `playbook` also reads the open rows of the org's own `comments_3` through `OrgReader`, as rows of kind `comment` whose `playbook` is `null` and whose address is `org/comments/<id>`, merged by `(at, playbook, key)` with the organization before any playbook, and the counts count them as a playbook's; inside, and in their cursor, their playbook is `''`, `{ at, playbook: '', key }`, which no playbook's id is. A `new` row is seen when its `by` is the caller, and a `comment` row when the caller made it and nobody reopened it since, or last reopened it; either is seen when the caller marked its address at its `at`, or when it is at or before `through`.

## Testing

`pnpm --filter @on.auto/activity test` runs every test beside the code, against the in-memory ledger of `@on.auto/operations/testing`, with 100% coverage per file. Each fold transition has a test in `src/projections`, and `src/operations` drives both operations through the dispatcher over a scenario of two playbooks (`src/testing/scenario.ts`); `src/comments` drives the operations of comments the same way (`src/testing/commenting.ts`). The server's `src/activity` drives the routes of the activity, and its `src/comments` those of comments, over HTTP and MCP on a temporary SQLite ledger.
