## The runtime's own keys

The runtime keeps keys of its own in its secret store, beside the secrets it keeps for others, and names each by a fact in a stream of the runtime's and of no org, `runtime/own-keys`, as `@on.auto/connections` names the clients it registers dynamically in `runtime/client-registrations` (`src/secrets/own-keys.ts`, beside the composition of the store the key is read from, which `src/database/ledger-stores.ts` opens, moves, rotates and logs right before). There are two purposes: `retry_state`, the key that signs the state an MCP client sends back when it retries a call, as [`@on.auto/api`](../api/README.md) says, and `content_address`, the key that signs the address a person or a browser follows to the bytes a playbook keeps, as [`@on.auto/identity`](../identity/README.md#content-addresses) says.

At start, once the secret store is open and rotated, `ownKeysOf` reads the key of each purpose in turn, `retry_state` then `content_address`, the same way: it folds the stream, and where no key is recorded for the purpose it keeps 32 random bytes in the store, as their base64, and records the fact, which the ledger appends under the version of the stream it read; where the record is refused because another server recorded one first, it forgets the row it kept and folds again, taking that one. It then reads the row, logs under a secret manager `The runtime read the key it keeps for retry_state in AWS Parameter Store under <prefix> in <region>`, or `for content_address`, with the annotations `own_key`, the purpose, and `own_key_id`, the secret's id, which names its parameter, `<prefix>/<own_key_id>`, and gives the API `retryStateKeys` with the key of `retry_state` as `current` and no `previous`, and the routes of the content at a signed address, `contentAddressRoutes`, the key of `content_address`, so every server that shares the ledger signs under the same keys, and a rotation of the secrets key wraps each again and leaves it as it is. A deliberate reset of the runtime's own keys is not offered yet. Nothing of the key is logged, and no key is derived from another.

## Payload offloading

`src/settings/content-settings.ts` reads the two settings of payload offloading beside where content is kept: `PAYLOAD_OFFLOADING`, `true` or `false`, on when left out, and `PAYLOAD_SIZE_THRESHOLD_BYTES`, a whole number from 2,048 to 65,536, 16,384 when left out, the same as Auto's cloud (`mostInlinePayloadBytes` of `@on.auto/operations/payloads`), each refused at start by its name and never its value, in one sentence, `The payload settings are invalid. …`, and both keys of the configuration file, `payload_offloading` and `payload_size_threshold_bytes`, whose schema carries the same bounds. The least, 2,048, is `mostShownFieldBytes` of `@on.auto/operations`, the most a page of events shows of a field whole, so a payload kept outside is one a page shows as its size anyway. A threshold set while offloading is off is checked all the same.

`payloadOffloadingOf` of `src/composition/process-layers.ts` gives the runtime the switch, `PayloadOffloading`, and one number for both thresholds, `PayloadSizeThreshold` of `@on.auto/operations/payloads`, which the writers of facts read, and `HeldValueSizeThreshold` of `@on.auto/workflow-host`, which the host hands the engine for the values a workflow holds; the application layer composes it, from the settings the composition root reads, or from their defaults where a test composes none. `src/composition/payload-offloading.test.ts` fails on any other source of the runtime that provides one of the three. At start the server logs `Payload offloading is on: a payload larger than 16384 bytes is kept as content, and its record names it by its SHA-256 and its size`, or `Payload offloading is off: a new payload stays inline in its record, whatever its size`, with `payload_offloading` and `payload_size_threshold_bytes` (`logPayloadOffloading` of `src/logging/logging.ts`).

Every record says which arm it holds, its value or its digest and size, and every reader reads both, so a change of either setting applies to new payloads alone and rewrites nothing; a payload kept while offloading was on is still read once it is off. With the switch on, the workflow host also holds a playbook at a record of a known type it cannot read, rather than passing over it (`holdsUnreadableIn` of `@on.auto/workflow-host`), logged once as `fact_unreadable`.

**The content its records name.** As the ledger opens, `ledgerLayerFor` of `src/composition/ledger-store.ts` asks the ledger's chore `heldContentFound` of `@on.auto/ledger` to look for the first 16 contents its facts hold, by the key of their playbook and their digest, where the server keeps content now: in the ledger's database, or, with `CONTENT_STORE_URL`, in the bucket, one request each for the head of its object (`withItsContentFound` of `src/database/ledger-of-content-not-found.ts`). A content counts found exactly as a read finds it (`found` of the ledger's content), so a content kept outside before heads were is found where the bucket holds it. A look the bucket fails stops the start in the words of a bucket that cannot be reached, `CONTENT_STORE_URL names the bucket <bucket> on <host>, which could not be reached: <answer>` (`unreachableBy` of `src/composition/content-store.ts`, `src/composition/content-store.test.ts`), never as content missing. A content kept in the other place, or not there, stops the start with `LedgerOfContentNotFound`, which says where the ledger and the content are kept, how many of those it looked for are kept elsewhere or are not there, and what to do, and never names a digest, a value or a credential; `ledgerRefusalIn` of `src/database/ledger-refusals.ts` finds it, as it finds a ledger of another version, so the command prints it as a notice. It fires where `CONTENT_STORE_URL` was set, removed or pointed at another bucket or prefix on a ledger that holds content, and where the bucket was emptied or restored from another time than the database; it reads 16 heads at most, so a store that lost only some of its content may pass it. `src/database/ledger-of-content-not-found.test.ts` holds its words and a start refused over a ledger whose content is kept elsewhere, and `src/workflow-runs/kept-payloads-over-http.test.ts` a start refused over a kept answer gone from its store, on SQLite and PostgreSQL.

## The attempts it makes, and the runs whose process is gone

Each process of the server holds every attempt of a run it makes under a lease in the ledger's database, so a run whose process dies, runs out of memory or is killed is ended or resumed by itself rather than left `started` for ever. The composition root makes one id for the process, a random UUID at each start, which it gives the workflow host as `holder` and the live attempts of the application layer as theirs (`attemptHoldingOf`), with `WORKFLOW_SWEEP_INTERVAL`, so the lease lasts three sweeps and at least 30 seconds from its last renewal; it is not a setting of its own. `liveAttemptsOver` of `src/composition/process-layers.ts` wraps the core's `LiveAttempts` (`src/attempt-leases/leased-attempts.ts`): each attempt, `attempt` or `outliving`, takes its lease before its start is recorded, names it by its fencing token as the core tells it (`acquired`), and lets it go as it ends, or hands it over where its run is still started, as an attempt the server stopped while it ran, and also where the attempt was cut short, interrupted or dying, before it named its token, since its start may already be recorded and only the run's facts tell, which the sweep reads; a lease the database could not take, name or let go is reported as an incident and the attempt runs all the same. A process that stops closes its live attempts before its other work, and its stop begins there: the holding records that it began (`stopping`), and only then are the attempts interrupted and their leases handed over, while the host, which stops later, takes up no run of a gone holder once the stop began, nor one it handed out before (`src/attempt-leases/attempts-work.ts`), so a lease the process hands over as it stops is left for the next server, never taken up by the process that could make no attempt of it any more. The workflow host renews the process's leases at every sweep, whether it runs the workflows or stands by.

The host that runs the workflows ends what a lapsed or handed-over lease of another process names (`src/attempt-leases/attempts-work.ts`, `gone-holders.ts`), through the core's `deadAttemptEnder`, the run's own correlation as its lineage. A run that outlives its call and that no workflow's step made, a reasoning run a client or a trigger started, is resumed at once: the host takes a lease for it, then makes its next attempt with `make_next_attempt`, a playbook operation of its own that no transport serves (`src/attempt-leases/next-attempts.ts`), as the caller who started the run, read again by its id as the server would take it now (`callersByIdOf` of `@on.auto/identity`, `src/attempt-leases/attempt-holding.ts`; the playbook itself for a trigger), with `attemptDied`, so the core claims the attempt and the dispatcher's check decides whether that caller may still run it. The host lets the lease of the process that is gone go only once it holds the resume's own. It hands the resume's lease over where the next attempt was refused because this process is stopping, an interruption the dispatcher's error boundary passes through (`src/dispatch/error-boundary.ts` of `@on.auto/operations`), so the next sweep, here or on another server, takes the run up again; a next attempt that dies once it has begun hands over the lease it shares with the resume, as any attempt cut short does. Every other answer of the dispatch lets the resume's lease go, a failure among them, since the dispatcher answers a defect as `failed`: so a dispatch that fails before the next attempt takes its lease leaves the run started with no lease. Whether a run is one the server resumes is decided before any attempt, from the kind of its starter's id alone (`readsAgain` of `callersByIdOf`): a key in a server that takes credentials or admits nobody, the local developer in local mode, or the playbook itself. A run whose starter is of no such kind, a signed-in person, whose grant comes with each token and is kept nowhere, or the local developer outside local mode, is never resumed: the host names its attempt to the ender with `noNextAttempt`, and the run ends as a run within its call does, in the ender's words for what it had done. A key no longer listed is given no grant, so the check refuses it; a run whose caller is refused, a key removed or narrowed, is ended with `refuse_next_attempt`, as the playbook itself, `unavailable` with the words of the refusal. A run a workflow's step made is the workflow host's to make again, and one that finishes later its settlement's. The host also asks again a caller's cancel the core recorded alone on a run that may still have been live (`src/attempt-leases/cancels-again.ts`), once that run has been quiet as long as it may take and a second more, a minute on where it could not be asked, for an hour at most; an attempt whose process dies meanwhile is ended by its lease with the kind of that cancel.

## The subscriptions of the playbooks

The server runs the loop of `@on.auto/providers` that decides which subscriptions a playbook holds at the services of its connections, so a service tells the runtime at once what happens there (`src/subscriptions`). `subscriptionLoopPortsOf` of `@on.auto/connections` composes its ports, where its README says what each does and the bound of subscriptions through one connection, and the server gives it its parts (`subscriptionLoopPartsOf`): the connection's calls through the tool access, its tool calls and the requests its entry declares (`requestOnce` of the access `makeToolAccess` made, which `requestWaysOf` of `src/composition/served-tool-access.ts` forwards); its one ledger, both as the record of a playbook's subscriptions and as the registry of the org's connections; the secret store; whether it receives over HTTP, as `receive_events` says; and the reader of the requests that wait, `waitingRequestsOf` of `@on.auto/interaction`, each open request delivered in a conversation through a connection, its arguments rendered within the bound the tool access gives a request to that connection, `mostRequestBytesTo`.

The runtime knows no address its services may deliver to, so the loop arranges no webhook: the server gives the composition no address, so its port answers none for each connection, and a destination is arranged only where a runtime is given one. A runtime that nothing outside can reach is served so whatever its entries declare, and no entry the runtime ships declares a destination arranged for each subscription, which `src/subscriptions/served-subscriptions.test.ts` holds, so a running installation arranges nothing and records nothing new.

A round of the loop, the loop and then the playbook's next due time (`subscriptionRoundOf` of `@on.auto/connections`), calls out of the server, as a delivery does, so it is due work of the workflow host in the lane of the items that call out (`subscriptionRoundsOf`, `src/subscriptions/subscription-rounds.ts`), one item a playbook keyed by the playbook, which the host never hands out while it is in flight: one round of a playbook at a time. A playbook is due when a row of `event_subscriptions_1` says it is, `reconcile_due_at`, which a fact about a subscription sets to its own time and each round sets on every subscription of the playbook to the time it answered, read through the index across playbooks the table keeps; and when a request of it is delivered or answered, or its run ends, which may change where a request waits: the delivery and the answer as the interaction capability's due work records them, through the recorder of work it is given (`recording`), while the runtime arranges a way, and the ending of a run as the follower of the playbooks hands it to a consumer of `run_succeeded`, `run_rejected` and `run_failed`, the facts of `requestPlaceFacts` of `@on.auto/interaction` the follower hands a consumer, so it reads no other record of a playbook for it. Both owe the playbook a round in the process's memory, which the host performs at its next wake, within a sweep; a process that stops in between leaves the request to its timer until the next of these facts, or the playbook's next due time, which a round never sets later than `resyncedAfterMs` of `@on.auto/providers` after it ran, the round taking the host's time it was handed. A round that fails is tried again after the due work's own wait for its key. The consumer wants the facts of every playbook while the runtime arranges a way, and otherwise only of a playbook that holds a subscription, one read of the table a pass.

## The company's sign-in

A runtime whose people sign in through their company's own identity provider sets `OIDC_ISSUER`, `OIDC_CLIENT_ID` and `OIDC_CLIENT_SECRET` or `OIDC_CLIENT_SECRET_FILE`, beside `PUBLIC_URL`, or `OIDC_ISSUER` alone where the provider lets a client register itself (below), and may set `OIDC_SCOPES`, `openid profile email` when left out, `OIDC_TOKEN_ENDPOINT_AUTH_METHOD`, and `OIDC_REQUIRED_CLAIM`; each is the parameter of the standards behind the prefix `OIDC_`, and the last is Kubernetes's `--oidc-required-claim`, "A key=value pair that describes a required claim in the ID Token. If set, the claim is verified to be present in the ID Token with a matching value." `readOpenIdConnectSettings` (`src/settings/openid-connect-settings.ts`) reads them where the other sign-in settings are read, and refuses the start, naming the setting, when a client ID or a secret is set without `OIDC_ISSUER`, when `OIDC_ISSUER` is set with one of the client ID and a secret and not the other, when both forms of the secret are, beside `IDENTITY_ISSUER`, `IDENTITY_JWKS` or `IDENTITY_JWKS_URL`, in local mode, without `PUBLIC_URL`, with scopes that lack `openid`, with an issuer that is not an `https` URL or has a query or a fragment, with a secret file that is missing, unreadable or empty, with `OIDC_REQUIRED_CLAIM` but no `OIDC_ISSUER`, or with an `OIDC_REQUIRED_CLAIM` that is not `claim=value`. That is one pair, split at its first `=`, as Kubernetes splits one flag, so a value may hold a `=`; an environment variable is set once, so there is one claim, where Kubernetes repeats the flag for more. The claim and the value are each taken exactly as written, and the start is refused, not trimmed, where either is empty or begins or ends with a space or a line ending, since Kubernetes's flag trims both and an empty value could not be told from a forgotten one. Without any of the three, nothing is read and nothing changes. The secret is kept `Redacted` and never logged.

With them, the server reads the provider's metadata as it starts (`@on.auto/openid-connect`) and refuses to start on a provider it cannot use. It then takes no token of the Auto identity service and serves no protected resource metadata, since the runtime issues no token of its own yet: an API key or the session of a person who signed in there authenticates a call, and `signIn` names the provider's issuer, so every rule that asks whether people sign in, the access mode among them, holds as with the Auto identity service. Three public routes serve the sign-in (`src/sign-in/company-sign-in.ts`):

- `GET /auth/oidc` sends the person to the provider, keeping the `state`, the `nonce` and the PKCE verifier of the sign-in for 10 minutes in a cookie `__Host-sign-in`, `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `Path=/`, `SameSite=Lax`, so that it comes back with the provider's redirect, a navigation from another site. The paths take the shape of OmniAuth's, `/auth/:provider` and `/auth/:provider/callback`; the start is a `GET`, a navigation, as Passport's and Grafana's are, where OmniAuth's request phase is a `POST`.
- `GET /auth/oidc/callback`, the redirect URI, finishes the sign-in with that cookie, decides the person's role, keeps their record, ends the session whose cookie the request carries, and keeps a new one in the cookie `__Host-session`, `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `Path=/`, `SameSite=Strict`, for at most 30 days, then sends the person to `/` with `303`. The provider's redirect is a navigation from another site, which carries no `SameSite=Strict` cookie unless the provider is on the runtime's own site, so a session the browser held is seldom ended here: a sign-in puts the new cookie in its place, and the old session's row stays until it expires. A callback no sign-in of this browser started, one the provider refused or the library could not complete, or one whose role could not be recorded, answers `400`; the provider's error code, or the failure's, is logged with the issuer, never the query, the code, a token or anything of the person. A person an admin removed is answered `403`, `forbidden`, and gets no new session; a session they began before the removal is not ended by it, and lasts until it expires, 7 days after its last use or 30 days after it began, whichever comes first. A person whose ID Token does not carry the claim `OIDC_REQUIRED_CLAIM` names, as text equal to its value character for character (`valueOfTheRequiredClaim` of `@on.auto/openid-connect`), is answered `403`, `forbidden`, too, in words of its own, and gets no new session: that claim's name is logged at `warn` with the issuer, never its value or anything of the person (`logSignInRefusedForTheClaim`), and the start logs at `info` which claim a person must carry, never the value. Both refusals end the session whose cookie the request carries, which the provider's redirect seldom brings, as above.
- `POST /auth/sign-out`, from a page of the runtime's own origin alone, ends the session at once and removes its cookie.

Where `OIDC_REQUIRED_CLAIM` is set, the claim is checked first, after the checks of the ID Token and before anything is decided or kept (`admittedAfter` of `src/sign-in/admission.ts`): a person refused for it is given no role, no record and no session. The person's role is decided before their session is issued, by one decision of the role decider of `@on.auto/access-control`, `enrol`, in the organization `default`, at every sign-in (`src/sign-in/admission.ts`): the first person to sign in is given `admin`, recorded as `bootstrap`; a person who never held a role here is given `viewer`, recorded as `enrolment`; a person who holds one is given nothing; a person an admin removed is given nothing and answered as above. Each is recorded by the person themselves, `person:` and their id. The decision answers which of the four applied, and nothing else decides the role. Two people who sign in at once to a server with no admin meet in the role stream and exactly one is its admin; the other is decided again and is a viewer. Only then is the person's record kept, their id, the `name` and the `email` of the ID Token and the times, in the sign-in's store, `auth_people`, and only then their session, so no one holds a session whose role is undecided: a decision that cannot be recorded leaves no record and no session, and the person may simply sign in again, since every sign-in after the first decides nothing. As the routes are served, while nobody holds a role that gives roles, the server logs at `info` that the first person to sign in becomes the admin of the organization, and when the first person does, that they are, by their id alone (`logFirstSignInAwaited`, `logFirstAdmin` of `src/logging/sign-in-logging.ts`, which holds every line the sign-in's routes log). The roles recorded decide only who may sign in; they do not yet decide what a person may do: the server holds every person an editor (`servedRoleSource`) and only reports.

The server opens the sign-in's store of `@on.auto/openid-connect` at every start, first of what `serve` of `src/composition/composition-root.ts` composes, in local mode too and whatever is set, so every database gains the store's empty `auth_` tables at its first start on this release (`signInOpened` of `src/composition/ledger-store.ts`, which also gives the sign-in its decision over the ledger, `enrollingIn` of `src/sign-in/admission.ts`); a store a newer release carried forward stops the start with `SignInStoreOfANewerVersion`, which says where the ledger is and what to do, and which `ledgerRefusalIn` of `src/database/ledger-refusals.ts` finds as it finds a ledger of another version (`src/database/sign-in-store-of-a-newer-version.ts`, `src/lifecycle/ledger-startup.test.ts`). The routes keep their sessions and their people in that store: `serve` answers its rows as the `sessionRows` and the `peopleRows` of `Served`, beside the decision, `enrolling`, and `signInRoutesOf` of `src/sign-in/company-sign-in.ts` gives them to the sign-in, so a restart signs nobody out, and every server of one PostgreSQL database knows a session another made and ends it when the person signs out there. A composition that opens no store, as a test's may, has no rows and no decision, and a company sign-in on it is refused as a defect of the composition. A navigation that comes from another site, the provider's redirect and the one that follows it, may carry no `SameSite=Strict` cookie, so whatever decides on a navigation whether a person is signed in asks the API from the page instead.

The API takes that session (`signedInApiOf`, handed to `createApiHandler` as its `sessions`, kept in the same `sessionRows` as the routes'): a request with no `Authorization` header that carries the cookie `__Host-session` and the header `X-CSRF: 1` is served as the person of the session, and without that header the cookie is no credential. The header is static, and a page can send it only to its own origin or to one whose answer to the CORS preflight allows it, which the API gives no page, since it allows only the headers it lists and never credentials; so a page of a sibling subdomain, which is the same site and gets the `SameSite=Strict` cookie, cannot ride the session, and a navigation, which carries no header of a page's choosing, is never signed in (OAuth 2.0 for Browser-Based Applications, RFC 10017, 6.1.3.3; the header is Duende BFF's). The person is the caller `person:` and their id, `personIdOf` of the issuer and the subject the session holds, named by that id too, in the organization `default` alone, with every permission and no calling client, so the decision point takes the call as made in Studio (`via` `studio`), while an API key arrives as a key; it holds every such person an editor (`servedRoleSource`) and only reports, as for every signed-in person. The API takes `PUBLIC_URL` as the runtime's public origin, for the `Origin` check, CORS, the landing page, links to Studio and the API catalog, never the address a request came in on.

## The client the server registers itself

With `OIDC_ISSUER` and no client ID or secret, the server registers its client itself, by OAuth 2.0 Dynamic Client Registration (RFC 7591), where the provider declares a registration endpoint, in its OpenID Connect metadata or else in its RFC 8414 metadata (`registeringProviderOf` of `@on.auto/openid-connect`); there is no setting for it, since registering is what a relying party with no client of its own does where the provider offers it, as mod_auth_openidc does, and a person who wants a client of their own sets its ID and it wins. Whether it may is read from what the provider declares and from nothing else, never from its address or its name. It registers as it starts, after the ledger and the secret store are open, so a start meets a provider that offers no registration, refuses the request, cannot be reached or answers a client the relying party cannot sign in as, and stops, in words that name the setting to change and say to register Auto with the provider by hand where it cannot (`refusedAtStart` of `src/sign-in/registration-refusals.ts`). It sends the name `Auto Runtime at` and the host of `PUBLIC_URL`, such as `Auto Runtime at auto.example.com`, so that whoever runs the provider can tell two servers apart (`clientNameAt` of `src/sign-in/company-client.ts`).

The registration is kept as the runtime keeps its own keys and the clients its connections register: a fact, `sign_in_client_registered { issuer, redirect_uri, client_id, secret_id }`, by the runtime, `by: runtime`, in a stream of the runtime's own and of no org, `runtime/sign-in-client-registrations`, apart from the connections' `runtime/client-registrations`, since a server may hold a connection and a sign-in at one issuer; and the client's secret, its method and the end of its secret, sealed in the secret store under the id the fact names, never in the fact, a log line or an answer (`src/sign-in/client-registrations.ts`). The fact is decided by a Decider, `signInClientsDecider`, whose command names the client it replaces, none for the first: it records the new client only while the latest one for the issuer and the redirect URI is the one named, and refuses otherwise, `conflict` of the kind `concurrent_change`, which the ledger appends under the version of the stream it read. So two servers of one database that register at the same moment keep one client: the later one forgets the secret it sealed and signs people in as the client the earlier one recorded, and the client it registered stays unused at the provider, which it logs. No projection or read model folds the stream: it is folded, a handful of facts, where a sign-in needs it, and a projection folds the streams of an org or a playbook alone, so none meets this one.

The server signs people in as the latest client for its issuer and its redirect URI (`registeredClientsOf` of `src/sign-in/registered-clients.ts`), read at each sign-in, so every server of one database uses the client any of them registered last, and its secret is read from the store once for each client. A restart registers nothing. A changed `PUBLIC_URL` is another redirect URI, which has no client yet, so the server registers one at its next start and people consent once more. A client whose secret the store no longer keeps, or keeps sealed under a key it no longer has, or whose secret has ended (`client_secret_expires_at`), is replaced by a new registration, recorded in its place. A pending sign-in notes the client it began as, its `client_id` in base64url as a fourth part of the cookie `__Host-sign-in`, and finishes as that client, which must be one this server registered at this issuer for this redirect URI, since a code is redeemed by the client it was issued to; a runtime with a client given keeps the three parts it always had. Where the token endpoint refuses the client, by `invalid_client` or a challenge, and it is still the latest, the server registers anew once, however many sign-ins it refused at once, and records the new client in its place; that person is told they are not signed in and the next try signs them in. A sign-in that cannot begin because the server could not register again is answered as a sign-in that failed. The registrations are logged with the issuer and the client's ID alone (`logClientRegistered`, `logClientRegisteredElsewhereFirst`, `logClientNotRegistered` of `src/logging/sign-in-logging.ts`).

## Testing

The servers the tests start in the process run on `patientWorkerPool` of `src/testing/servers/worker-pools.ts`, the server's worker pool whose checks at save may take 30 s, through `checkingWithin` of `@on.auto/definitions/testing`, and whose inputs may take 30 s, through `decidingWithin` of `@on.auto/workflow-engine/testing`, so a check or a decision that a busy machine slows is never refused by the 2 s a save allows or the 2,000 ms of an input; `scriptedWorkerPool` answers the outcomes a test scripts before it hands the rest to that pool. The refusal of a check at its deadline stays held by `src/computation/checks-over-http.test.ts` and `src/computation/checks-over-mcp.test.ts`, whose pools answer `stopped: deadline`, and the answer of a start whose input runs past its deadline by `src/workflows/workflow-lifecycle.test.ts`, whose evaluations answer `exhausted: deadline`. The servers the tests spawn as processes keep both bounds.

A test that waits for what a workflow's step waits for before the step's timeout ends it runs its server on `movedClock` of `@on.auto/workflow-host/testing` and moves the clock past the timeout once it has seen the state (`src/interaction/interaction-over-http.test.ts`, `src/workflow-calls/workflow-cancels.test.ts`, `src/workflow-runs/workflow-tools.test.ts`), and the table of the endings of a run that asks a system gives its calls `patientTiming` of `@on.auto/tool-servers/testing` and only the rows that outlast a call or an opening the short one (`src/asking-a-system/ending-rows.ts`).

A test that stages or reads what a server keeps in its ledger's file goes through the ledger's own library, never through a second SQLite library on the same file, which the [workflow host README](../workflow-host/README.md#where-a-run-is-kept) warns lost committed writes: `readOfTheLedgerIn(file, statement)` of `src/testing/records/ledger-file.ts` reads through `sqliteLedgerReads` of `@on.auto/ledger/sqlite`, which opens the file for reads alone, without the ledger's migration and so without its write lock, so a test that polls a file a running server writes, in the test's process or in one of its own, never waits on it; `onTheLedgerIn(file, use)` opens the ledger on the file as the server does, hands `use` its connection and closes it after, for the tests that write while the server is stopped or beside a ledger the test opened; `everyRowSet` and `tableDropped` write a projection's table by its name, made with `statementOf` of `@on.auto/ledger`, and `tablesNamedIn` lists the tables a pattern names. A test that needs the ledger to refuse a command wraps the server's `Ledger` instead, through `ledgerOver` of `src/testing/servers/served-parts.ts` (`src/asking-a-system/endings.test.ts`).

The composition of main, started in the process with a shutdown timeout longer than the test, stops with an idle keep-alive connection open after a workflow ran, and while a function a workflow called still runs, without waiting for that timeout (`src/lifecycle/shutdown.test.ts`); so no test of a spawned process holds it to how long it takes: `src/main.test.ts` holds main to its exit code alone, `src/workflows/workflow-lifecycle.test.ts` to its exit code, to what it says at its start, to leaving nothing running and to taking the call it was stopped in up again once started again.

The tests start the servers, the `auto-runtime` command and the development runner they run through `spawnForTheTest` of `@on.auto/operations/testing` (`src/testing/processes/spawned-server.ts`, `runtime-command.ts` and `development-process.ts`), which ends each once its test finished, whether it passed, failed or timed out: with SIGTERM, on which the server stops, and with SIGKILL if it is still there `stoppedWithinMs`, 10 s, later; `src/lifecycle/shutdown.test.ts` holds the server's longest stop, `longestStopMs`, under that bound. Two tests start their processes through the code they test instead: `src/development/children.test.ts` through the runner's `startChild`, which starts each child in a process group of its own, and ends each with SIGKILL once its test finished, and `src/background-start/started-in-the-background.test.ts` through `startInBackgroundOf`, whose recording entry ends itself once it has written what the test reads, and whose other entry, `src/testing/entries/stay-until-signalled.ts`, runs until the test signals it through the handle the start hands back, with `endWithTheTest` registered for its pid. The processes the helper starts are attached to the test's worker, in its process group, so a signal to that group, as Ctrl+C in a terminal sends, stops them too; a worker that dies alone runs no hook, and leaves them running until they next write to the output it held. A runtime started in the background, `start --background --instance <id>`, runs in a process group of its own and writes to its log, so nothing of the test's ends it with the test. `src/agent-relay/agent-relay.test.ts` starts one alone, in the test that proves it runs on outside the process group of the agent that started it, and starts a second one the same way once the first was stopped; another has the relay start one as it starts, and a second once the test stopped the first and the relay's grace passed; two of its other tests start the runtime in a terminal, attached, and another starts none, since another program holds its port. `src/agent-relay/relay-replacement.test.ts` starts one in place of a runtime in a terminal that the test stops, and one through the relay of `src/testing/entries/relay-with-short-bounds.ts`, which the test freezes with SIGSTOP and the relay stops and replaces; its third test freezes a runtime in a terminal, which the relay leaves alone, and lets it go on with SIGCONT before it stops it with Ctrl+C. Each test that lets a relay start a runtime registers, before anything starts, `runtimesStartedInTheBackgroundEndedWithTheTest` of `src/testing/processes/background-runtimes.ts`. Once the test finished, it reads the lines the relay writes to the `runtime.log` of the test's home as it acts, `Auto Runtime was started in the background as process <pid>, instance <id>` and `Auto Runtime, process <pid>, ended`, and ends each process started and not ended: with SIGTERM, and with SIGKILL if it is still there `stoppedWithinMs` later, each sent only while its command line, as `ps` reads it, still carries that instance, and the process counts as gone once it no longer does. It asks the port nothing, so a runtime that answers slower than a probe's 2 s, or one whose relay was ended while it held the starting lock, is ended all the same, and a pid used again by another process is not signalled. The suite's teardown does the same with what a test left in the temporary folder of the run, as when its worker died or its hook timed out: `vitest.config.ts` names `src/testing/test-run/test-run-folder.ts` in place of the shared global setup, `aFolderOfTheTestRun` of `@on.auto/operations/test-run-folder` over `runtimesStillRunningInTheBackgroundIn` of `src/testing/processes/runtimes-in-the-background.ts`, which reads the `runtime.log` of each home left there, so a runtime still running in one is ended once the run ends and named, with its home, in the failure of the run.

The relay of `auto-runtime mcp` holds its bounds on Effect's test clock, in `src/agent-relay/relay-watch.test.ts`, `src/agent-relay/relay-losses.test.ts` and `src/runtime-recovery/runtime-stop.test.ts`; `src/agent-relay/relay-replacement.test.ts` runs a relay on real time with a silence of 1 s and a stop of 0.5 s in place of 15 s and 9.1 s, and holds what happens, never how long it took. The waits of its start, for a runtime it starts to answer, for an older one to stop and for one that restarts to come back, probe the port every 100 ms on Effect's clock and end with the relay: a start the relay makes while it relays ends at once when the agent leaves, aborts the probe in flight and frees the starting lock. `src/background-start/background-start.test.ts` holds them on the test clock, by the probes its fake answers and by the time they take, and `src/local-runtime/runtime-commands.test.ts` holds the waits of `stop` and `restart`, which run the same way. Some tests keep a bound on real time on purpose. `src/lifecycle/shutdown.test.ts` holds a request still running to being cut off no sooner than the shutdown timeout less 50 ms, a lower bound a slow machine only lengthens. `src/computation/computation-over-http.test.ts` holds a run that never ends by itself to being refused no sooner than its 10 s deadline less the slack of a timer, and a read of the definition and a run on another worker to answering before that deadline, a race the server wins by the pool's 10 s on the real clock, which the pool keeps and no clock of the server reaches; its saves check within 30 s through `checkingWithin`. `src/reasoning/prompts-and-answers-on-stores.test.ts` holds a reasoning run whose 25 calls each answer 1 MB to reading every answer back within `toolBounds.runMs`, the 600 s a run that calls tools may take, on the real clock on purpose, since that is the bound such a run meets in the server. `src/telemetry/telemetry-shutdown.test.ts` holds the shutdown of telemetry whose collector cannot be reached, and of telemetry whose collector takes its exports and never answers them, to ending before a timer of the same second as its bound, `longestShutdownMs`, started after it, since that shutdown races its exports against a timer of 1 s of the platform, for which the exporters take no clock; the two timers fire in the order they were started, however slow the machine. `src/development/source-changes.test.ts` saves a file again every 500 ms until the watcher reports it, and `src/workflows/workflow-lifecycle.test.ts` waits until the timers of a stopped server's runs are due before it starts the next process, waits a slow machine only lengthens.

Three subpaths serve the repository's own scripts and no program that runs the server: `./openapi-document` answers the OpenAPI document a throwaway server of this release serves, which `docs:openapi` keeps; `./served-to-an-agent` (`src/testing/agent-texts/served-to-an-agent.ts`) what a throwaway server serves an agent over MCP, read by `servedSurfaceAt` of `@on.auto/api/testing` as the caller of local mode, after `list_playbooks` on its new org, and the page it answers at `/debug`; and `./setting-names` (`src/testing/agent-texts/setting-names.ts`) the name of each setting the server declares, each variable it reads and each key of its configuration file. `agent-texts` lists every text of what is served, the behavioural check shows a model what a client shows of it, and `docs:setting-names:check` finds no setting's name in any of those texts beyond the ones its list still names. All three live under `src/testing`, which the npm package leaves out of a package that offers no `./testing`, so none ships. `src/testing/agent-texts/served-to-an-agent.test.ts` holds what it reads to its page at `/debug`, text for text and name for name.
