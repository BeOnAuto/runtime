# @on.auto/connections

The connections of an org: tool servers a person adds to the org through their agent, a provider by its name (`@on.auto/providers`) or a server given by hand, which the org owns, which every playbook of the org may use or the playbooks its entry names, and which are kept as facts, added and removed rather than run. A connection's entry is the non-secret part of a tool server entry; its credential, when it has one, is a row of the ledger's secret store, referred to by id and never held in a fact. The agent never carries a secret: `add_connection` answers a one-time link to Studio's page, where the person who added the connection approves it and gives its credential. This package keeps connections and manages them, and serves them to `@on.auto/tool-servers` as the tool servers of their org, through which every function and every operation over tool servers calls (see [The calls](#the-calls)). The operations are defined on the application layer, [`@on.auto/operations`](../operations), and `docs/self-host/connections.md` is the operator's guide.

## A connection

| Field                  | What it holds                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `id`                   | A UUIDv7, `randomUUIDv7` of `@on.auto/operations`, chosen when it is added                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `name`                 | The name a function gives it, checked by `serverNameProblems` of `@on.auto/tool-servers` as a tool server's name is; unique among the connections of the org that are not removed, and never the name of an entry of `TOOL_SERVERS`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `type`                 | `mcp` or `openapi`, `ConnectionTypeSchema`, the two types of the `type` of `ServerEntrySchema` of `@on.auto/tool-servers` that a connection may be                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| `provider`             | The name of the provider it was added from, or `null` for a server given by hand                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `placeholders`         | The values the caller gave for the provider's placeholders, by name, or `null` for a server given by hand                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `entry`                | `url`, `document`, `base_url`, `headers`, `query_params`, `allowed`, `playbooks`, `record_content` and `request_id`, the fields of `ServerEntrySchema` of `@on.auto/tool-servers` that hold no credential, with descriptions of their own                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `credential`           | One flat object, `CredentialSchema`: `kind`, `none`, `reference`, `secret` or `client_credentials`, and the fields its kind takes: `name` and `in`, `header`, `query` or `security`, where its value is sent, and for `in: header` an optional `scheme`, which the runtime writes before the value, for `secret`, and with them `variable`, the variable of the runtime that holds the value, for `reference`; `issuer`, an optional `client_id`, which the person gives on the page with the secret where it is left out, and an optional `scope` for `client_credentials`; none for `none` |
| `status`               | `pending`, `connected`, `broken` or `removed`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `added_by`             | The id of the caller who added it, such as `person:<subject>`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `added_at`             | When it was added                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `connected_at`         | When it was connected, or `null`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `connected_by`         | The id of the caller who connected it, approving it or giving its credential, from the context of `connection_connected`, or `null`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `credential_ends_with` | The ending the secret store keeps of its secret, as the ending of a credential below says, or `null`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `broken`               | The sentence of why it is broken while it is, else `null`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

`ConnectionSchema` is the schema of the record. Times are ISO 8601 UTC strings read from Effect's `Clock`.

The fields `in`, `name` and `scheme` of a placement, `issuer` and `scope` are `wayFields` of `@on.auto/providers`, the one place a provider's way and a connection's credential take them from; the credential adds what a way never has, `variable` and `client_id`. The credential is one shape wherever it appears: what `add_connection` takes, what every operation answers in the record, and what `connection_added` keeps, with the client id the person gives on the page set on it once the connection is connected. Its fields are optional in the schema, each described with the kind it belongs to, and a check on the schema holds each kind to exactly its fields, so a credential that lacks one or gives another is `invalid_input` at the field, in words that name it, as `Expected name, which a reference credential takes` or `Expected no in: a none credential takes no other field`. The same check refuses a `reference` whose `variable` is no variable name (`variableNamePattern` of `@on.auto/config`), a `secret` or a `reference` sent as a header whose `name` is no header name (`headerNamePattern` of `@on.auto/tool-servers`), and an `issuer` that is neither https nor on a loopback address (`isPinnableUrl` of `@on.auto/tool-servers`). A `scheme` is refused in words of its own: of a kind that places no value, `Expected no scheme: a none credential places no value in a header for the runtime to write a scheme before`; beside an `in` other than `header`, `Expected no scheme, which goes before a value sent in a header: this credential is sent in a query parameter`; holding `$`, which the reading of the placed entry takes for a reference or an escape, `Expected a scheme without $, which the reading of the entry takes for a reference or an escape`; and holding a character that is no HTTP token's, the characters of a header name (`headerNamePattern`), `Expected a scheme of the characters a header name holds, such as Bearer`.

## The facts and the fold

The connections of an org live in one stream, `connections` relative to the org, `org/<org>/connections` (`connectionsStreamOfOrg`). Every fact carries `by` and `at` in its metadata, the `ActedContext` of `@on.auto/operations`, and none holds a secret, a token or a value derived from either but the link's digest:

| Fact                     | Data                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `connection_added`       | `connection`, `name`, `server_type`, the record's `type`, named so since the fact's own `type` names the fact, `provider`, `placeholders`, `entry` and `credential`, as resolved                                                |
| `connection_link_issued` | `connection`, `token_sha256`, the SHA-256 of the link's token in hex, and `expires_at`, ten minutes on; appended with `connection_added`, in one decision                                                                       |
| `connection_approved`    | `connection`                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `connection_connected`   | `connection`, and for a credential of the kind `secret` or `client_credentials` `secret_id`, the id the secret store keeps the value under, `algorithm` for a private key, and `client_id` where the person gave it on the page |
| `connection_broken`      | `connection` and `reason`, the sentence of why                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `connection_removed`     | `connection`                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

There is no read model: each call folds the stream (`connectionsFold`, the fold of `registryDecider`). The fold keeps, by id, the record, the open link, the id of its secret, which it keeps after a removal so the store can forget it, and the algorithm of a private key. It sets the `client_id` of `connection_connected` on the record's credential, so a connection connected holds one shape of credential, its client id given with the credential or on the page, and a pending one whose credential leaves its client id out is answered without it. It answers the connections newest first. `connection_connected` closes the link, and so does `connection_removed`. A pure decider holds the rules, and the handlers pass it who and when in the command:

- `add` refuses a name a connection of the org holds that is not removed, `conflict` of the kind `taken`, and a connection past the 50 an org may hold that are not removed (`mostConnectionsOfAnOrg`), `conflict` of the kind `connections_full`, `The org holds 50 connections, the most it may; remove one before adding another`, which bounds the links the tool access keeps for the org, and records the connection and its link.
- `approve` and `give` look the connection up, `not_found`, then the link: open while the connection is pending, its digest the token's and `expires_at` later than now, else `not_found`, `There is no open link of this token: it is unknown, used up or expired`. Where people sign in, the caller must be `added_by`, else `forbidden`; `makeConnectionOperations` hands the two whether a link binds a person, which it does where `access` is `people`. `approve` connects a connection of the credential `none` or `reference`, recording `connection_approved` and `connection_connected`, and refuses one that takes a value with `conflict`; `give` records the two with the secret's id, and refuses a connection that takes none with `conflict`.
- `remove` records `connection_removed`, and nothing for a connection already removed.
- `break` records `connection_broken` with its `reason` for a connection that is connected, and nothing for one that is not, so a credential two calls find unreadable at once breaks its connection once. Only the source of [The calls](#the-calls) issues it, as the runtime, `by: runtime`. Every failure to record a `break`, a change that came first among them, is logged, `logError` of Effect with the cause, and the connection is refused broken all the same; the decider's `not_found` cannot arise, since the source reads the connection from the stream it records the break on and the fold never drops one. The source keeps nothing and the tool access keeps no broken answer, so the next use reads the credential again and records the break again.

## The operations

`makeConnectionOperations(services)` makes the eight operations, all org operations, so their routes are relative to the org, as one list for a catalog. `add_connection`, `list_connections` and `remove_connection` are also MCP tools, and `list_connections` shows an agent each connection's `status`; `get_connection`, which reads one connection by its id, `list_providers`, which a page reads, and the three of Studio's page are defined with `defineHttpOperation`, and so served over HTTP alone. The services are what the server composes: `secrets`, the `keep`, `read`, `endingsOf` and `forget` of the ledger's `SecretStore`; `policy`; `fileEntries`, the names of the entries of `TOOL_SERVERS`; `modelProviders`, the names of the model providers and gateways; `providers`, the `ProviderList` the server read at start, and `providerValues`, the values the environment sets for the variables its references name, which are variables the runtime reads for itself and `connections.variables` may not name; `pages`, Studio's origin and the runtime's, read when a link is made; and `access`, who the runtime admits: `people` where it signs people in, `keys` where it takes API keys alone, and `local` in local mode.

| Operation           | Kind    | Route                                | Permission  | Answer                            | Rejections of the handler                             |
| ------------------- | ------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| `add_connection`    | command | `POST /connections`                  | `org:write` | the record and `connect`, `201`   | `invalid_input`, `forbidden`, `conflict`              |
| `list_connections`  | query   | `GET /connections`                   | `org:read`  | `{ connections }`, newest first   | none                                                  |
| `get_connection`    | query   | `GET /connections/{id}`              | `org:read`  | the record                        | `not_found`                                           |
| `remove_connection` | command | `DELETE /connections/{id}`           | `org:write` | the record, removed               | `not_found`, `conflict`                               |
| `read_link`         | query   | `GET /connections/{id}/link/{token}` | any         | the record and `link`             | `not_found`                                           |
| `approve`           | command | `POST /connections/{id}/approve`     | any         | the record                        | `not_found`, `forbidden`, `conflict`                  |
| `give_credential`   | command | `POST /connections/{id}/credential`  | any         | the record                        | `not_found`, `forbidden`, `conflict`, `invalid_input` |
| `list_providers`    | query   | `GET /providers`                     | `org:read`  | `{ providers, may_add, refusal }` | none                                                  |

- `add_connection` takes one flat input, two ways in (`AdditionSchema` of `src/operations/connection-fields.ts`, whose check holds each way to its fields as the credential's check holds each kind to its own). With `provider`, the name of a provider, it takes `placeholders`, `name`, the provider's when left out, `credential.kind`, the provider's first way when left out, and of `entry` only `playbooks`, `allowed` and `record_content`, and refuses `type`, the server part of `entry` and every field of `credential` but `kind`, each at its field, `Expected no url: the provider gives the server`. Without, it takes `name`, `type`, `entry` and `credential` as a server given by hand, each required, `Expected type, which a server given by hand takes, or provider`, and refuses `placeholders`. A provider is resolved first (`src/providers/provider-additions.ts`): `providerNamed` of `@on.auto/providers` refuses a name the runtime has no provider of at `/provider`, to which this package adds where the names are, `There is no provider named crm here; search lists the providers a person may connect`, and `additionOfProvider` turns the provider and what the caller gave into the name, the type, the entry and the credential a server given by hand would have been, refusing a placeholder or a kind at the pointer the caller wrote. Everything after is the path of a server given by hand, over the resolved addition: the credential's check, the entry's checks, the reading, the policy and the names, none skipped and none run twice; a problem they find in a field the provider resolved is reported at `/provider`, a name's problem at `/name` where the caller gave the name and at `/provider` where it is the provider's, and a problem of `playbooks`, `allowed` or `record_content` at its field. A provider's document beside its entries is read from the folder of the providers, as a file's document is read from the configuration's folder, where a document given by hand must be a URL. A provider's header or query value may hold a reference, `${NAME}`, for a value of the operator's, such as a developer token: the record keeps it as the provider wrote it, the entry's checks take it since the provider and not the caller wrote it, while an entry given by hand still refuses every reference, and the reading substitutes it from `providerValues`, so a provider whose value is not set is refused at `/provider` in the reading's words, `Refers to the environment variable METRICS_DEVELOPER_TOKEN, which is not set`. The record and `connection_added` carry `provider` and `placeholders`, and the resolved entry, so a later change to a provider's entry changes no connection there is.
- `add_connection` first refuses a caller who cannot add a connection, `forbidden` (`adderRefusalOf`): where the runtime signs people in, a caller that is not a signed-in person (`isSignedInPerson` of `@on.auto/identity`), since a key's connection would wait for a person who never comes, and, where it takes keys alone, every caller, with the sentence that this runtime signs nobody in, so no person can approve a connection. In local mode the local developer adds any. It then checks the entry, every problem at its pointer in one `invalid_input` (`additionIssuesOf` in `src/entries/entry-checks.ts`): the name with `serverNameProblems`; an `mcp` connection's `url` that is neither https nor on a loopback address (`isPinnableUrl` of `@on.auto/tool-servers`), and a `document` that is not a URL, which would be a file on the runtime's disk; the entry with `credentialProblems` of `@on.auto/config`, so a value that looks like a credential is refused, and with `substituted` of the same package against an empty environment, so any `${…}` is refused as a reference; and the credential: a header the runtime sets itself (`headersTheRuntimeSets` of `@on.auto/tool-servers`, which the headers of an entry are checked against too) or the entry sends already, a query parameter or a security scheme for an `mcp` connection, and a query parameter the entry sends already. Where all of these pass (`entryIssuesOf`), it reads the entry with its credential placed, a value standing in for the one the person gives (`readAddition`), as the source reads it to serve it with the real value (see [The calls](#the-calls)), so what the file's reading refuses is refused here, at its pointer under `/entry`, and no connection reaches the ledger that its use would refuse for its entry. Client credentials that leave their `client_id` to the page are read with a client id standing in for it too, since the reading builds the `auth` block, which takes one; every other check runs on what was given. What the reading refuses in a client id, one that looks like a credential, is refused at `/credential/client_id`, the field the agent wrote, in words of its own, `Looks like a credential, which a client id never is: leave the secret out, and the person gives it on the page the link opens`, which replace the reading's (`heldCredentialAsClientId`, keyed on the reading's `/entry/auth/client_id`). A secret may go in `Authorization`, which the runtime sets itself only for client credentials and an entry never sends, since its name names a credential. It then applies the policy, `forbidden` with the sentence of the rule, refuses the name of an entry of the file, `conflict` of the kind `taken`, and records the connection with a link: a token of 32 random bytes in base64url, of which the fact keeps the SHA-256 alone. `connect.url` is `<studio>/connect?server=<runtime>&org=<org>&connection=<id>&token=<token>` (`connectPageOf`). Its definition gives `linkToOpen`, the page and a message saying what to do there, which the MCP tool asks a client to open (`@on.auto/api`).
- `list_providers` (`src/providers`) answers the providers the policy could allow here, sorted by name, each with `name`, `title`, `description`, `website`, `type`, its ways, `credentials`, as `{ kind, in?, name?, scheme?, issuer?, scope?, description?, offered, because? }`, and the placeholders a person gives, `placeholders`, as `{ name, description, pattern?, example?, required, choices? }`. A way is listed where `credential_kinds` takes its kind and the hosts it reaches, `hostAlternativesOf` of `@on.auto/providers`, could pass `allowed_hosts`: a fixed host a pattern takes; a host with a placeholder where every host is allowed, where an allowed host has its shape, the placeholder standing for letters, digits and hyphens, or where a pattern `*.` and a host ends the part of the host after its last placeholder; none where `allowed_hosts` is empty; and, of a placeholder whose choices set the host, the hosts of any one choice. A way is `offered: false` while a variable its provider's references name is not set, with `because` saying whoever runs the runtime sets it up first. A provider with no way listed is left out. `may_add` says whether this caller may add a connection here, and `refusal`, where it may not, the first sentence that refuses, in this order: the sentence a caller without `org:write` is refused with (`permissionRefusalOf` of `@on.auto/operations`), which the dispatcher checks before any handler runs, then the adder check `add_connection` begins with, then `people_may_add`; it is `null` otherwise. Nothing of an entry is secret, so the list is answered whole.
- `makeProviderOffering(services)` (`src/providers/provider-offering.ts`) is the `ProviderOffering` of `@on.auto/tool-servers`, which the server gives the org's `search`: to a caller for whom `may_add` would be `true`, by the same judgement, `refusalToAdd`, it answers the providers `list_providers` lists with at least one way `offered`, each by `name`, `title` and `description`, reading `offered` from the listing and judging nothing again, so a provider none of whose ways is offered is left out of `search` while the page lists it with its ways marked, and to any other caller nothing, so `search` lists the providers a person may connect only to a caller who may add one.
- `read_link` matches the token as `approve` does, never binding a person, and answers the record and `link`, `{ expires_at }`, the time `connection_link_issued` holds and the match checks, so the page can show the connection and how long the person has. `approve` and `give_credential` answer the record alone, since they use the link up.
- `give_credential` checks the link and the fields the credential's kind takes, `value` alone for a `secret`, `client_secret` alone or `private_key` with `algorithm` for `client_credentials`, else `invalid_input`. Client credentials take `client_id` beside them where the credential holds none, and refuse it where it holds one, each at `/client_id`: `Expected client_id: the connection's credential holds no client id, so it is given here with the secret or the key` and `Expected no client_id: the connection's credential holds its client id already`. A client id given here is read as `add_connection` reads the entry, with the value standing in for the secret (`clientIdReadingIssuesOf`), and one that looks like a credential is refused at `/client_id` before anything is kept, so no connection is connected with a client id its use would refuse: `Looks like a credential, which a client id never is: the connection's record keeps the client id in the open, so the secret or the key goes in its own field, where it is sealed`. A `secret` whose credential names a `scheme` refuses a value that starts with the scheme and a space, in any case, at `/value`, `Paste the token alone, without Bearer before it: the runtime writes Bearer itself`. It keeps exactly the value in the secret store, a value the store refuses being `invalid_input` at its pointer with the store's sentence, and records the credential's id, and the client id given on the page. A decision that fails after the value was kept, as when the connection was removed meanwhile, forgets it.
- `remove_connection` forgets the connection's secret once its removal is appended, and forgetting twice is forgetting once.
- The three operations of the page are permitted by every permission, since the token binds them and, where people sign in, the person: their caller must still belong to the org of the route.

## The ending of a credential

`give_credential` hands the secret store, with a secret or a client secret of at least twelve characters, its last four, `keep(value, endsWith)`, and no ending with a shorter value or a private key. The store keeps the ending in the value's row and forgets it with the value, so `remove_connection` forgets both; no fact holds it and the fold records nothing of it. Every operation answers the connection with `credential_ends_with` beside `connected_at`, never inside `credential`, which is what `add_connection` took and the client id given on the page: `list_connections` and `get_connection` read it with `endingsOf` for each connection that has a secret, `give_credential` answers the ending it kept, and the others, whose connection holds none, answer `null`.

## The policy

`readConnectionPolicy(environment, ownVariables)` reads `CONNECTIONS`, a JSON object, `connections` of the configuration file through `ConnectionPolicySchema`: `people_may_add`, `true` when left out; `allowed_hosts`, each a host or `*.` and a host, every host when left out; `credential_kinds`, every kind when left out; and `variables`, the variable names a `reference` may give, none when left out. A name in `variables` that is one of `ownVariables`, the variables the server reads for itself, is a problem at its pointer, so the server stops at start rather than let a connection send one of them. A pattern `*.example.com` takes every host under `example.com` and not `example.com` itself, and the hosts checked, at add and at use, are those of an `mcp` connection's `url`, of an `openapi` connection's `document` and `base_url`, or, where its provider's document lies beside the entries and the provider gives no `base_url`, the server that document names, `documentServerOf` of `@on.auto/providers`, and of a `client_credentials` credential's `issuer`, which the client secret or the key's assertion reaches. A malformed value is `ConnectionPolicyInvalid`, naming each problem at its pointer.

## The calls

`makeConnectionSource({ registry, secrets, policy, variables, modelProviders })` (`src/serving/connection-source.ts`) is the `ConnectionSource` of `@on.auto/tool-servers`, which the server gives its tool access, so a connected connection is a tool server of its org for every function and every operation over tool servers, through the same code as an entry of `TOOL_SERVERS`. `registry` is the ledger, read and written by the full stream name, `secrets` the `read` of the secret store, `policy` the policy read at start, `variables` the values of the variables `policy.variables` names that the runtime's environment sets, read at start, and `modelProviders` the names of the model providers and gateways a connection's entry is checked against. `takesConnections` is `policy.peopleMayAdd`.

`connectionsOf(org)` folds the org's stream, from its first fact on every reading, as the playbook registry folds the org's playbooks for every status it answers, and keeps nothing between readings, so the tool access reads it once for each operation over tool servers; it answers each connection that is not removed, newest first, with its id, how it serves, its name, its `type`, its `provider` as its record holds it, so `search` names the provider of each server it lists, `orgs` the org, and the `playbooks`, `allowed`, `record_content` and `request_id` of its entry, each as `servedFieldsOf` of `@on.auto/tool-servers` gives it, the default where the entry leaves it out, as for a file's entry, and its state: a pending one unready, `connection_pending`, `Nobody has approved the connection crm yet; the person who added it opens its link, or removes it and adds it again for a new link`; a broken one unready, `connection_broken`, with its `broken`; and a connected one with `server`, which the tool access runs once for each connection a process holds, as it makes its link:

1. The policy of the moment is checked with the rules `add_connection` applies (`useRefusalOf`), and a connection a rule no longer takes is `connection_not_allowed` with the rule's sentence, its credential unread: `credential_kinds`, `variables` for a `reference`, and `allowed_hosts` over the entry's `url`, `document` and `base_url` and the credential's `issuer`. With `people_may_add` `false` the runtime uses no connection, in words that speak of using rather than adding, `This runtime uses no connection: its operator sets up its tool servers itself`, while `list_connections` still answers each as it is.
2. The connection is checked with `entryIssuesOf`, the checks of `add_connection` that need no value, against the model providers of the moment, so an entry the runtime no longer takes, as one named as a model provider set up since, is `connection_not_allowed`, `The entry of the connection <name> is not one this runtime takes any more: <the words of each problem>`, its credential unread.
3. The credential is read (`src/serving/given-credentials.ts`): none for `none`; for a `reference`, the value of its `variable`, and a variable the environment leaves unset is `connection_not_allowed`, saying whoever runs the runtime sets it and starts it again; for a `secret` or `client_credentials`, the store's row of its secret's id. A row the store cannot read, `SecretKeyUnknown`, or one that is gone breaks the connection: the source issues `break`, records `connection_broken` with the sentence of why, `The credential of the connection crm cannot be read, so the person who added it removes it and adds it again: <the store's words>` or `… is no longer in the secret store, so …`, and answers it `connection_broken` in it. Nothing un-breaks a connection; the person removes it and adds it again.
4. The credential is placed as the file places a reference (`src/serving/placed-credentials.ts`): the entry, every `$` in it written `$$` so that it is sent as it is written, with a `secret` or a `reference` as `${…}` in the header, the query parameter or the security scheme its `in` and `name` say, after its `scheme` and a space where it names one, as in `Bearer ${…}`, or `client_credentials` as the `auth` block with its issuer, client id and scope, and the client secret, or the private key, as `${…}` beside the algorithm, the issuer, the client id, the scope and the algorithm each written with `$$` for `$` as the entry is; a `reference` names its own variable, and a value given on the page stands under a variable of its own, in an environment of that one entry. In a connection made from a provider, the header and query values are read as the provider wrote them, its references substituted from `providerValues` and scrubbed as every reference's value is, and the rest of the entry as it is written. In one of client credentials, each `{client_id}` the entry holds stays as the provider wrote it, and the client id is handed to `readEntry` as its context's `placedValues`, which places it in the header and query values once the entry as written is judged, as its text, so the client id the person gave on the page is sent where the provider says and, kept in the open in the record, is scrubbed from nothing; the reading at add places the stand-in client id. `readEntry` of `@on.auto/tool-servers`, the reading of an entry of the file, then resolves it and makes it `ServerSettings`, its type the connection's and its org the org, with `substituted`, `credentialProblems`, `unreferencedSecurity`, `misplacedReferences` and `secretsOfEntry` as they are for the file: every value the reference resolves to is a secret, the whole value of a security scheme too, and a value in a header that is a scheme and a credential, as `Bearer <token>`, a secret whole and its credential alone, so the scrubber catches it in every form `@on.auto/tool-servers` lists. This is `readAddition`, the reading `add_connection` makes with a value standing in for the person's, made once here with the real value; what it refuses is `connection_not_allowed` in the words of step 2, so a check that a value can fail refuses in its own words, as an empty value of a reference does.

## Source

`src/index.ts` is the only entry point. `src/registry` holds the record, its entry and credential, the facts and commands of the stream, the fold, the decider and its rules, and the lookups; `src/links` the token and Studio's page; `src/policy` the policy and its rules; `src/entries` the checks of an entry and a credential; `src/serving` the source of the connections as tool servers, the reading and the placement of their credentials and their words; `src/providers` the resolution of an addition by a provider and where its problems are reported, `list_providers` with the listing the policy allows, and the offering of those providers to a caller, which `list_providers` and the org's `search` share; `src/operations` the four operations of the connections, all but `get_connection` tools as well, with the fields, words and services the seven share, how they load and record the stream, and `makeConnectionOperations`; `src/page` the three operations of Studio's page and the fields and checks of what the page gives; `src/testing` what the tests share, and `src/testing/index.ts`, the entry `@on.auto/connections/testing`, the fold and the name of the stream, with which a test of the server reads the id of a connection's secret from the ledger. `operations`, `page` and `serving` depend on the others, and `registry` on none of them.
