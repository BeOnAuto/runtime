# @on.auto/connections

The connections of an org: tool servers a person adds to the org through their agent, a provider by its name (`@on.auto/providers`) or a server given by hand, which the org owns, which every playbook of the org may use or the playbooks its entry names, and which are kept as facts, added and removed rather than run. A connection's entry is the non-secret part of a tool server entry; its credential, when it has one, is a row of the ledger's secret store, referred to by id and never held in a fact. The agent never carries a secret: `add_connection` answers a one-time link to Studio's page, where a person who may approve it, an admin of the org where people sign in, approves it and gives its credential. This package keeps connections and manages them, and serves them to `@on.auto/tool-servers` as the tool servers of their org, through which every function and every operation over tool servers calls (see [The calls](#the-calls)). The operations are defined on the application layer, [`@on.auto/operations`](../operations), and `docs/self-host/connections.md` is the guide for whoever runs the server.

`ConnectionSchema` is the schema of the record. Times are ISO 8601 UTC strings read from Effect's `Clock`.

The fields `in`, `name` and `scheme` of a placement, `issuer` and `scope`, and the fields of consent, `parameters`, the three endpoints and `clients`, are `wayFields` of `@on.auto/providers`, the one place a provider's way and a connection's credential take them from; the credential adds what a way never has, `variable`, `client_id`, a client id as `ClientIdSchema` of `@on.auto/tool-servers` holds one, and `client_source`, which is `ClientSourceSchema` of the same package, `auto` among its sources, the client of Auto's consent service. An MCP server given by hand that takes consent holds `issuer`, `clients` and `scope` as the server names them (see below), and takes `client_id` and `client_source` once connected, as a provider's way does; its `clients` is required, each one a client the runtime makes for itself, `metadata_document` or `dynamic`, which a check on the data of `connection_added` holds (`src/registry/connection-events.ts`), so a missing list never falls back to `registered` and Auto's client. The credential is one shape wherever it appears: what `add_connection` takes, what every operation answers in the record, and what `connection_added` keeps, with the client id the person gives on the page set on it once the connection is connected. Its fields are optional in the schema, each described with the kind it belongs to, and a check on the schema holds each kind to exactly its fields, so a credential that lacks one or gives another is `invalid_input` at the field, in words that name it, as `Expected name, which a reference credential takes` or `Expected no in: a none credential takes no other field`. The same check refuses a `reference` whose `variable` is no variable name (`variableNamePattern` of `@on.auto/config`), a `secret` or a `reference` sent as a header whose `name` is no header name (`headerNamePattern` of `@on.auto/tool-servers`), an `issuer`, or an endpoint of an `authorization` credential, the addresses `wayAddresses` of `@on.auto/providers` names, that is neither https nor on a loopback address (`isPinnableUrl` of `@on.auto/tool-servers`), and what `consentIssuesOf` of `@on.auto/providers` refuses in an `authorization` credential, as in its provider's way. A `scheme` is refused in words of its own: of a kind that places no value, `Expected no scheme: a none credential places no value in a header for the runtime to write a scheme before`; beside an `in` other than `header`, `Expected no scheme, which goes before a value sent in a header: this credential is sent in a query parameter`; holding `$`, which the reading of the placed entry takes for a reference or an escape, `Expected a scheme without $, which the reading of the entry takes for a reference or an escape`; and holding a character that is no HTTP token's, the characters of a header name (`headerNamePattern`), `Expected a scheme of the characters a header name holds, such as Bearer`.

A `secret` takes `instructions`, `key_page`, `example` and `pattern` beside `name`, `in` and `scheme`, and no other kind takes them; each is `keyFields` of `@on.auto/providers`, the one place of their checks and of a provider's way, which its README sets out. They sit on the credential with its other fields, which the facts and every answer hold in the open, and never beside the secret, which the person gives on the page and the secret store keeps. `add_connection` requires `instructions` and `key_page` of a `secret` given by hand, each at its field in words that say what to write, `Expected instructions, which a secret credential given by hand takes: in Markdown, where the person makes the key, with a link to that page, the plan or role it needs, the permissions it needs and its format`; it refuses the four beside a provider, whose way gives them, `Expected no instructions: the provider gives the credential but its kind`, and beside a credential of consent given by hand, `Expected no example: the person signs in at the server's authorization server, so no key is made`. A provider's way passes them to the connection's credential as it wrote them (`additionOfProvider`), so `read_link` answers them for either, and `provider`, `null` or a name, says whether the agent or the provider wrote them.

## The facts and the fold

The connections of an org live in one stream, `connections` relative to the org, `connectionsStream`, and `org/<org>/connections` in full, `connectionsStreamOfOrg(org)`, and the documents they were added with in streams of their own (see [The documents connections were added with](#the-documents-connections-were-added-with)); the registrations of the runtime's clients live in another, `runtime/client-registrations`, `clientRegistrationsStream` (see below). The main entry exports the three, so a shell that keeps the streams of an org apart from those of the runtime, or lends them from objects of their own, routes each by its name rather than by restating it. Every fact carries `by` and `at` in its metadata, the `ActedContext` of `@on.auto/operations`, and none holds a secret, a token, a code, a verifier or a state, or a value derived from them but the digests of the link's token and of the consent's state:

### A connection added under another name of its provider

Every reader of the stream folds it through `connectionsFoldOf(providers)`, and every reader of `connections_1` reads it through the same providers (`namedNowIn` and `actingAsAddedOf`, the two readings the fold makes of a `connection_added`, applied to the kept connection while its addition still stands for what they read), the fold of `registryDeciderOf(providers)` (`src/registry/registry-decider.ts`), made from the providers the reader serves: the operations, the source of the calls, the break a call records and the signing keys alike (`SigningReading` names its `providers` for that). There is no fold or decider of the stream without them, so no reader can see a name the others do not. Before each fact is folded, `factsAsNamedIn(providers)` (`src/registry/facts-as-named.ts`) reads a `connection_added` as its provider is named now, through `asNamedIn` of `@on.auto/providers`: a `provider` recorded under a name a provider's entry declares in its `aliases` reads as that provider's own name, and the entry's `document`, where both it and the provider's own are documents beside the entries, as the provider's own. The connection's `name`, the person's word, by which a function's steps call it, and every other field read as recorded. So a release that renames a provider and keeps its old name as an alias lists, serves, reconnects and removes the connections made before it under the new name, and every lookup of their provider finds it (`src/operations/renamed-providers.test.ts`).

It is upcasting, the established way an event-sourced system reads a fact an earlier release recorded: "To upcast an event means to transform it from its original structure to its new structure" (Axon Framework, [Event Versioning](https://docs.axoniq.io/axon-framework-reference/4.11/events/event-versioning/)), done as the fact is read and never written back; and the mapping it applies is an alias the reader's own entry declares, as Avro's reader maps a writer's name: "Aliases function by re-writing the writer's schema using aliases from the reader's schema" ([Aliases](https://avro.apache.org/docs/1.12.0/specification/#aliases)). It is the right one here, rather than a fact of a rename or a rewrite of the stream: the fact is true as recorded, since the connection was added from the provider then called by that name; the rename happened in a release and not in the org, so no fact belongs in the org's stream; one line of data lets every reader read the fact, where copying a stream to rewrite its facts is for facts no reader can read any more; and every copy of the facts, a night's export, a restore point or the merge of an org's stores, stays valid before, during and after a rename, in any order. It is no reader of an earlier shape (`readingEarlierShapes`, [A reader for an earlier shape](../ledger/README.md#a-reader-for-an-earlier-shape)): the shape of the fact does not change, only which provider one of its values names, which the release declares as data, so it takes no fixture, and `ConnectionEventSchema` is the one schema of the stream for every reader. The fold is kept nowhere, so nothing is rebuilt: each read folds the stream as before, with one lookup in the list of providers for each `connection_added`. Where no provider declares `aliases`, no fact is read under another name of its provider (`src/registry/registry.test.ts`); a `connection_added` an earlier release recorded is still read for what it acts as (see [What a connection acts as](#what-a-connection-acts-as)).

### What a connection acts as

A connection records what it acts as, so a page can say it and an Admin can mark it: `permissions` on the credential of its `connection_added`, `application` where it acts as the app and `delegated` where it acts as an account at the service, Microsoft's names for the two, and, where it is `delegated`, `account_type` on the same fact, `service` for an account the company made for automations or `personal` for the account of the person who connects. `add_connection` copies `permissions` from the provider's way it takes, the first of the kind the caller names that is offered, or for a server given by hand from the kind, `application` for `client_credentials` and `delegated` for any other but `none`, which records none; it takes neither as an input, since the person chooses how Auto connects on Studio's pages and no agent can know it, and records the account type `accountTypeWhereNoneIsGiven` of `@on.auto/providers` answers for the way, `personal` where the way takes it. The type is a recorded choice, not a finding: nothing at the service says whether an account is one a company made for automations, so the person says so and `set_connection_account_type` changes it later. The record answers `permissions` beside `account_type`, `null` for a credential of the kind `none`, and the credential it answers holds no `permissions`, so a credential read back is taken by `add_connection` as it is (`connectionShown` of `src/registry/connection.ts`). The runtime uses `permissions` to find a connection's way among its provider's ways (`wayOfTheConnection` of `src/registry/what-connections-act-as.ts`), for the groups of more, an app credential, a key's test and the ending codes; it acts on `account_type` nowhere yet.

A `connection_added` an earlier release recorded holds neither, and nothing is written back for it: the two members are optional in the one schema of the fact, as `asked_by`, `document` and `events` were when they came, and the fold reads a fact that holds no `permissions` before it folds it, beside the reading of its provider's name (`factsAsReadIn` of `src/registry/facts-as-named.ts`, `factsAsTheyAct` of `src/registry/what-connections-act-as.ts`): its `permissions` are those its provider's way of its kind declares, where its provider has exactly one way of that kind, as every provider had when such a fact was recorded, or else those of its kind, and its `account_type` is the one recorded where none is given, `personal` for every way shipped today, so every sign-in and key connected before reads as the personal account of the person who connected it. This is a tolerant reading of a weak schema (Young, "Versioning in an Event Sourced System", "Weak Schema"), not a reader of an earlier shape (`readingEarlierShapes`): a way's permissions are declared only in its provider's entry, which a reader of a shape, a function of the fact alone, cannot see, as a connection made through a gateway or with a bot's key shows. Every fixture of `connection_added` from release 0.46.0 to 0.69.0 is folded so in `src/registry/what-connections-act-as.test.ts`, and the fixtures of the new shape and of the new fact are in `src/registry/0.70.0`. `connection_account_type_set` is a new type of the stream, so a runtime of a release before does not read a stream that holds it: the server's ledger refuses to open, naming the type, and a fold of the stream fails, so a self-hosted runtime that has recorded one does not go back to an earlier release. There is no read model and no projection: each read folds the stream, as for every fact here.

Both ask the decision point, as `may_add` does, and no role name is compared.

### The way a pending connection connects

An agent adds a connection, and the person who opens its link chooses how Auto connects: one of the ways its provider's entry lists, or one of the ways a server given by hand offers, and, for a way that acts as an account, whether that account is a service account or their own. `choose_way` (`src/page/way-choices.ts`) takes `{ token, credential: { kind, permissions? }, account_type?, placeholders? }` through the link, as the other operations of the page do, and records `connection_way_chosen` with the credential the way makes. So the connection keeps its id, its name, its playbooks and its link, which a removal and an addition again would lose.

- The decision is pure (`decideOnItsWay` of `src/registry/way-decisions.ts`, through `registryDeciderOf(providers)`). The link must be open, as for `approve`. The connection must be `pending`. The way is found among the ways offered (`waysOfferedFor`): its provider's ways, or, for a server given by hand, the ways its `connection_added` kept, or its credential alone where it kept none, by kind and permissions together (`isTheWayAsked` of `@on.auto/providers`), so the connection finds the way chosen afterwards as it finds any way. An account type is checked against the types the way takes (`accountTypesOf`), in the words of `accountTypeIssueOf`, and refused for a way that acts as the app or sends no credential. Choosing the way the connection holds, with the values and the address it holds, records `connection_account_type_set` where the type given differs and nothing otherwise, so the operation is repeatable and keeps a sign-in under way. Choosing it with other values records `connection_way_chosen` again, keeping the account type unless one is given. Choosing another records `connection_way_chosen`, with the type given or, left out, `personal` where the way takes it (`accountTypeWhereNoneIsGiven`).
- Before it is decided, the handler makes the credential (`credentialChosenFor` of `src/page/chosen-ways.ts`). For a provider it is made from the entry as `add_connection` makes it, with the provider's values the connection was added with, the way's own values given, or, left out, those the connection holds, the order of the ways here and the organization's own OAuth client where no client here covers the way (`providerCredentialChosen` of `src/providers/chosen-provider-ways.ts`), which also answers the values the connection then holds and the address they resolve to; a value the way asks for that is missing or does not fit, or a value it does not ask for (`valuesUnaskedBy`), is refused at `/placeholders`, and a way through a gateway this runtime does not have at `/credential`. A server given by hand asks no value, so any is refused. For a server given by hand it is the way kept. The runtime's rules then judge the connection with that credential (`policyRefusalOf`), `forbidden` in their words. After the decision, the client secrets the fold let go of and the verifier of a dropped start are forgotten.
- A server given by hand keeps every way its metadata names. Where `namedWay` answers that its authorization server lists `client_credentials` in `grant_types_supported` (RFC 8414), the addition records `ways`: its sign-in first, and client credentials at the same issuer and scope beside it. Where the agent gave client credentials and the server names a sign-in, the sign-in is kept beside them. Each kept way holds its `permissions` and none holds `account_types`, so a way that acts as an account takes both, since nothing Auto read says which the server's accounts are. `read_link` answers them as `ways`.
- The patterns: the Decider (Jérémie Chassaing, "Functional Event Sourcing Decider", 2021), so each rule is a function of the folded state and the command, proven with no I/O (`src/registry/way-decisions.test.ts`); the aggregate as the consistency boundary (Vaughn Vernon, "Effective Aggregate Design", 2011), since the fact belongs to the org's connections stream, whose one fold holds the link, the status and the name, so a choice is decided against the state an approval is; and a weak schema (Greg Young, "Versioning in an Event Sourced System", "Weak Schema"), since `ways` is an optional member of `connection_added`, absent from every fact an earlier release recorded, which reads as the credential alone, so no reader of an earlier shape is needed. The fixture of the new shape is in `src/registry/0.71.0`, since `0.70.0` holds a shape already recorded, and the new fact's in `src/registry/0.70.0`.
- The values and the address are carried by the fact, as resolved when the way was chosen, as it carries the credential (event-carried state transfer, Martin Fowler, "What do you mean by Event-Driven?", 2017), so the fold (`wayChosenAfter` of `src/registry/kept-settings.ts`) reads no entry and a later change to an entry moves no connection. `placeholders` and `base_url` are optional members (a weak schema again): a fact without them leaves the connection's values and address as they were, `base_url: null` takes the address away, so the calls go to the server the document names. Their fixture is in `src/registry/0.72.0`, beside the earlier shape's in `0.70.0`, which the same schema reads.
- `connection_way_chosen` is a new type of the stream, so a self-hosted runtime that has recorded one does not go back to an earlier release: the earlier release refuses to open the ledger and names the type.
- It is left out of `loopConnectionFacts`, as `connection_added` is: a pending connection subscribes to nothing, and the fact changes none of what `loopConnectionsOf` reads.

### Someone who may not approve a connection asks for it

`add_connection` asks the decision point whether the caller may approve the connection it adds, the question `may_connect` of `list_providers` answers, asked of `approve` as the page serves it (`approvingAskedOf` of `src/policy/policy-rules.ts`), never by a role's name. Where it may, the call is as it was: a link. Where it may not, it records a request and answers no link (`addedAsARequest` of `src/operations/add-connection.ts`): the connection `pending`, its `request` open and no `connect`, so a client tells a request from a link by its members, and the words say an admin approves it. Where the decision point cannot decide, the call records nothing and is `unavailable` (`mayCallEach` of `@on.auto/operations`), since a request recorded on an error would stand. `approve` declares that it manages connections, so where people sign in an Admin is answered a link and an Editor records a request, while a key and the local developer, whom no role holds, are answered a link as before, as the pinned decision table of the server shows (`packages/server/src/composition/served-decision-table.test.ts`); see [Who manages the connections](#who-manages-the-connections).

- `message` is taken by `add_connection`, at most 500 characters (`mostMessageCharacters` of `src/operations/request-fields.ts`), kept in the request's fact where a request is recorded and ignored where the answer is a link. It is the person's words: it is answered in `request.message` and nothing reads it.
- A request has no age and no link. It ends when the connection is connected, through a link `reconnect_connection` gives it as it gives any pending connection one, when it is declined, or when it is removed (`requestedAfter` of `src/registry/kept-requests.ts`, which folds every fact of the connection, so each end is read from the fact alone).
- `decline_connection_request`, `POST /connections/{id}/decline`, served over HTTP alone since an agent never declines, refuses a connection with no open request before anything else, then removes the connection as `remove_connection` does, with `removedAround`, forgetting what was given on its page, and records the decline beside the removal. It declares that it manages connections, as `remove_connection` does, so only an Admin declines a request.
- The group of more of an addition, `more`, is checked for a request as for a link and is not kept: a request has no link to ask it with.
- The patterns: the Decider (Jérémie Chassaing, "Functional Event Sourcing Decider", 2021), so the request and the decline are functions of the folded connection and the command, proven with no I/O (`src/registry/request-decisions.test.ts`); the aggregate as the consistency boundary (Vaughn Vernon, "Effective Aggregate Design", 2011), since a request holds a name and counts toward the 50 connections of an org, which only the connections stream can check; and events that say what happened in the business's words (Greg Young, "CQRS Documents", 2010), the request and the decline each its own fact beside the addition and the removal, as `connection_approved` is beside `connection_connected`. No read model: the stream is folded on each call, as every connection is.
- `connection_requested` and `connection_request_declined` are new types of the stream, so a self-hosted runtime that has recorded one does not go back to an earlier release: the earlier release refuses to open the ledger and names the type.
- Neither is in `loopConnectionFacts`: neither changes what `loopConnectionsOf` reads, and the `connection_removed` beside a decline asks for a round already.

### The documents connections were added with

The OpenAPI document an HTTP API is given whole, in `document_text`, is kept in a stream of its own, `connection-documents/<sha256>` relative to the org and `org/<org>/connection-documents/<sha256>` in full, named by the SHA-256 of its text in UTF-8, in hexadecimal (`src/documents/document-streams.ts`), rather than in the connections' stream, which every operation over connections reads whole. The stream holds one fact, `connection_document_kept`, `{ text, sha256, bytes }`, with the context of who kept it and when, which `documentDecider` records only where the stream holds none, so the same text given again, by another connection or by an addition asked again, keeps nothing twice. Its kind, `connection-documents`, the first segment of the name, is not `connections`, whose every stream the org's change feed reads and decodes strictly (`connectionChanges`), so neither the feed nor a fold of the connections reads a text.

- `add_connection` runs every check first, the read when added and the check of the text for a credential among them, then refuses a name taken and an org full by one read of the connections, by the rule the decider holds (`additionRefusalOf` of `src/registry/registry-decisions.ts`), so an addition refused leaves no text behind; it keeps the text (`keepDocument` of `src/documents/kept-documents.ts`), and then records `connection_added` and `connection_link_issued` as for any connection, the decider deciding again (`recordedAdditionOf` of `src/operations/recorded-additions.ts`). The two writes are made one after the other, since the ledger appends to one stream at a time: a failure between them leaves a text that no connection names, which nothing reads, since a text is reached only from a connection's record, and the addition asked again keeps nothing more.
- A process reads the text once, as it first prepares the connection (`documentTextOf`), from the stream its record's `sha256` names, and takes it only where its own SHA-256 and size are the record's. A text that is not there, or another, is a defect, as a fact that no longer decodes is: the connection answers as failing, `The tool server ledgers could not be used: The OpenAPI document the connection ledgers was added with is not kept`, or `… is not the one it was added with`, sends nothing to its service, and is prepared again at its next use. `list_connections`, `get_connection` and the change feed read the record alone.
- `read_link` reads the text too, for Studio's page, from the same stream on the same two checks (`readableDocumentText` of `src/documents/kept-documents.ts`), and where a check fails answers `tools: null` rather than failing the read, so the page can say so and offer no approval. It reads the text as the addition did, with the same stand-in for the credential (`readWithAStandIn` of `src/entries/entry-checks.ts`), through `describedToolServers`, and answers each tool the text offers that the entry's `allowed` allows, by `allowsTool`, the rule a served connection's tools are narrowed by, `{ name, summary, mark }`, by name: its mark by `markedTool`, the rule a listing marks a served tool by, and its summary by `summaryOf`, the first sentence `search` answers, all three of `@on.auto/tool-servers`, made plain: each control character (Unicode's `Cc`) becomes a space and the bidirectional formatting characters of UAX #9, sections 2.1 to 2.6, are taken out, since Unicode's security guidance says never to allow bidi overrides (UTR #36, 2.5), so a summary reads in the order it is written (`toolsOfTheDocument` of `src/documents/document-tools.ts`). It answers no other word of the text. The answer is a query over the record and the kept text, worked out as it is asked and kept nowhere: no projection, read model or fact holds it, and a document kept later is read the same way.
- A removal records `connection_removed`, as for any connection. No fact is deleted: the text stops being read, since a removed connection is never prepared.
- The text kept outside the stream every operation reads, which holds a reference to it, is the Claim Check of Hohpe and Woolf's _Enterprise Integration Patterns_: the record stays small whatever a document weighs, and the text is read once, where it is used. It is kept before the fact that names it, the rule the ledger holds for a payload kept outside a fact, "a payload is put before the fact that names it" ([Payloads kept outside a fact](../ledger/README.md#payloads-kept-outside-a-fact)), so no fact names a text that was never kept.

- The six operations of the page are permitted by `org:write` alone, as the operations that add and reconnect the connection are, since only a caller who holds it was given a link; a caller with `org:read`, `playbook:read` or `playbook:write` only is refused `forbidden` before any handler runs, even with the token. The token still binds them, and their caller must still belong to the org of the route and, where people sign in, manage the org's connections (see [Who manages the connections](#who-manages-the-connections)).

### The model Auto answers members with

On a company's own server, members can write to Auto through a connection whose provider's entry declares a `chat` and one of whose `websocket` destinations says under `chat` which messages a person wrote, through the app credential that destination names. Auto needs a model to answer them, which an Admin chooses. On a host that answers members through a connection's gateway (Auto's cloud), they can write to Auto through a connection made by a way that declares a `gateway` (`chatOfferedOn` of `src/providers/provider-listing.ts`); there Auto answers with a model of the host's own until an Admin chooses one.

- **The record.** `connection_model_set`, one more fact of the org's connections stream, decided by the same Decider (Jérémie Chassaing, "Functional Event Sourcing Decider", 2021): a new fact type rather than a changed one (Greg Young, "Versioning in an Event Sourced System", 2017), so earlier facts read unchanged. The fold keeps it beside the record, `model` of `KeptConnection`, `null` until one is set; `connected()` of `src/registry/registry.ts` carries it, so connecting again keeps it. There is no read model, no projection and no table: each call folds the stream, as for every other fact here. There is no unset.
- **Connect.** `give_credential` takes `model`. The rule that a connection that lets members write has a model spans the credential and the model of one connection, so both are decided in one decision and recorded in one append (Vaughn Vernon, "Effective Aggregate Design", 2011), as `playbooks` is. Where the app credential that opens the chat is given, the runtime lists a model to choose, the caller may set the model, and none is given or saved, Connect waits for a model: it is refused as `invalid_input` at `/model`, in words that name the app credential by its label in the entry, and nothing is kept. A chat through the connection's gateway, which no credential given here opens, a server that lists no model to choose and a caller who may not set the model do not wait, and a connection connected again keeps the model it has. A model given where the credential that lets members write is not given, or no chat is offered, or one the runtime does not offer, is refused as `invalid_input` at `/model`, and nothing is kept. A model given by a caller who may not set it is refused as `forbidden`.
- **Facts from outside the stream come in with the command** (Vaughn Vernon, "Implementing Domain-Driven Design", 2013, application services): the operation works out whether a chat is offered and through which credential (`chatOfferedFor` of `src/operations/connection-chat.ts`, from the entry, the way and whether the runtime opens sockets), whether the caller may set the model, which it asks the decision point about `set_connection_model`, whether the runtime lists a model to choose, which it asks only where Connect would otherwise wait, as `waitsForAModel` of the command, and the words of the check a run makes for a model given, and hands them to the decision, which stays pure (`src/registry/model-decisions.ts`) and checks the saved model and the credential given against the fold. The handler checks the same rule before it keeps any value, so nothing reaches a service for a Connect that would be refused.
- **Later changes.** `set_connection_model`, over HTTP alone, declares `connectionsManagement` of `@on.auto/operations`, as every operation that changes a connection does, so the decision point's policy `connections-managed-by-admins` keeps it to an Admin (see [Who manages the connections](#who-manages-the-connections)).
- **The answer.** `list_connections`, `get_connection`, `read_link`, `give_credential` and `set_connection_model` answer `chat`, `{ credential, held, model, default_model, may_set_model }`, worked out when read from the fold, the entry, the host and the decision point, and left out where no chat is offered, for a removed connection and wherever the services hold no `chatModels`. `credential` names the app credential that opens the chat and is left out for a chat through the connection's gateway, which is held while the connection is connected; `default_model` is the host's model, `throughAGateway.defaultModel`, and is left out where the chat goes through an app credential, since Auto answers nobody there until a model is chosen. Nothing derived is stored, so a release that changes an entry needs no migration.
- **Before a connection exists.** `list_providers` answers, on a way, `chat`, `{ credential, default_model, may_set_model }` by the same rule (`chatOfferedOn`) and with the same fields as a connection's, where a connection made that way would offer the chat: the server's answer states what the client may offer next (Roy Fielding, "Architectural Styles and the Design of Network-based Software Architectures", 2000, section 5.1.5), as `may_set_model` does on a connection, so a client never infers it from a provider's name.
- **Where it applies.** The services' `chatModels`, `{ listsAModel, refusalOf, throughAGateway? }`, are the host's: the server composes them only where it signs people in, since only there can members write to Auto, `listsAModel` from its model catalog and `refusalOf` from the resolution a run checks a model with (`modelChoiceOf` of `@on.auto/reasoning`) and no `throughAGateway`, and a server that signs nobody in composes none, so it answers no `chat` and serves no `set_connection_model`; Auto's cloud composes all three from the organization's plan, `throughAGateway.defaultModel` being the model Auto answers members with while none is chosen.
- **What a host reads for a turn.** `modelsChosenIn({ registry, providers }, org)` folds the org's stream and answers, for each connected connection that has an account and a saved model, its provider, the account's id and the model, in the order the connections were connected, so a host that answers members picks the model of the connection the turn's account names. It is a query that replays the stream, as `socketsHeldIn` is (Martin Fowler, "Event Sourcing", 2005).
- **One-way.** The first `connection_model_set` a runtime records, on a company's own server or on Auto's cloud, is a new type of the stream, so that runtime does not go back to an earlier release, which refuses to open the ledger and names the type. Upgrading alone records nothing.

### A connection made through a gateway

A provider's way of consent that names a `gateway` (`@on.auto/providers`) connects through a service of whoever runs Auto that makes the consent with its own client, keeps the vendor's tokens, and hands the runtime a key of the connection's own; every call of the connection reaches the vendor through that service with the key. It is Gateway Offloading of Microsoft's Azure Architecture Center ("Offload shared or specialized service functionality to a gateway proxy"). The connection stays what a sign-in is to a person, a way of the kind `authorization` with its link, its start, its state, its sealed verifier, its finish, its scopes and its words, and one thing is new, the source of its client, `gateway`, which its credential holds from its addition, as `own` is held (`additionOfProvider` of `@on.auto/providers`). Nothing names a service, a host, a header or an audience here: the gateway's address is read from the entry of the connection's provider each time it is needed (`gatewayOf` of `src/page/connection-gateways.ts`, `declaredGatewayOf` of `src/providers/provider-listing.ts`), as the document and the failure codes are, and the key's place is the security scheme its credential names.

- **Offered only where gateways are switched on.** `gateways` of the consent client (`@on.auto/tool-servers`) is a switch for whoever composes the runtime, which Auto's cloud turns on and the server never does, since a gateway takes only the signed note of the host that composes the runtime. Elsewhere `list_providers` lists the way `offered: false` with `The provider messages connects through a service that Auto's cloud runs, which this runtime does not have`, search offers no provider whose one way it is, and `add_connection` refuses it at `/provider` in those words before anything is recorded (`gatewayUnofferedOf` of `src/providers/provider-listing.ts`). A runtime that takes no inbound connection needs nothing here: every step is a request the runtime sends.
- **The start** sends the gateway the start's own `state` and the challenge of a verifier the runtime seals, and records `connection_consent_started` with the client the gateway answers and `client_source` `gateway`. A gateway that refuses the start is `conflict`, `The gateway of the connection messages refused to start its consent`; a runtime where gateways are not switched on is `conflict` in the words above; a provider that names no gateway any more is `conflict`, `The provider of the connection messages no longer names the gateway it is connected through; whoever runs this runtime names it again before the connection is connected or removed`.
- **The finish** takes the handle the gateway sent the person back with, `Expected handle: the consent of the connection messages began through its gateway, which answers a handle, not a code` for a code, and `Expected a handle its gateway takes; it refused this one` at `/handle` for a handle refused. It seals the key in the secret store as any credential, records its id as `secret_id` on `connection_connected` with the client, the scope granted and `account`, and answers the connection. Where the record fails after the hand-off, it ends the key it was just handed at the gateway, the Compensating Transaction of Microsoft's Azure Architecture Center, so nothing holds a key the record does not name; so does a hand-off whose account or scope the runtime does not take, `conflict`, `The gateway of the connection messages handed over an account or a scope this runtime does not take, so the connection is not connected and the person starts again`.
- **Connecting again** keeps the connection working on the key it held until the new key is recorded; then it ends the key it held before at the gateway, always, since a key is the connection's own and ending it ends nothing else, and forgets it. An end the gateway does not confirm is asked three times, with a pause between, for 5 seconds at most, then logged at the error level, `The key a connection held before it was connected again was not confirmed ended at its gateway; nothing here holds it any more`, and the connection stays connected on the new key. This is Parallel Change (Danilo Sato, martinfowler.com): the new key is made while the old one works, the connection moves to it, then the old one is ended.
- **A call** places the key under the security scheme the credential names, as a key given on the page is placed, and no consent of the runtime's is served for it. A gateway that refuses the key breaks the connection as `consent_ended`, `The consent of the connection messages was revoked or has ended, so it needs to be reconnected`.
- **The removal must be confirmed.** `remove_connection` ends the connection's key at the gateway before it records `connection_removed`: done on a confirmation, and on the gateway's word that it no longer keeps the key, so removing twice is removing once, an Idempotent Receiver (Gregor Hohpe and Bobby Woolf, _Enterprise Integration Patterns_). Anything else records nothing, keeps the key and answers `unavailable`, so a removal, an org's deletion among them, is asked again until it is confirmed: `The key of the connection messages could not be ended at its gateway, so the connection is not removed; try again shortly: its gateway did not confirm it` where the cause passes by itself, as it does too for a key the store cannot read now, and, without "try again shortly", where it passes only once whoever runs the runtime changes something: a host the rules refuse, a runtime where gateways are not switched on, and a provider that no longer names a gateway, `…: its provider no longer names the gateway it is connected through, which whoever runs this runtime names again before the connection is removed`, the likely cause of which, a release taken back or an entry written wrong, passes, while forgetting the key would leave it at the gateway for good. Where the runtime no longer holds the key at all, no try can ever end it: the connection is removed, and the error level says so, `The key of a connection made through a gateway was not ended there, since this runtime no longer holds it; the connection is removed, and nothing here holds the key any more`. A finish that connects the connection again between the end of its key and the record of the removal leaves the removed connection holding a newer key, which the removal ends too, as it ends a key a finish replaced, before it forgets it.
- **Log lines name ids.** Every line a removal or a finish logs carries the org and the connection by their ids, `org` and `connection` among its annotations, and never a key, a connection's name or a person, so a key left at a gateway is found by the ids it was left under.
- **Only a declared entry's own connections reach the gateway.** A credential whose source is `gateway` is recorded only from a provider's entry: `add_connection` takes no `client_source`, and `connection_added` refuses one given by hand, `Expected a provider, whose entry alone names the gateway a connection's consent is made through`. A server given by hand whose address is at the origin of a gateway, or of the address a provider with a gateway calls, is refused as it is added, again once the document it was given by its address has named its server, and again as it is used, `A connection given by hand may not reach https://gateway.example.com, which the provider messages connects through; add the provider messages instead` (`gatewayRefusalOf` of `src/policy/policy-rules.ts`), so no connection written by hand is sent to a gateway with the host's note. The same holds for a connection of a provider whose whole address a person gave, a placeholder of `format: uri`, since that address is written by hand too: at such an origin it is refused as it is added and as it is used, `A connection may not reach https://gateway.example.com, since the provider messages connects through it. Give the address of the server this connection is for.` The gateway holds the other two locks: the key it keeps the digest of, and the org of the note.
- **The key is in no answer, no fact and no log line.** It lives in the secret store under `secret_id`, is read to place it on a call and to end it, and is held among the secrets a call's answer is scrubbed of, as a key given on the page is.

The stored shapes follow the rule of the ledger, that a release reads what the releases before it recorded:

- `account` on `connection_connected` is a new optional member, read as naming none where a fact holds none: Weak Schema, Greg Young's _Versioning in an Event Sourced System_, chapter "Weak Schema", where "A new version of an event must be convertible from the old version of the event". Where the connecting names the account, as a sign-in does, it is a fact of that moment and does not change until the next connecting, so it belongs to the fact that records that moment. A release before the member passes over it, as the codec decodes with no strict option (`src/registry/registry.test.ts` reads `src/registry/0.59.0/connection_connected.json` through that release's schema). Its fixture is in `src/registry/0.59.0`.
- `account` on `connection_identified` is a new optional member, `{ id }` of the same `AccountSchema`, read as naming none where a fact holds none, by the same Weak Schema. A connecting that names no account, as a key does, gets it from the service's answer to the call its entry declares as `identity`, where the entry says where that answer names it: the same observation as the account the connection acts as, made once for the connecting, so one fact with two members and not a second fact or a second writer of `connection_connected`. The decider writes it once for a connecting, and the fold takes it only for a connecting that named none. A release before the member passes over it, and its connection shows no account, as before (`src/registry/registry.test.ts` reads `src/registry/0.71.0/connection_identified.json` through the shape of the release before). Its fixture is in `src/registry/0.71.0`.
- `gateway`, a new value of `client_source` on `connection_added`'s credential, `connection_consent_started` and `connection_connected`, with `in` and `name` on an `authorization` credential beside it, is one a release before cannot read, so it ships read first and is written later, the expand step of Parallel Change: this release reads it, and nothing writes it until an entry this runtime ships names a gateway, which none does. Its fixtures are in `src/registry/0.60.0`, and a release before refusing them is pinned by the same test. There is no roll-back past this release once a connection through a gateway exists: a release before it cannot read that connection's facts, and since every operation and every use of a connection reads and folds the org's whole stream of connections, it then fails every connection of that org, listing, adding, removing and serving among them, not that one alone.
- No projection's version, key or fill changes: there is no read model of connections, and every call folds the stream, which `connected` now reads `account` from, `null` where the fact names none.

### A provider another replaces

A provider's entry may name in `replaces` the providers it takes the place of (`@on.auto/providers`), as when a service comes to be connected another way and the connections made the earlier way are to be connected again. Where the list a runtime serves an org holds both, `replacedOf` of `src/replacements/replaced-providers.ts` answers the provider replaced with the one that replaces it, and nothing names a provider, a service or a host. A name in `replaces` names the provider it finds, by its own name or one of its `aliases`, so an entry replaces a provider by any name it is found by, the connections recorded under either among them, and `list_providers` lists the providers it replaces by the names they have now (`replacementOf` and `replacedNamesOf` of `@on.auto/providers`; `src/replacements/replaced-by-other-names.test.ts`):

- **It is not offered.** `list_providers` lists each way of the provider replaced `offered: false`, `The provider files is replaced here by messages, which a connection is added from instead`, and the provider that replaces it with its `replaces`; search offers no provider none of whose ways is offered, and `add_connection` refuses the provider replaced at `/provider` in those words before anything is recorded.
- **Its connections are not used.** The source serves a connection made from it as `connection_broken`, whatever its status, before anything is read or asked, `The connection files was added from the provider files, which messages replaces here, so no playbook may use it until it is reconnected, which adds it again from messages under the same name`, since reconnecting it is what puts it right, as for every connection served `connection_broken`; it records no break, so the connection's status stays what it was. `start` refuses its consent through a link still open, as `finish` refuses a consent begun before the replacement before it takes its code or handle, with `conflict`, `The connection files was added from the provider files, which messages replaces here, so it is not signed in to again: reconnecting it adds it again from messages under the same name`, so no one signs in the way replaced.
- **Reconnecting it adds it again.** Who may reconnect it is decided before anything is ended or recorded (see [Who starts a connection](#who-starts-a-connection)). Whoever may manage the org's connections may, whoever added it, unless the host starts no connection of the provider that replaces it for them, as Auto's cloud starts one made through a gateway for an admin alone: they are refused in the host's words, `forbidden`. The connection added again is theirs. It then admits an addition from the provider that replaces it, as `add_connection` admits one, under the connection's name, with the playbooks, the tools allowed and the choice of recording its entry holds, the values of the placeholders that provider declares, and the group of more the caller names, and ends the connection's key at its gateway where it has one, as a removal does. The registry then records, in one decision, `connection_removed` of the connection and `connection_added` and `connection_link_issued` of its addition (`decideReplacement` of `src/registry/registry-decisions.ts`), so the name passes from one to the other in one append, and nothing can take it between them. After the record it forgets what a removal forgets, revoking the tokens of a consent where the authorization server publishes a revocation endpoint, and answers the new connection with its link. A step that names the connection by its name serves the new one once the person allows access.
- **Left waiting, it is not a dead end.** A connection added again this way waits, `pending`, and whoever may manage the org's connections reconnects it for a new link at any time, the same connection, as any connection.
- **Who may reconnect, as `list_providers` answers it.** Beside `replaces`, `list_providers` answers the caller `may_reconnect`, `true` for a caller the host lets connect again a connection of that provider whoever added it, and `false` for a caller the host starts no connection of it for, with `reconnect_refusal`, the host's words, so a page shows Connect again to those who may and says to the others who can; it says nothing where the host sets no rule of its own for that provider, as a host that restricts nobody, or where the host cannot say just now. It decides nothing: `reconnect_connection` asks again as it is called.

The facts are the ones a removal and an addition record, so a release before this one reads them; no fact type, stream or shape is new, and no projection's version, key or fill changes. Who may reconnect is not recorded either: the decision point and the host decide it at the call, as they decide who signs in, before the decider is asked. Whether a connection may be used is not recorded: the replacement is what this release serves, not something that happened in an org, so it is read on every use from the entries served, beside the facts folded, as the policy of the moment is (step 1 of [The calls](#the-calls)). An entry that no longer names the provider it replaced serves again, as they were, the connections made from it that nobody reconnected meanwhile, since nothing of them was recorded or forgotten. A connection reconnected meanwhile does not come back: reconnecting it removed it and forgot its credential, as a removal does, and the connection added again under its name stays.

### Who manages the connections

Only an Admin connects an app for the org, and any Admin may approve, reconnect or remove any connection, whoever added it. It is one declaration and one policy, the Policy Enforcement Point and Policy Decision Point of XACML 3.0 and NIST SP 800-162: every operation that changes a connection or acts through its link declares `connectionsManagement` of `@on.auto/operations`, `reconnect_connection`, `remove_connection`, `decline_connection_request`, `set_connection_playbooks`, `set_connection_account_type`, `set_connection_model`, the two signing-secret operations and the seven of the page, `read_link` among them, and the built-in policy `connections-managed-by-admins` of `@on.auto/access-control` refuses such an operation to a person who is not an Admin, however the call arrived, before any handler runs. It reads the caller's role and what the operation declares, never a provider's name. A key and the local developer are not held to a role, as no built-in policy holds them to one. `add_connection` declares nothing: it asks the decision point whether its caller may `approve` what it adds, the question `may_connect` of `list_providers` answers, and records a request where they may not (see [Someone who may not approve a connection asks for it](#someone-who-may-not-approve-a-connection-asks-for-it)). `list_connections` answers `may_manage`, asked of `remove_connection`, so Studio offers the actions that change a connection to whoever may take them. A link is bound to the token alone, not to the person who added the connection: any Admin finishes, reconnects or removes it, one an Editor added among them, which keeps working as it is until an Admin does.

### Who starts a connection

A host may let only some of an org's people start a connection of a provider: Auto's cloud starts one made through a gateway for an admin of the org alone, as its door signs the note of such a start. `starts`, a service the host composes (`ConnectionStarts`), answers, for a provider's name and the caller of the call under way, `anyone`, where the host sets no rule of its own for that provider, `may take over`, where its rule lets the caller connect a connection of the provider, whoever added it, or `{ refused }`, with the host's words, where the host starts none for the caller; it fails `unavailable` where it cannot say, and nothing is then changed. A host that composes none restricts nobody, as the Node shell. The core asks it only where it would change something a start then needs, before it does: reconnecting a connection whose provider another replaces, and the listing of a provider that replaces another. The start itself is still the host's to refuse, whatever the core asked.

## The ending of a credential

`give_credential` hands the secret store, with a secret or a client secret of at least twelve characters, its last four, `keep(value, endsWith)`, and no ending with a shorter value or a private key. The store keeps the ending in the value's row and forgets it with the value, so `remove_connection` forgets both; no fact holds it and the fold records nothing of it. Every operation answers the connection with `credential_ends_with` beside `connected_at`, never inside `credential`, which is what `add_connection` took and the client id given on the page: `list_connections` and `get_connection` read it with `endingsOf` for each connection that has a secret, `give_credential` answers the ending it kept, and the others, whose connection holds none, answer `null`.

## The policy

`readConnectionsSetting(environment)` reads `CONNECTIONS`, a JSON object, `connections` of the configuration file through `ConnectionsSettingSchema`, once, and answers `ConnectionsSetting`: `policy`, what people may add; `registeredClients`, the clients whoever runs the runtime registered at authorization servers (see [The registered clients](#the-registered-clients)), which is no rule about what people may add; `autoClients`, `auto_clients` as the setting gives it, whether a way that takes a registered client takes Auto's client too through Auto's consent service, or `undefined` where the setting says nothing, which the server then decides by local mode; `clientVariables`, the variables their references name; `providers`, the providers whoever runs the runtime adds beside those it ships (see [The providers the setting adds](#the-providers-the-setting-adds)); and `receiveEvents`, `receive_events` as the setting gives it, the ways the runtime receives what a connection's service sends, of `http`, a delivery signed with the connection's signing secret at a request's answer address, or `undefined` where neither the setting nor `CONNECTIONS_RECEIVE_EVENTS` says anything; that variable, the ways separated by commas and none where it is empty, wins over the setting, and a way it does not know is refused like a setting's (`receiveEventsIn` of `src/policy/receiving-ways.ts`). `receivesOver(setting, way)` says whether the runtime receives that way, every way where nothing is said. The policy is `people_may_add`, `true` when left out; `allowed_hosts`, each a host or `*.` and a host, every host when left out; `credential_kinds`, every kind when left out; and `variables`, the variable names a `reference` may give, none when left out. `namingNoOwnVariable(setting, ownVariables)` checks `variables` apart from the reading, since a reader may know the variables it reads for itself only once it has read what refers to them: a name in `variables` that is one of `ownVariables`, or of `clientVariables`, is a problem at its pointer, so the server stops at start rather than let a connection send one of them. A pattern `*.example.com` takes every host under `example.com` and not `example.com` itself, and the hosts checked, at add and at use, are those of an `mcp` connection's `url`, of an `openapi` connection's `document` and `base_url`, so one given its document whole asks an allow-list for its `base_url`'s host alone, or, where its provider's document lies beside the entries and the provider gives no `base_url`, the server that document names, `documentServerOf` of `@on.auto/providers`, and of a `client_credentials` credential's `issuer`, which the client secret or the key's assertion reaches, and of an `authorization` credential's `issuer` and endpoints. A malformed value is `ConnectionsSettingInvalid`, naming each problem at its pointer.

### The registered clients

`registered_clients` lists the clients whoever runs the runtime registered at authorization servers (`RegisteredClientsSchema` of `src/registered-clients/registered-clients.ts`), each with `issuers`, the issuers it is registered at, at least one, `client_id`, and one of `client_secret` as a reference, `private_key` as a reference with `algorithm`, or neither for a public client, and, where the authorization server's metadata leaves the choice, `token_endpoint_auth_method`, one its kind authenticates with: `client_secret_basic` or `client_secret_post` with a secret, `private_key_jwt` with a key and `none` without either. Each issuer is an https URL, or an http URL on a loopback address, judged by the rule a provider's addresses are judged by, `isPinnableTemplate` of `@on.auto/providers`, so one written with a placeholder, as `https://login.example.com/{tenant}`, is kept as its template. A client may name `scopes`, at least one, none of them empty, the scopes it may ask at its issuers, each as a provider's way writes it. Where clients name issuers that compare equal, a trailing slash aside, as the MCP client compares issuers (`isSameIssuer` of `@on.auto/tool-servers`), each names its scopes: one that names none is refused at its `scopes`, in words that say it would serve every way there that no client before it covers, and a client after it never. ## The subscriptions of a playbook

A playbook may hold a subscription at the service of one of its connections, so the service tells the runtime what happens at once rather than waiting to be read. The loop that decides which a playbook holds and arranges them is `subscriptionLoopOf` of `@on.auto/providers`; this package keeps their record, as the event-sourced history of each subscription and a read model of what every playbook holds, and the adapters of the loop's ports over the ledger, the secret store and the registry of connections, their composition, which every shell that runs the loop uses, and a round of it (`src/subscriptions`).

Each subscription is one stream of its playbook, `playbook/<org>/<playbook>/event-subscriptions/<id>`, its id a version 5 UUID of its connection and its topic, so a key names one stream and one stream holds one key, whatever the playbook asks of it after (`eventSubscriptionStreamOf`). Three facts happen to it, each decided against the stream's version as every fact of the ledger is, with `by` the runtime: A check's reads are kept apart from it, one short stream for each point read to (`eventSubscriptionReadStreamOf`), so the subscription's stream stays as short as its life, however often a check finds something (Oskar Dudycz, "Keep your streams short!"); `EventSubscriptionEventSchema` reads the four facts, the three of the subscription's stream and the read.

| Fact                         | Data                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `event_subscription_made`    | `connection`, `topic`, `type` (`webhook` or `queue`), `id`, the service's id of the subscription, `secret_id`, the id in the secret store of the secret its deliveries are proven with, where there is one, never the secret, and `declared`, the SHA-256 of the declaration it was made by; for a check, `type` `polling`, `declared` and `since`, the point it reads from, and no id |
| `event_subscription_failed`  | `connection`, `topic`, `kind` and `because`, the class and the reason of the failure, as the performer of `@on.auto/providers` classes them, and `attempted`, what the attempt saw: `connected`, when the connection was last connected, and `declared`; absent for a subscription the bound of the loop refused                                                                       |
| `event_subscription_removed` | `connection`, `topic`; decided once, so a removal of a subscription the stream does not hold records nothing                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| `event_subscription_read`    | `connection`, `topic` and `since`, the point a check read to once it took at least one event in; on a stream of its own, `event-subscription-reads/<id>`, its id a version 5 UUID of the connection, the topic and the point, decided once, so the same point recorded twice records nothing                                                                                           |

`eventSubscriptionPresenter` (`src/subscriptions/subscription-presenter.ts`) shows the three among the playbook's events, as `list_playbook_events` and `get_event` read them, each under the type it is recorded by with its `connection` and `topic`, and a failure with its `kind` and `because`; it never shows the service's id of a subscription, the id of its secret, a digest or what an attempt saw. It reads the facts as they were recorded and fills no table, and the server and the cloud each compose it beside the presenters of `@on.auto/definitions`.

A renewal and a passing fault of a subscription made are not facts: the time it lapses, when it is next due and its faults in a row are positions the loop moves in place, which the next round can work out again. A key, a topic and an id are each a part as the delivery bounds one, some text of at most 256 bytes with no forbidden character, `BoundedPart` of `@on.auto/operations`, which the record holds and nothing longer (`holds`).

`eventSubscriptions` (`src/subscriptions/subscription-rows.ts`) is the read model, a keyed projection the server keeps, `event_subscriptions_2`, one row a subscription, keyed by its stream's id, a read keyed by the subscription it names: `connection` and `topic`; what was made, `type`, `id`, `secret_id` and `declared`; the failure, `failure_kind`, `failure_because`, `failure_connected` and `failure_declared`; `removed`; the positions, `failures_in_a_row`, `lapses_at` and `due_at`, columns its reader advances, which no fact sets but a removal, which empties them; `reconcile_due_at`, when the playbook's subscriptions are next due to be reconciled; and, for a check, `since`, the point it reads from, which a made fact and a read set and the check advances between them, and `page_token`, the continuation of a walk a round cut, which only the check advances and a made fact or a removal empties. A read of a key removed or never made changes nothing. A made fact clears the failure and leaves the positions; a failed fact leaves what was made and the positions, and starts a row of its own for a key never made; a removal leaves the row, removed, with nothing made, failed or positioned, so a later fact of the key starts it afresh. Every fact sets `reconcile_due_at` to its own time, and a removal empties it. The fold is a function of the row and the fact alone, so the table rebuilt from the facts holds what it held as each was appended, but for the positions, which start empty, as no fact stands for them; the loop then treats a subscription whose positions are unknown as due at once where its lease lapses. The table is filled as the ledger opens, from the facts of its four types alone, and its layout steps, `subscriptionLayoutSteps`, 28 for its first version and 31 for its second, take no statement, the ledger making the table. A rebuilt row holds the point the last fact recorded and no page, and a check reads again from there with no second start, taking again only what the intake records once.

The adapters of the loop's ports:

- `subscriptionRecordsOf(ledger)`, the record: `read(playbook)` reads every row not removed, a page of 100 at a time; `made`, `failed` and `removed` decide the fact on the key's stream at the time of Effect's `Clock`; `moved` advances the three positions of a row not removed, in one `UPDATE`, and does nothing to a key it does not hold; `marked` advances a check's `since`, page token and positions in one `UPDATE`, only while the row still stands at the `since` the check read from; `readThrough` records `event_subscription_read` only where the row stands at that point.
- `subscriptionSecretsOf(secrets)`, the secrets, kept in the secret store a connection's signing secret is kept in: `make` makes one with `madeSigningSecret` of `@on.auto/identity`, as the Standard Webhooks specification writes one, and keeps it; `keep`, `read` and `forget` keep a secret a service answered, read one or nothing where the store no longer has it, and forget one. A secret leaves the store only `Redacted`, and a failure of the store ends the round as a defect, which the next round meets again.
- `loopConnectionsOf({ registry, providers })`, the connections of a playbook: each one of the org not removed that serves the playbook, by the `playbooks` of its entry, with its id, its name, when it was last connected, the id of the account it was connected in where the service named one, and the destinations its provider's entry declares, in their order, or, for a connection given by hand, the destination its `events` declares, or none where it was given none.
- The account a connection acts as, read once for each connecting: `loopConnectionsOf` gives a connection `identify`, the call its provider's entry declares as `identity`, where the connection is connected and its record holds no answer for its connecting (`identityOwedBy` of `src/subscriptions/owed-identities.ts`, the rule a socket holder carries too); a connection given by hand declares none. The ports' `identified({ org }, connection, identification)` records the answer the loop read, the account the connection acts as and, where the entry says where the answer names it, the account it is in, as two members, through the registry given, by the runtime, `identify` with the `connected_at` the loop read, at the time of Effect's `Clock`, a store that fails it failing the round as the record's writes do; and `triggersAsker` is the triggers' asker, the one of `askers` whose subscriptions carry events, through whose connections alone the loop reads it, since a reply compares no account.
- The names of the places triggers listen in, read once for each place and connecting: `loopConnectionsOf` gives a connection connected `placeNaming`, for each type its provider's entry declares `subject.name` for, the declaration and the places whose names the fold holds for its connecting (`placeNamingOf` of `src/subscriptions/owed-place-names.ts`); a connection pending, broken or removed, or given by hand, carries none. The ports' `placeNamed(playbook, connection, named)` records a name the reader of `@on.auto/providers` read, through the registry given, by the runtime, `name_place` with the `connected_at` the round read, at the time of Effect's `Clock`, a store that fails it failing the round as the record's writes do.
- `playbooksDueOf(ledger, now, most)`, `reconcileDueAfter(ledger, after)` and `reconcileDueSet(ledger, playbook, now, dueAt)`, the schedule of the rounds: the playbooks with a row whose `reconcile_due_at` has come, through the index across playbooks `event_subscriptions_1_reconcile_due`, each once, so `most` counts playbooks and one with many rows due, or whose round keeps failing, leaves room for the others; the soonest after a moment; and the time a round answered, written on every row of the playbook not removed, and never later than `resyncedAfterMs` of `@on.auto/providers` after the round, so a playbook that holds a subscription is looked at again within that period whatever was lost in between, as controller-runtime's `SyncPeriod` reconciles every object again (`cache.Options`, 10 hours by default). `holdsASubscription(ledger, playbook)` says whether a playbook holds one.
- `loopConnectionFacts`, `listensThroughAConnection(parts, playbook)` and `connectionChangesRelayed(parts)`, what asks a playbook for a round when one of the org's connections changes, which no fact of the playbook does. The facts are those after which what `loopConnectionsOf` reads of a connection may change: `connection_connected`, `connection_removed` and `connection_playbooks_set`, so whether it is removed, the playbooks it serves and when it was last connected; `connection_added` and `connection_way_chosen` are left out, since a connection still pending cannot subscribe. A playbook listens through a connection where it holds a subscription, where `sentIn`, a function a shell gives, says it holds an open request sent through one (`holdsASentRequest` of `@on.auto/interaction`), or where `triggersIn` names a trigger that names one: a rule of states and shapes alone, which may say yes where the round then does nothing and never says no where an asker of the loop would name a subscription. One pass of the relay (`src/subscriptions/connection-changes.ts`) reads the facts of the org's connections stream after its place, 200 a read and without their data, and where one of them is of `loopConnectionFacts` it asks each playbook `playbooks` gives, through `asked`, awaits every answer and only then moves its place, to the stream's newest fact as the asks began; a relay that keeps no place, or whose place the ledger no longer holds, asks every playbook once and starts there. A pass answers how many playbooks it asked and how many did not answer, and moves nothing while one did not, so the next pass asks again. It is the message relay of the Transactional Outbox ([Pattern: Transactional outbox](https://microservices.io/patterns/data/transactional-outbox.html), Chris Richardson), with the org's connections stream as the outbox, delivered at least once to an [Idempotent Receiver](https://www.enterpriseintegrationpatterns.com/patterns/messaging/IdempotentReceiver.html) (_Enterprise Integration Patterns_), since a second round sends nothing; to the loop it is the watch of a resource the controller reads and does not own, mapped to the objects to reconcile, as controller-runtime's `Watches` with `handler.EnqueueRequestsFromMapFunc` ([Not Owned Resources](https://book.kubebuilder.io/reference/watching-resources/secondary-resources-not-owned), the Kubebuilder Book), begun by listing once, as client-go's `Reflector` lists and then watches (`ListAndWatch`). Where the place is kept, what an ask does and what wakes a pass is each shell's own (see the server's README).

The ports are composed by `subscriptionLoopPortsOf(parts)` (`src/subscriptions/loop-ports.ts`), from what a shell gives it: the playbook's `ledger`, for the record; the `registry` of the org's connections, given apart from it, since a shell may keep the two in different places, which the ports read and to which they write the account a connection acts as and the names of the places it reaches; `waitingIn`, the reader of the requests that wait in a playbook, a function, since this package does not depend on `@on.auto/interaction`, whose `waitingRequestsOf` a shell gives; `triggersIn`, the reader of the triggers of the playbook's active workflows that name a connection, a function too, whose `connectionTriggersOf` of `@on.auto/definitions/connection-triggers` a shell gives; the connection's calls, `tools`; the secret store, `secrets`; the `providers`; whether the runtime receives over HTTP, `receivesOverHttp`; whether it opens sockets, `opensSockets`; where it is given one, the address its services deliver to, `eventsAddressOf`; and, where it is given one, the intake a check hands what it lists to, `takeIn`, the shell's own hand-off to the workflow host's. The runtime checks an app on a timer only where it is given an intake, and a runtime given none takes nothing in. The address of a connection is the one given, for a webhook and while the runtime receives over HTTP, and none otherwise, so a runtime given none arranges no webhook; the runtime receives a webhook over HTTP, a socket where it opens sockets, and no queue, and a `websocket` destination is taken for a connection only where it holds the app credential the destination names and the service has not refused it, so a connection that holds none is arranged through the next destination its entry declares (`src/subscriptions/loop-connections.ts`); the askers are `requestsAskerOf` and `triggersAskerOf` of `@on.auto/providers` over the two readers, so a subscription a request or a trigger asks for stays while either asks; and a playbook holds at most `mostSubscriptionsThroughAConnection`, 8, through one connection, a constant and no setting: past it the loop records the refusal once, and the request is read on a timer as before. `subscriptionRoundOf({ loop, ledger })` is what a round is: the loop for the playbook, then its next due time written as `reconcileDueSet` writes it, from the time the round is given. A shell that must have a round over by a time hands it a deadline as well, which it passes to the loop, and the round answers what the loop answered, so the shell knows whether the loop stopped at it (`deadlineExceeded`); the server hands none. What wakes a round, and when one is owed, is each shell's own (see the server's README).

What a delivery to a connection's events address is proven against, the two ports the server composes for the address of `@on.auto/api` (`src/subscriptions/subscription-deliveries.ts`): `deliveryDestinationOf({ registry, providers })` answers, for a place, the `webhook` destination per subscription the entry of the connection declares, or a connection given by hand its `events`, the connection being one of the org not removed that serves the playbook, as `loopConnectionsOf` reads them, so the address answers for the destination the loop arranges through and nothing else; `deliverySecretsOf({ ledger, secrets })` answers, for a place and `subscription`, the secret of each webhook subscription the playbook holds through that connection, by the `secret_id` of its row and the secret store, each with that row's `topic` and the `id` its service gave, so that a delivery is tied to the one subscription whose own secret proves it, passing over one the store no longer has, and nothing for `app`, since this runtime holds no secret of an app.

## The events a connection reports, for a trigger

`hearingOf(connection, entry, received, type)` (`src/triggers/connection-hearing.ts`) is the one rule of whether this runtime hears an event a connection reports, by shapes and declarations alone, never a provider's name: of the destinations of the entry that map the type, in the order the entry declares them, a `webhook` that arranges a subscription (`isArranging` of `@on.auto/providers`) is heard where the runtime receives per subscription; a `webhook` made `per: app` where it receives per app; a `websocket` where it opens sockets and the connection holds the destination's app credential unrefused, and where it holds none or the service refused it, the answer is `lacking`, with the label of that credential its entry gives; and a `polling` destination wherever the runtime runs, since a check needs no address and no socket. The first heard wins, else the first lacking, else it is not heard. What the runtime receives, `EventsReceived`, is three facts each composition gives: the server receives per subscription where an address is set and opens sockets, and Auto Cloud receives per subscription and per app and opens none. `list_connections` and `get_connection` answer each connection's `source` and, for one connected, `event_types`, the types its entry declares that the rule hears or answers `lacking` for, so an agent that reads the list reaches the save's refusal that names the credential to ask for, each with its `title`, its `subject` as `title`, `required` and the `description` the entry gives, never its `name`, which says how the runtime reads what the service calls the place, `speed`, `instant`, or `polling` beside `polling_interval_seconds`, the pace of the checks, the larger of a minute and what the app allows (`pollingIntervalSecondsOf` of `@on.auto/providers`), the same two a saved trigger's standing gives, and `data` (`src/operations/reported-events.ts`); none for one pending, broken or removed, or given by hand, as the description of `event_types` says.

`connectionEventsOver({ registry, providers, subscriptions, received }, listenedIn)` (`src/triggers/connection-events.ts`) answers the port a workflow's trigger that names a connection is checked through when it is saved, and read through when its workflow is, `ConnectionEvents` of `@on.auto/coordination`. It reads the connection as kept, as the intake does (`connectionsServingOf`): for a playbook and a connection's name, whether it is pending, connected or broken, a connection made from a provider another replaces being broken, the types its entry declares, what the playbook's active workflows listen to through it, from `listenedIn` (`listenedConnectionsOf` of `@on.auto/definitions`), and, for a type and a subject, what the rule answers, with the topic and the most places one connection holds where the destination arranges a subscription; nothing where no such connection serves the playbook. For a trigger, `standingOf` answers how it stands now, one pure decision over what the records hold (`triggerStandingOf`, `src/triggers/trigger-standing.ts`): whether the workflow is paused; the connection's recorded status, account and identity; what the rule answers; and, for a destination that arranges a subscription, the playbook's row of `event_subscriptions` for the connection and the topic, read by its key, its failure held to the current connecting and declaration (`isHeldBy`). For a subscription a check keeps, its made `type` being `polling`, the failure stands only while the check fails now, since a read that works records no fact: while a reconnect or a changed entry is what clears it (`holdsARead`) and it is held, or while the reader's count of failures in a row, `failures_in_a_row`, is above 0, which a read that works sets back to 0. A rebuilt row holds no count, so such a failure that is not held shows again only once a check fails. A failure of a first check stands as it is, since the check that works records a made fact. It never calls the service. It answers `listening`, or a kind that stays the same, words that say what to do, and whether only a reconnect mends it, and, where the trigger names a place whose name the kept connection holds (`placeNames`, see `name_place` above), that name, with the `prefix` the type's `subject.name` declares before it; it answers no name where none is held, so none after the connection is connected again until a round reads it anew.

`servedConnectionOf({ registry, providers })` (`src/triggers/connection-events.ts`) answers the port the workflow host's intake reads a connection through at every event it is handed (see [the workflow host](../workflow-host/README.md#an-event-a-connection-reports)), which the server and Auto Cloud compose over the org's connections and their providers: for a playbook and a connection's name, the connection as `loopConnectionsOf` reads it, one of the org not removed that serves the playbook now (`connectionsServingOf`, which `loopConnectionsOf` reads too) and is connected, or nothing where none does, so the intake takes nothing through a connection pending or broken, as a trigger's standing says (`connection_pending`, `connection_broken`); the id of the account the runtime recorded it acts as for its current connecting, `user`, from the fold's `identified`, and none where the fold holds none, before the first round reads it or once it is connected again, which the intake compares with the account an event names as having caused it; and, for an event's `type` and `subject`, the topics the event mappings of its `webhook` destination per subscription render from that subject, by the one rendering the triggers' asker of the loop gives a trigger (`triggerTopicsOf` of `@on.auto/providers`), so an event's topic and the topic a trigger on the same type and subject subscribes to are one string, and none for a connection with no such destination, or whose destination maps no event of that type with a topic.

## Source

`src/index.ts` is the only entry point. `src/registry` holds the record, its entry and credential, the facts and commands of the stream, the fold, the decider and its rules, and the lookups; `src/documents` the streams of the documents connections were added with, their fact and decider, their keeping and their read; `src/links` the token, Studio's page and the return address of a consent; `src/policy` the reading of `CONNECTIONS`, the policy and its rules, the providers the setting adds, and whom the host starts a connection for; `src/replacements` the providers another replaces, and the reconnecting of a connection made from one, or left waiting by another admin, which adds it again; `src/registered-clients` the clients whoever runs the runtime registered and their checks; `src/registrations` the clients the runtime registered itself; `src/entries` the checks of an entry and a credential, and the values a credential of each kind takes where they are given; `src/serving` the source of the connections as tool servers, the reading and the placement of their credentials, the store of the tokens of a consent over their row, and their words; `src/providers` the resolution of an addition by a provider and where its problems are reported, the resolver of a provider an entry of the settings file names, `list_providers` with the listing the policy allows, and the offering of those providers to a caller, which `list_providers` and the org's `search` share; `src/operations` the five operations of the connections, all but `get_connection` tools as well, with the fields, words and services they share with the page, how they load and record the stream, the admission of an addition, with the way a server given by hand names, and `makeConnectionOperations`; `src/signing` the two operations of a connection's signing secret; `src/subscriptions` the facts, their presenter, the read model and the layout step of the subscriptions of a playbook, the adapters of the loop's ports, their composition and a round; `src/triggers` what a connection reports for a workflow's trigger; `src/page` the five operations of Studio's page, which `pageOperationsOf` makes, the start and the finish of a consent among them, and the fields, checks and words of what the page gives; `src/testing` what the tests share, and `src/testing/index.ts`, the entry `@on.auto/connections/testing`, the fold and the name of the stream, with which a test of the server reads the id of a connection's secret from the ledger. `operations`, `page` and `serving` depend on the others, and `registry` on none of them.

`shipped-tools.json` holds, for each way of each shipped provider whose server is an HTTP API, the SHA-256 of every tool the bridge of `@on.auto/tool-servers` derives from its document, as an agent is listed it (its description, input schema and annotations), so a change to the bridge or to a document that alters a shipped tool shows in its diff; a test refuses it while it is not what the documents derive, and `pnpm --filter @on.auto/connections shipped-tools` writes it again (`src/testing/shipped-additions.ts`).

`shipped-credentials.json` holds, for each way of each shipped provider and each example of its values, the SHA-256 of the credential a connection made that way records and of the `auth` its server is read with, keys in order, so a change to how a shipped way asks for a token or a consent shows in its diff; a test refuses it while it is not what the entries resolve to, and `pnpm --filter @on.auto/connections shipped-credentials` writes it again (`src/testing/shipped-additions.ts`). Both are read with what the serving of a connection made that way provides (`providingOf`), so a way's `path_values` count.

A connection's served entry takes the `path_values` of the way its credential was made through, read from its provider's entry as it is now (`providingOf` of `src/providers/provider-providing.ts`, the way found by its kind and permissions, as its `ending_codes` are), and never kept on the connection: they belong with the document, which the serving reads from the provider too, so a document translated again keeps a connection made before calling as it did. An entry of the settings file that names a provider takes them from its way the same way (`src/providers/provider-resolver.ts`).

## The sockets of connections

A runtime that opens sockets keeps one for each `websocket` destination and value of an app credential that a connection holds; the server's sockets keep them (see its README). What a registry asks for is `socketsHeldIn({ registry, providers }, org)` (`src/sockets/socket-holders.ts`), read from the org's stream by its own fold, with its version: each connection that is `connected`, whose provider's entry declares a `websocket` destination whose `credential` the connection holds by a secret's id and the service has not refused, with when it was connected, the destination's place among the entry's, the label the entry's secret way declares for that app credential, its `description`, the secret's id, the entry's `failure_codes`, the id of the account the connection is recorded in, where it is recorded in one, and whether the service is still to be asked who the connection is, never a value; a connection given by hand, pending, broken or removed holds none, and neither does a destination whose `credential` the secret way does not declare, which an entry cannot do once it is read. `appCredentialRefused(registries, org, holder)` records that the service refused the value, `refuse_app_credential` by the runtime with the words a person and an agent read in the connection's `app_credentials`, which name the app credential by that label and never by its `name`, decided only while the connection is still connected at the time the socket saw and holds that value unrefused, so a refusal never lands on a connection connected again since; a refusal it cannot record is logged and the socket stays closed until the server starts again. `socketChecksOver(opens)` (`src/sockets/socket-checks.ts`) is the port `give_credential` checks a value with where a runtime opens sockets: it opens the destination's socket once, within 20 seconds, and closes it at once, answering `taken`, `refused` where the service refused the value, or `unreached` naming the host it could not open at, and logs the end it met, never the value.
