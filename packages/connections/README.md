# @on.auto/connections

The connections of an org: tool servers a person adds to the org through their agent, a provider by its name (`@on.auto/providers`) or a server given by hand, which the org owns, which every playbook of the org may use or the playbooks its entry names, and which are kept as facts, added and removed rather than run. A connection's entry is the non-secret part of a tool server entry; its credential, when it has one, is a row of the ledger's secret store, referred to by id and never held in a fact. The agent never carries a secret: `add_connection` answers a one-time link to Studio's page, where the person who added the connection approves it and gives its credential. This package keeps connections and manages them, and serves them to `@on.auto/tool-servers` as the tool servers of their org, through which every function and every operation over tool servers calls (see [The calls](#the-calls)). The operations are defined on the application layer, [`@on.auto/operations`](../operations), and `docs/self-host/connections.md` is the operator's guide.

`ConnectionSchema` is the schema of the record. Times are ISO 8601 UTC strings read from Effect's `Clock`.

The fields `in`, `name` and `scheme` of a placement, `issuer` and `scope`, and the fields of consent, `parameters`, the three endpoints and `clients`, are `wayFields` of `@on.auto/providers`, the one place a provider's way and a connection's credential take them from; the credential adds what a way never has, `variable`, `client_id`, a client id as `ClientIdSchema` of `@on.auto/tool-servers` holds one, and `client_source`, which is `ClientSourceSchema` of the same package, `auto` among its sources, the client of Auto's consent service. An MCP server given by hand that takes consent holds `issuer`, `clients` and `scope` as the server names them (see below), and takes `client_id` and `client_source` once connected, as a provider's way does; its `clients` is required, each one a client the runtime makes for itself, `metadata_document` or `dynamic`, which a check on the data of `connection_added` holds (`src/registry/connection-events.ts`), so a missing list never falls back to `registered` and Auto's client. The credential is one shape wherever it appears: what `add_connection` takes, what every operation answers in the record, and what `connection_added` keeps, with the client id the person gives on the page set on it once the connection is connected. Its fields are optional in the schema, each described with the kind it belongs to, and a check on the schema holds each kind to exactly its fields, so a credential that lacks one or gives another is `invalid_input` at the field, in words that name it, as `Expected name, which a reference credential takes` or `Expected no in: a none credential takes no other field`. The same check refuses a `reference` whose `variable` is no variable name (`variableNamePattern` of `@on.auto/config`), a `secret` or a `reference` sent as a header whose `name` is no header name (`headerNamePattern` of `@on.auto/tool-servers`), an `issuer`, or an endpoint of an `authorization` credential, the addresses `wayAddresses` of `@on.auto/providers` names, that is neither https nor on a loopback address (`isPinnableUrl` of `@on.auto/tool-servers`), and what `consentIssuesOf` of `@on.auto/providers` refuses in an `authorization` credential, as in its provider's way. A `scheme` is refused in words of its own: of a kind that places no value, `Expected no scheme: a none credential places no value in a header for the runtime to write a scheme before`; beside an `in` other than `header`, `Expected no scheme, which goes before a value sent in a header: this credential is sent in a query parameter`; holding `$`, which the reading of the placed entry takes for a reference or an escape, `Expected a scheme without $, which the reading of the entry takes for a reference or an escape`; and holding a character that is no HTTP token's, the characters of a header name (`headerNamePattern`), `Expected a scheme of the characters a header name holds, such as Bearer`.

## The facts and the fold

The connections of an org live in one stream, `connections` relative to the org, `connectionsStream`, and `org/<org>/connections` in full, `connectionsStreamOfOrg(org)`; the registrations of the runtime's clients live in another, `runtime/client-registrations`, `clientRegistrationsStream` (see below). The main entry exports the three, so a shell that keeps the streams of an org apart from those of the runtime, or lends them from objects of their own, routes each by its name rather than by restating it. Every fact carries `by` and `at` in its metadata, the `ActedContext` of `@on.auto/operations`, and none holds a secret, a token, a code, a verifier or a state, or a value derived from them but the digests of the link's token and of the consent's state:

| Fact                         | Data                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `connection_added`           | `connection`, `name`, `server_type`, the record's `type`, named so since the fact's own `type` names the fact, `provider`, `placeholders`, `entry` and `credential`, as resolved, and `says`, the words of the scopes of the provider's way, by scope, as the way wrote them when the connection was added, empty for a server given by hand                                              |
| `connection_link_issued`     | `connection`, `token_sha256`, the SHA-256 of the link's token in hex, and `expires_at`, 24 hours on; appended with `connection_added`, in one decision, and alone by `reconnect`                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `connection_consent_started` | `connection`, `state_sha256`, the SHA-256 of the consent's state in hex, as the link's token is kept, `verifier_secret_id`, the id the secret store keeps its PKCE verifier under, `client_id` and `client_source`, the client the start chose, and `expires_at`, ten minutes on from the start                                                                                           |
| `connection_approved`        | `connection`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `connection_connected`       | `connection`, and for a credential of the kind `secret` or `client_credentials` `secret_id`, the id the secret store keeps the value under, `algorithm` for a private key, and `client_id` where the person gave it on the page; for one of the kind `authorization` `secret_id`, the id of the row of its tokens, `client_id`, `client_source` and `scope`, the scope the server granted |
| `connection_broken`          | `connection`, `kind`, the kind of the break, and `reason`, the sentence of why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `connection_removed`         | `connection`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

There is no read model: each call folds the stream (`connectionsFold`, the fold of `registryDecider`). The fold keeps, by id, the record, the open link, the open start of a consent, `consent`, with its `started_at` from the context of its fact, which `connection_connected` drops and a later start replaces, the id of its secret, and the algorithm of a private key; it keeps the secret's id and the start's `verifier_secret_id` after a removal, so the removal can forget both. It answers the scopes the credential asks with the words `connection_added` keeps, so the words a connection shows are those of the way it was made from, whatever a later version of its provider says, and a break's `kind`, `reason` and the `at` of its fact as `broken`. It sets the `client_id` and the `client_source` of `connection_connected` on the record's credential, so a connection of consent says the client it was made with, and its `scope` on the record as `granted_scope`, and as `granted_scopes` with the words of the scopes asked, beside the scope the credential keeps as its way asked it, so a connection the person allowed less than its way asked shows it; a connection connected holds one shape of credential, its client id given with the credential or on the page, and a pending one whose credential leaves its client id out is answered without it. It answers the connections newest first. `connection_connected` closes the link, and so does `connection_removed`; a later `connection_link_issued` replaces the link before it, open or not, so only the latest link of a connection opens, and the fold keeps the digest of each link a later one replaced, `replacedLinks`, so a page given the token of one hears that a newer link replaced it. A connection connected again, through a link `reconnect` issued it while it was connected or broken, takes from that `connection_connected` its secret's id, its algorithm, its client and its granted scope anew, `connected_at` and `connected_by` are the new ones, and `broken` is `null`. A pure decider holds the rules, and the handlers pass it who and when in the command:

- `add` refuses a name a connection of the org holds that is not removed, `conflict` of the kind `taken`, and a connection past the 50 an org may hold that are not removed (`mostConnectionsOfAnOrg`), `conflict` of the kind `connections_full`, `The org holds 50 connections, the most it may; remove one before adding another`, which bounds the links the tool access keeps for the org, and records the connection and its link.
- `approve` and `give` look the connection up, `not_found`, then the link: open while its digest is the token's and `expires_at` later than now, whatever the connection's status, since `reconnect` issues one to a connection connected or broken and `connection_connected` and `connection_removed` close it, else `not_found`, `There is no open link of this token: a newer link of the connection crm replaced it` for the token of a link a later one replaced, and `There is no open link of this token: it is unknown, used up or expired` for any other. Where people sign in, the caller must be the one `added_by` names, by id, whatever name they go by now, else `forbidden`; `makeConnectionOperations` hands the two whether a link binds a person, which it does where `access` is `people`. `approve` connects a connection of the credential `none` or `reference`, recording `connection_approved` and `connection_connected`, and refuses one that takes a value with `conflict`; `give` records the two with the secret's id, and refuses a connection that takes none with `conflict`. Both refuse a connection whose credential is `authorization` with `conflict`, `The connection drive is connected by signing in to its provider and allowing Auto, which its page starts`, and `give_credential` refuses it so before it keeps anything.
- `start` and `finish` take a connection whose credential is `authorization` and refuse another kind with `conflict`, `The connection crm is not connected by signing in to its provider, so it has no consent to start or finish`. `start` checks the link as `approve` does and records `connection_consent_started`. `finish` checks the link's token and its binding but not its own `expires_at`, since the start's ten minutes govern: a connection with no open start, none recorded or one whose `expires_at` is not later than now, is `not_found`, `There is no open consent of the connection drive: none was started, or it was started more than ten minutes ago`, a state whose digest is not the start's is `invalid_input` at `/state`, `Expected the state the consent was started with`, and otherwise it records `connection_approved` and `connection_connected` in one decision, as `give` does.
- `remove` records `connection_removed`, and nothing for a connection already removed.
- `reconnect` records `connection_link_issued`, a new link, for a connection that is `broken`, `connected`, or `pending`, whether or not its link is open, so the new link replaces the one before and only the newest works, but for a `reference` that is `broken`, which it mends in place, recording `connection_approved` and `connection_connected` with the caller as `connected_by`, where the variable it sends is among `variablesSet`, the variables the runtime read at start, and refusing it with `conflict` otherwise, `The variable CRM_API_KEY that the connection crm sends is not set on this runtime; whoever runs it sets the variable and starts it again` (`variableUnset`, the words the source refuses such a connection with too). Where people sign in, the caller must be the one `added_by` names, the rule of the link, `bindingRefusalOf`, else `forbidden` in the page's words, `The connection crm was added by another person, and only they may approve it`; and it refuses with `conflict` a connection removed, `The connection crm is removed, so it is added again rather than reconnected`. It refuses no connection for a link that is open, since the link of an addition is in the agent's answer alone and the person may need another: a page given the token of the link before says a newer link replaced it.
- `break` records `connection_broken` with its `kind` and its `reason` for a connection that is connected at the `connected_at` the break names, the moment of the connecting the use that found it was prepared for, and nothing for one that is not connected or was connected again since, so a credential two calls find gone at once breaks its connection once, and a use that began before a reconnection never breaks the connection that reconnection made. Only the source of [The calls](#the-calls) issues it, as the runtime, `by: runtime`. Every failure to record a `break`, a change that came first among them, is logged, `logError` of Effect with the cause, and the connection is refused broken all the same; the decider's `not_found` cannot arise, since the source reads the connection from the stream it records the break on and the fold never drops one. The source keeps nothing of a break and the tool access keeps no broken answer, so the next use finds the break again and records it again.

## The operations

`makeConnectionOperations(services)` makes the eleven operations, all org operations, so their routes are relative to the org, as one list for a catalog, the five of Studio's page from `pageOperationsOf` of `src/page/page-operations.ts`. `add_connection`, `list_connections`, `reconnect_connection` and `remove_connection` are also MCP tools, and `list_connections` shows an agent each connection's `status`; `get_connection`, which reads one connection by its id, `list_providers`, which a page reads, and the five of Studio's page are defined with `defineHttpOperation`, and so served over HTTP alone. The services are what the server composes: `secrets`, the `keep`, `read`, `replace`, `claim`, `endingsOf` and `forget` of the `SecretStore` of [`@on.auto/secrets`](../secrets/README.md), `SecretKeeping`, whose read answers a `KeptRow` and whose replacement and claim answer `TokensReplaced` and `TokensClaimed` of `@on.auto/tool-servers`; `policy`; `fileEntries`, the names of the entries of `TOOL_SERVERS`; `modelProviders`, the names of the model providers and gateways; `providers`, the one `ProviderList` the server reads at start, the providers it ships and those the setting adds, and `providerValues`, the values the environment sets for the variables its references name, which are variables the runtime reads for itself and `connections.variables` may not name; `pages`, the Studio settings and the runtime's origin, read when a link or a return address is made; `access`, who the runtime admits: `people` where it signs people in, `keys` where it takes API keys alone, and `local` in local mode; and `consent`, the `ConsentClient` of `@on.auto/tool-servers`, the runtime acting as a client at an authorization server, which the server makes once with the clients whoever runs it registered, `PUBLIC_URL`, `makeClientRegistrations` and the switch of Auto's clients, and gives its tool access too.

- `add_connection` takes one flat input, two ways in (`AdditionSchema` of `src/operations/connection-fields.ts`, whose check holds each way to its fields as the credential's check holds each kind to its own). Its `credential` is a schema of its own, the fields a credential given by hand takes, kept apart from the record's, which holds the fields of a way of consent beside them. With `provider`, the name of a provider, it takes `placeholders`, `name`, the provider's when left out, `credential.kind`, and where it is left out the first way the listing marks offered to the caller, `isOfferedWay` of `src/providers/provider-listing.ts`, the rule `list_providers` marks each way by, or the provider's first where it offers none, which the checks then refuse in their words, and of `entry` only `playbooks`, `allowed` and `record_content`, and refuses `type`, the server part of `entry` and every field of `credential` but `kind`, each at its field, `Expected no url: the provider gives the server`. Without, it takes `name`, `type`, `entry` and `credential` as a server given by hand, the first three required, `Expected type, which a server given by hand takes, or provider`, and `credential` required of an `openapi` server alone, `Expected credential, which an HTTP API given by hand takes, or provider`, since an `mcp` server says how it authenticates (see below), and refuses `placeholders`. A credential left out of an `mcp` server is `{ kind: authorization }` through the checks and the policy (`givenByHand` of `src/providers/provider-additions.ts`), the one kind it can be recorded as, so a runtime whose `credential_kinds` leaves `authorization` out refuses it before the server is asked anything. A credential of the kind `authorization` given by hand takes its `kind` alone, for an `mcp` server, whose authorization server the runtime finds from the server itself (see below): every other field beside it is refused at its field, `Expected no issuer: the server names its authorization server itself`, and an `openapi` server refuses the kind at `/credential/kind`, `Expected another kind: an HTTP API given by hand takes no consent, since its document names no authorization server; name a provider, as search lists it, in provider`. An entry given by hand could name any server, so for a server given by hand the issuer comes from the server's own metadata; the client is one the runtime registers at that issuer for each server origin given by hand, keyed by the issuer and the server's origin and named `Auto Runtime for <host>`, so the person sees which server they allow, never a client registered for a provider, the registration a provider's way or a server on another origin uses at that issuer, nor Auto's consent service, and the runtime's metadata document only where the issuer registers no clients dynamically; every authorization request asks `prompt=consent`, so an issuer that honours it shows its consent screen even for a client it saw before; and the token request names the resource at every step, a start, a finish and a renewal being refused where the server no longer publishes its metadata, `The server at <url> no longer names its authorization server, so the consent cannot go on; remove the connection and add it again`, while the revocation of `remove_connection` goes to the issuer the connection recorded, so its tokens do not stay alive, rather than going on with the issuer the connection recorded, as a provider's way does; so a server given by hand is never handed tokens a person consented to elsewhere. A provider is resolved first (`src/providers/provider-additions.ts`): `providerNamed` of `@on.auto/providers` refuses a name the runtime has no provider of at `/provider`, to which this package adds where the names are, `There is no provider named crm here; search lists the providers a person may connect`, and `additionOfProvider` turns the provider and what the caller gave into the name, the type, the entry and the credential a server given by hand would have been, refusing a placeholder or a kind at the pointer the caller wrote. Everything after is the path of a server given by hand, over the resolved addition: the credential's check, the entry's checks, the reading, the policy and the names, none skipped and none run twice; a problem they find in a field the provider resolved is reported at `/provider`, a name's problem at `/name` where the caller gave the name and at `/provider` where it is the provider's, and a problem of `playbooks`, `allowed` or `record_content` at its field. A provider's document beside its entries is read from the folder of the providers, as a file's document is read from the configuration's folder, where a document given by hand must be a URL. A provider's header or query value may hold a reference, `${NAME}`, for a value of the operator's, such as a developer token: the record keeps it as the provider wrote it, the entry's checks take it since the provider and not the caller wrote it, while an entry given by hand still refuses every reference, and the reading substitutes it from `providerValues`, so a provider whose value is not set is refused at `/provider` in the reading's words, `Refers to the environment variable METRICS_DEVELOPER_TOKEN, which is not set`. The record and `connection_added` carry `provider` and `placeholders`, and the resolved entry, so a later change to a provider's entry changes no connection there is.
- `add_connection` first refuses a caller who cannot add a connection, `forbidden` (`adderRefusalOf`): where the runtime signs people in, a caller that is not a signed-in person (`isSignedInPerson` of `@on.auto/identity`), since a key's connection would wait for a person who never comes, and, where it takes keys alone, every caller, with the sentence that this runtime signs nobody in, so no person can approve a connection. In local mode the local developer adds any. It then checks the entry, every problem at its pointer in one `invalid_input` (`additionIssuesOf` in `src/entries/entry-checks.ts`): the name with `serverNameProblems`; an `mcp` connection's `url` that is neither https nor on a loopback address (`isPinnableUrl` of `@on.auto/tool-servers`), and a `document` that is not a URL, which would be a file on the runtime's disk; the entry with `credentialProblems` of `@on.auto/config`, so a value that looks like a credential is refused, and with `substituted` of the same package against an empty environment, so any `${…}` is refused as a reference; and the credential: a header the runtime sets itself (`headersTheRuntimeSets` of `@on.auto/tool-servers`, which the headers of an entry are checked against too) or the entry sends already, a query parameter or a security scheme for an `mcp` connection, and a query parameter the entry sends already. Where all of these pass (`entryIssuesOf`), it reads the entry with its credential placed, a value standing in for the one the person gives (`readAddition`), as the source reads it to serve it with the real value (see [The calls](#the-calls)), so what the file's reading refuses is refused here, at its pointer under `/entry`, and no connection reaches the ledger that its use would refuse for its entry. Client credentials that leave their `client_id` to the page are read with a client id standing in for it too, since the reading builds the `auth` block, which takes one. ## The ending of a credential

`give_credential` hands the secret store, with a secret or a client secret of at least twelve characters, its last four, `keep(value, endsWith)`, and no ending with a shorter value or a private key. The store keeps the ending in the value's row and forgets it with the value, so `remove_connection` forgets both; no fact holds it and the fold records nothing of it. Every operation answers the connection with `credential_ends_with` beside `connected_at`, never inside `credential`, which is what `add_connection` took and the client id given on the page: `list_connections` and `get_connection` read it with `endingsOf` for each connection that has a secret, `give_credential` answers the ending it kept, and the others, whose connection holds none, answer `null`.

## The policy

`readConnectionsSetting(environment)` reads `CONNECTIONS`, a JSON object, `connections` of the configuration file through `ConnectionsSettingSchema`, once, and answers `ConnectionsSetting`: `policy`, what people may add; `registeredClients`, the clients whoever runs the runtime registered at authorization servers (see [The registered clients](#the-registered-clients)), which is no rule about what people may add; `autoClients`, `auto_clients` as the setting gives it, whether a way that takes a registered client takes Auto's client too through Auto's consent service, or `undefined` where the setting says nothing, which the server then decides by local mode; `clientVariables`, the variables their references name; and `providers`, the providers whoever runs the runtime adds beside those it ships (see [The providers the setting adds](#the-providers-the-setting-adds)). The policy is `people_may_add`, `true` when left out; `allowed_hosts`, each a host or `*.` and a host, every host when left out; `credential_kinds`, every kind when left out; and `variables`, the variable names a `reference` may give, none when left out. `namingNoOwnVariable(setting, ownVariables)` checks `variables` apart from the reading, since a reader may know the variables it reads for itself only once it has read what refers to them: a name in `variables` that is one of `ownVariables`, or of `clientVariables`, is a problem at its pointer, so the server stops at start rather than let a connection send one of them. ## The calls

`makeConnectionSource({ registry, secrets, policy, pages, variables, modelProviders, providers, providerValues })` (`src/serving/connection-source.ts`) is the `ConnectionSource` of `@on.auto/tool-servers`, which the server gives its tool access, so a connected connection is a tool server of its org for every function and every operation over tool servers, through the same code as an entry of `TOOL_SERVERS`. `registry` is the ledger, read and written by the full stream name, `secrets` the `read`, `replace` and `claim` of the secret store, `pages` Studio's origin and the runtime's, from which the return address of a consent is made, `policy` the policy read at start, `variables` the values of the variables `policy.variables` names that the runtime's environment sets, read at start, and `modelProviders` the names of the model providers and gateways a connection's entry is checked against. `takesConnections` is `policy.peopleMayAdd`.

## Source

`src/index.ts` is the only entry point. `src/registry` holds the record, its entry and credential, the facts and commands of the stream, the fold, the decider and its rules, and the lookups; `src/links` the token, Studio's page and the return address of a consent; `src/policy` the reading of `CONNECTIONS`, the policy and its rules, and the providers the setting adds; `src/registered-clients` the clients whoever runs the runtime registered and their checks; `src/registrations` the clients the runtime registered itself; `src/entries` the checks of an entry and a credential, and the values a credential of each kind takes where they are given; `src/serving` the source of the connections as tool servers, the reading and the placement of their credentials, the store of the tokens of a consent over their row, and their words; `src/providers` the resolution of an addition by a provider and where its problems are reported, the resolver of a provider an entry of the settings file names, `list_providers` with the listing the policy allows, and the offering of those providers to a caller, which `list_providers` and the org's `search` share; `src/operations` the five operations of the connections, all but `get_connection` tools as well, with the fields, words and services they share with the page, how they load and record the stream, the admission of an addition, with the way a server given by hand names, and `makeConnectionOperations`; `src/page` the five operations of Studio's page, which `pageOperationsOf` makes, the start and the finish of a consent among them, and the fields, checks and words of what the page gives; `src/testing` what the tests share, and `src/testing/index.ts`, the entry `@on.auto/connections/testing`, the fold and the name of the stream, with which a test of the server reads the id of a connection's secret from the ledger. `operations`, `page` and `serving` depend on the others, and `registry` on none of them.
