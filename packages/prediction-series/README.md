# @on.auto/prediction-series

The model runtime of the release for the format `series`: the runtime's own models, each a recipe written as JSON, a name and its attributes, executed in TypeScript with no native dependency, behind the contract of [`@on.auto/prediction-contract`](../prediction-contract/README.md). It is an extension as any other is: its main entry exports `manifest`, which fills `prediction/model-runtime` for the format `series`, and the server lists it as the extension of the release, after those its settings list, as [Model runtimes](../../docs/reference/model-runtimes.md) says. The prediction capability, [`@on.auto/prediction`](../../capabilities/prediction/README.md), imports nothing from it, and it imports nothing of the capability; its measure runs the capability's `measure-models.ts` as a command. User documentation of the format is [The runtime's own models](../../docs/reference/prediction-format.md#the-runtime-s-own-models), and of its arithmetic [The runtime's own models](../../docs/reference/model-runtimes.md#the-runtime-s-own-models) in the reference of model runtimes; update both alongside the recipes.

## The manifest

`manifest` (`src/runtime/series-runtime.ts`) is named `@on.auto/prediction-series`, at the version of the release, read from the repository's `package.json`, and provides one model runtime, `seriesRuntime`: the name `series`, the version of the release, the one format `series`, every capability, `features`, `given`, `totals`, `calendar` and `fits`, and an inline binding whose module is `src/recipes/series-models.ts`, which the server imports into its own process. A function's features and given features ahead are fed to a recipe, which reads neither; it reads the target `y`, the periods to the end of a total `h` and the period of its origin `at`.

## A recipe

A recipe (`src/recipes/recipe-schema.ts`) is a JSON object of at most 16,384 bytes, `{ recipe, attributes }`. `recipe` is one of `persistence`, `mean`, `drift`, `seasonal-naive`, `level-adjusted-seasonal-naive`, `run-rate` and `linear`. `attributes.answers` is `{ steps }`, the steps it answers, from 1 to 1,000, or, for the seasonal recipes and the run-rate, `{ total: { grain, accumulates } }`, a total over the periods to the end of the enclosing period, `accumulates` that enclosing period or null. `attributes.interval` is `{ method: 'residual' }` or, for the linear recipe, `{ method: 'conformal', low, high }`, the bounds of each step as ratios to the level; either may be `{ method: 'none' }`, which answers the likely value as the low and the high. The seasonal recipes name their `season`, from 2 to 366 periods, and the level-adjusted form its `k`; the linear recipe names its `grain`, `lags`, `marks`, `level` and `weights`, one weight for the intercept, each lag and each column of its marks, which `linearIssueOf` checks.

`loadModel(path)` (`src/recipes/series-models.ts`) refuses a file of more than 16,384 bytes, one the schema does not take and a linear recipe whose weights or bounds do not match its lags and steps, each in words, and answers the loaded model of the contract: its signature, the recipe itself; no length, since a recipe forecasts from a window of any length; the issue of its answers with what a function feeds, the steps of its horizon or a total where the function asks for the other, or another count of steps; and its run, which answers `q`, `[1, steps, 3]`, the low, the likely value and the high of each step, or `[1, 1, 3]` for a total, or `badly` for a recipe that answers a total fed no `h` or reads the calendar fed no `at`.

## The recipes

Each recipe forecasts from the window it is fed, up to the origin (`src/forecasts`), and answers a finite number for every series. The history of a series is its periods from the first it observed to the origin; where it does not allow a recipe, the recipe answers as the one it falls back to.

| Recipe                          | It forecasts at step h                                                                                                                                                                                                | It falls back to                                  | Its interval                                                    |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `persistence`                   | The last value observed                                                                                                                                                                                               | 0, with nothing observed                          | Residual, √h                                                    |
| `mean`                          | The mean of the T values observed                                                                                                                                                                                     | 0, with nothing observed                          | Residual, √(1 + 1/T)                                            |
| `drift`                         | The last value plus h times the mean change from the first value observed to the last, over T periods                                                                                                                 | Persistence, with one value observed              | Residual, √(h(1 + h/(T − 1)))                                   |
| `seasonal-naive`                | The value k + 1 whole seasons before the step, k = ⌊(h − 1)/m⌋ for a season of m                                                                                                                                      | Persistence, under m + the steps asked            | Residual, √(k + 1)                                              |
| `level-adjusted-seasonal-naive` | ŷ(T+h) = ŷ(T+h−m) × r, ŷ the value observed up to the origin and r the mean of the last k periods over the mean of the same k periods a season earlier: the value k + 1 whole seasons back times r to the power k + 1 | The seasonal naive, under m + k + the steps asked | Residual, the seasonal naive's                                  |
| `run-rate`                      | For a total, the mean of the r periods of the enclosing period so far times the p periods left, or of the last p periods when it has just begun                                                                       | 0, with nothing observed                          | Residual, the drift's of the running total, h = p and T = r + 1 |
| `linear`                        | A ridge regression of the next period on its lags and the calendar mark of the period ahead, each step fed the steps before it                                                                                        | Persistence, where the last periods have no level | Conformal                                                       |

- A residual interval (`src/intervals/residual-constants.ts`) takes σ, the root mean square of the recipe's own one-step errors over the window, and puts the low and the high 1.2816 × σ × its constant either side of the likely value, the 10th and 90th percentiles of a normal error. The constants are the textbook ones of each method's h-step error.
- The seasonal naive answers a total as the sum of the same periods as many whole seasons earlier as the total needs, as the baseline of its name does, and its level-adjusted form as the sum of its own forecast of each period, so each is scaled by the level once for each season it reaches back; the constants of the periods are added in quadrature, and without a season a total falls to the run-rate.
- A forecast the level would take past the largest number is the seasonal naive's value.
- The level r is 1 where the k periods a season earlier average 0 or where the two means differ in sign.
- The linear recipe (`src/forecasts/linear-forecasts.ts`) weighs each lag as a ratio to its level, the mean size of its last `level` periods, so one model fits series of any size, and answers the weighted sum times the level. Its calendar marks (`src/calendar/periods.ts`) are one-hot columns, the first left out, of the day of the week of a day, the month of a month and the quarter of a quarter; a week and a year have none. The period of a mark is told by `at`, the index of the origin's period the contract defines.
- A conformal interval (`src/intervals/conformal.ts`) is the 10th and the 90th percentiles, interpolated, of the model's errors as ratios to the level at each step, moved to the likely value where every error falls on one side of it, so the low never rises above the likely value nor the high falls below it.

## Fitting

`fitModels(shape, training)` (`src/fitting/fitted-recipes.ts`) answers the recipes a build scores as candidates of a function that names no trainer: the mean and the drift; the level-adjusted seasonal naive, `k` a season, with a season; and the linear model, where the training rows hold enough samples. For a total it answers the level-adjusted seasonal naive with a season, and nothing without one. It fits no persistence, seasonal naive or run-rate: those forecast as the baselines `persistence` and `seasonal-naive` do, which every candidate is scored against, so they could never beat them. `defaultModel(shape)` answers the recipe of the default forecast: the level-adjusted seasonal naive with a season, persistence without one, and the run-rate for a total without one.

The linear model (`src/fitting/linear-fit.ts`) takes the lags 1, 2 and 3, and the season when the function has one that is not among them, and a level read over as many periods as its longest lag. Each sample is a period of a series whose lags and level are observed, its lags as ratios to the level and its target the period over its level. The samples stream into the normal equations of two fits (`src/fitting/linear-system.ts`), so no matrix of samples is held: those whose target falls before the last `holdout` training periods, and all of them. The model is fitted on the first when they hold at least four samples for each weight, and on all otherwise, with a ridge penalty of 1% of the samples on every weight but the intercept, by Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting; a pivot under 10⁻¹² leaves the function with no linear recipe. Its weights are kept to six significant digits. Its conformal bounds come from its errors at the last `holdout` origins of the training rows, the rolling origins of the scoring, each step's own.

## Memory and time

The recipes run in the server's own process, which is why only the release's own extensions may bind a runtime inline. No process limit bounds their memory, and nothing stops a job but the job itself: the server answers `timed_out` at a job's deadline and signals it to stop, a walk stops at the next series, and a check, a run, a fit and a default forecast run to their end. So the recipes bound themselves: a recipe takes at most 16 KiB, read before it is decoded, a job reads only the rows it is handed, the fit streams its samples into normal equations of at most 16 weights instead of holding them, and a walk yields to the server's other work between series. At most as many run at once as the lanes of [the capability's model processes](../../capabilities/prediction/README.md#the-model-processes) admit.

`pnpm --filter @on.auto/prediction-series measure` fits the recipes of 1,000 daily series of 192 training days and runs the capability's `measure-models.ts` with this runtime's module, the default recipe and the linear one, bound inline. On an Apple M4 Max, arm64, 16 cores, Node 26.11.1, at a load of 2.2 to 2.5, measured twice on 2026-10-28:

| Measure                                                                               | Took                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The four recipes fitted from 192,000 training rows of 1,000 series                    | 155 ms, 155 ms, the heap 2.0 to 2.2 MiB smaller after it; the largest recipe 351 bytes |
| A model loaded and checked, cold                                                      | 6 ms, 7 ms                                                                             |
| A question                                                                            | 0 to 1 ms                                                                              |
| A walk over 200,000 rows of 1,000 series, 8 origins and 2 steps                       | 142 to 169 ms                                                                          |
| A shadow's walk at one close over 1,000 series                                        | 99 ms, 101 ms                                                                          |
| The linear recipe walked over the widest rows the bounds admit, 74 MiB of 40 features | 1,110 ms, 1,115 ms, resident memory 167 MiB before it and 347 and 354 MiB at the peak  |

So a walk over the widest rows took about 180 to 190 MiB more resident memory at its peak, and at most `PREDICTION_BUILDS_AT_ONCE` such walks run at once, about 380 MiB with the default 2; the fit of a build's recipes took about a 190th of its 30-second deadline.

## Testing

The recipes' arithmetic is tested on flat and trending series, seasonal series of two seasons and series that accumulate (`src/forecasts/*.test.ts`), with each interval's constant (`src/intervals/residual-constants.test.ts`), a conformal interval from given errors (`src/intervals/conformal.test.ts`), the rule of two seasons and the fallbacks. `src/fitting` holds the tests of the linear system, the fit and the recipes fitted and defaulted, and `src/recipes/series-models.test.ts` loads, refuses and runs recipes through the module of the binding. The server's tests of prediction functions answer a question from the default forecast, fit and activate a build of a function that names no trainer and answer from its version through the release's extension, loaded as the server loads it.

## Source

`src/index.ts` is the entry point. `src/runtime` holds the manifest and the runtime it provides; `src/recipes` the schema of a recipe, its dispatch and the module of the binding; `src/forecasts` the recipes' arithmetic over a fed window; `src/intervals` the residual and conformal intervals; `src/fitting` the recipes fitted and the linear system; and `src/calendar` the periods and their marks. `measure-inline.ts` is the measure.
